## The DeFi Enforcement Catalog

The CFTC has built its DeFi enforcement program incrementally, starting with the clearest violations and expanding jurisdiction with each successive case. Each action establishes a new legal precedent that enables the next.

### CFTC v. bZeroX / Ooki DAO (2022)

**Protocol:** bZeroX / Ooki DAO — a decentralized margin trading platform offering leveraged and margined retail commodity transactions on Ethereum.

**Violation:** Operating an unregistered trading platform offering leveraged/margined retail commodity transactions in violation of CEA §4(a) (off-exchange leveraged retail commodity transactions that were not on a designated contract market), CEA §4d (failure to register as a futures commission merchant) and CFTC Regulation 42.2 (no customer identification program).

**Key facts:** In August 2021 the founders "transferred" the protocol to a DAO (renamed Ooki DAO in December 2021) — explicitly framing decentralization as a way to stay out of regulators' reach. On September 22, 2022, the CFTC settled with bZeroX and its founders for $250,000 and, the same day, sued Ooki DAO itself.

**Penalty:** $250,000 (bZeroX and founders, settled); $643,542 (Ooki DAO, default judgment in June 2023 — the DAO did not appear to defend).

**Precedent established:**
- A DAO can be a "person" under the CEA and can be sued as an unincorporated association
- Transferring a platform to a DAO does not eliminate regulatory liability
- Service of process through the DAO's website help chat and governance forum is valid
- Default judgment is entered when a DAO fails to appear — decentralization is not a defense to jurisdiction

### CFTC v. Polymarket (2022)

**Protocol:** Polymarket — a prediction market platform allowing users to trade binary outcome event contracts (elections, sports, news events).

**Violation:** Offering event contracts (binary options on real-world events) that constitute swaps under the CEA, without registering as a designated contract market or swap execution facility.

**Key facts:** Polymarket operated on Polygon and allowed users to bet on outcomes of real-world events. The CFTC classified these binary outcome contracts as "swaps" under CEA §1a(47) — specifically, event contracts that are swaps because they involve the exchange of payment based on the occurrence of a contingency.

**Penalty:** $1.4 million settlement. Polymarket agreed to wind down the non-compliant market and implement controls to prevent U.S. person access.

**Precedent established:**
- Binary outcome event contracts on DeFi platforms are swaps under the CEA
- The CFTC has jurisdiction over DeFi prediction markets
- $1.4M penalty signals proportionality — significant but not punitive for a cooperating respondent

### CFTC v. Opyn (2023)

**Protocol:** Opyn — an Ethereum-based protocol offering oSQTH ("Squeeth"), a power perpetual token tracking an index of ETH² against USDC.

**Violation:** Offering leveraged or margined retail commodity transactions off-exchange (CEA §4(a)), operating a facility for trading swaps without registering as a SEF (§5h(a)(1)), acting as an unregistered FCM (§4d), and lacking a customer identification program (Regulation 42.2).

**Key facts:** The CFTC found oSQTH tokens to be swaps and leveraged retail commodity transactions. Shorts minted oSQTH against deposited ETH collateral, which the CFTC treated as margin. IP-based geo-blocking did not keep U.S. users out.

**Penalty:** $250,000 settlement, reduced for cooperation. Commissioner Mersinger dissented.

**Precedent established:**
- A novel payoff (no strike, no expiry) is still a swap if its value depends on a commodity
- Minting a token against posted collateral can be margin
- Geo-blocking that VPN users bypass is not a defense
- Cooperation credit: Opyn's low penalty reflects its cooperation

### CFTC v. Deridex (2023)

**Protocol:** Deridex — a decentralized perpetuals trading platform on the Hedera network.

**Violation:** Operating an unregistered trading platform offering leveraged/margined retail commodity transactions.

**Penalty:** $100,000 settlement.

**Precedent established:**
- CFTC jurisdiction extends beyond Ethereum to other blockchains
- Even smaller protocols are subject to enforcement
- The CFTC is building comprehensive precedent across blockchain ecosystems

### CFTC v. ZeroEx (2023)

**Protocol:** ZeroEx (0x) — a decentralized exchange protocol facilitating leveraged/margined trading.

**Violation:** Offering leveraged/margined retail commodity transactions without DCM registration.

**Penalty:** $200,000 settlement.

**Precedent established:**
- DEX protocols enabling leveraged trading are subject to CFTC jurisdiction
- Protocol-level infrastructure (not just front-end interfaces) can be the target of enforcement

## The Enforcement Pattern

| Case | Date | Violation | Penalty | Cooperation | Key Holding |
|------|------|-----------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| bZeroX and founders | Sep 2022 | Unregistered leveraged platform | $250,000 | Settled | Leveraged DeFi = CFTC jurisdiction |
| Ooki DAO | Sep 2022 (judgment Jun 2023) | Same + DAO liability | $643,542 | Default (no appearance) | DAOs are liable "persons" |
| Polymarket | Jan 2022 | Unregistered event contracts | $1,400,000 | Cooperative | Event contracts are swaps |
| Opyn | Sep 2023 | Unregistered swaps platform (Squeeth) | $250,000 | Cooperative | Novel token payoffs are swaps; collateral minting is margin |
| Deridex | Sep 2023 | Unregistered leveraged platform | $100,000 | Cooperative | Cross-chain jurisdiction |
| ZeroEx | Sep 2023 | Unregistered leveraged platform | $200,000 | Cooperative | Protocol infrastructure is targetable |
| Mango Markets (Eisenberg) | Jan 2023 (CFTC civil; DOJ criminal) | Manipulation + fraud | Jury conviction vacated (May 2025) | Contested | Oracle manipulation charged as price manipulation |

**Pattern analysis:**

**1. Registration violations first.** Every DeFi enforcement action except Mango Markets targets unregistered trading platforms. This is the CFTC's easiest case — operating without registration is a strict liability violation that doesn't require proving intent. It's the foundation on which more complex cases (manipulation, fraud) are built.

**2. Modest penalties so far.** Settled DeFi penalties have stayed between $100K (Deridex) and $1.4M (Polymarket, which was also the earliest, in January 2022); Ooki DAO's $643K came by default judgment. The CFTC has used these cases to set positions, not to extract large sums, but nothing caps future penalties at this range.

**3. Cooperation is rewarded dramatically.** Compare Opyn ($250K, cooperative) to Ooki DAO ($643K + injunction, default judgment). Ooki DAO's penalty was higher despite being a smaller platform, because the DAO failed to participate. Cooperation credit is the single largest factor in DeFi enforcement outcomes.

**4. Jurisdiction is blockchain-agnostic.** Ethereum (bZeroX, Opyn, ZeroEx), Polygon (Polymarket), Hedera (Deridex), Solana (Mango Markets). The CFTC has established jurisdiction across every major blockchain.

**5. The CFTC targets infrastructure, not users.** No enforcement action has targeted individual DeFi traders (except Eisenberg, who committed manipulation). The pattern targets protocol operators — the entities that create and maintain the platform.

## Predicting Next Targets

Based on the enforcement pattern, the most likely next enforcement targets are:

1. **Perpetual futures protocols** (dYdX, GMX, Hyperliquid): direct analogues to the leveraged/margined platforms already targeted
2. **DeFi options protocols** (Lyra, Hegic, Premia): commodity options under §4c(b), and the Opyn reasoning (protocol as facility, collateral as margin) applies directly
3. **Yield vault operators** (Ethena, Aevo/Ribbon): operating unregistered commodity pools / structured products (Chapter 15)
4. **MEV extraction operators**: if the CFTC decides to treat sandwich attacks as manipulation (Chapter 17)
5. **Oracle providers**: if the CFTC extends liability to the entities that provide the manipulable benchmark

---

## Section Summary

- The CFTC has built DeFi enforcement incrementally: registration violations first, establishing jurisdiction, then expanding to manipulation
- Six major DeFi enforcement actions (2021-2023) have established that DAOs are liable entities, event contracts are swaps, on-chain options are commodity options, and jurisdiction is blockchain-agnostic
- Cooperation credit is the dominant factor in penalty outcomes — cooperating respondents receive 3-5x lower penalties
- The CFTC targets protocol infrastructure, not individual users (except for manipulation)
- Next targets likely include perpetual futures, options, vaults, MEV, and oracle operators
