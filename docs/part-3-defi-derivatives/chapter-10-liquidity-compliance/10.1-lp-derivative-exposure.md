# 10.1 LP Positions as Derivative Exposures

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain the mathematical equivalence between LP positions and short options
    - Distinguish between different LP position types and their derivative characteristics
    - Assess when passive liquidity provision crosses into regulated market-making
    - Calculate the options-equivalent exposure of LP positions

---

## The Mathematical Proof

In Section 8.2, we introduced the concept that AMM LP positions have options-like payoff profiles. Here we formalize that analysis.

**Constant Product AMM (Uniswap v2):** An LP deposits equal dollar amounts of Token A and Token B into a pool governed by x × y = k. As the price of Token A increases relative to Token B, the pool sells Token A and buys Token B (rebalancing). The LP's portfolio is continuously rebalanced toward the depreciating asset — the opposite of what a rational holder would want.

The value of the LP position versus a simple buy-and-hold portfolio can be expressed mathematically. If the price ratio changes by a factor r, the impermanent loss is:

**IL = 2√r / (1 + r) - 1**

This loss function is identical to the payoff of a **short straddle** (selling both a put and a call at the same strike). The LP earns fees (analogous to the options premium collected from writing the straddle) and loses when prices move significantly in either direction.

**Concentrated Liquidity (Uniswap v3):** The options equivalence becomes even more precise. An LP providing liquidity in the price range [Pa, Pb] holds a position that is equivalent to:

- **Short one put** struck at Pa (the lower bound)
- **Short one call** struck at Pb (the upper bound)

The narrower the range, the higher the effective leverage and the larger the premium (fees earned). Guillaume Lambert's peer-reviewed research demonstrated that Uniswap v3 LP positions can be valued using the Black-Scholes framework, with implied volatility derivable from fee income.

| LP Position Type | Payoff Profile | Options Equivalent | Regulatory Classification Risk |
|-----------------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Uniswap v2 (full range) | Continuous rebalancing; loss on large price moves | Short straddle with infinite-range strikes | Low-moderate — wide range dilutes the derivative character |
| Uniswap v3 (narrow range) | Concentrated exposure; significant loss on moderate moves | Short put at Pa + short call at Pb | **High** — concentrated positions are economically indistinguishable from written options |
| Perp protocol LP (GMX GLP/GM) | Counterparty to all trader PnL | Short portfolio of options with variable strikes | **Very high** — LP is writing derivatives as a market-making function |
| Options protocol LP (Hegic, Lyra) | Direct options writing through pool participation | Direct short options — pool IS the options writer | **Maximum** — LP is explicitly writing commodity options |

## When Does LP'ing Become Market-Making?

Under the CEA, a "swap dealer" includes any person who "makes a market in swaps." The question: does providing liquidity to a derivatives AMM constitute "making a market in swaps"?

The analysis hinges on the nature and scale of the activity:

**Passive, small-scale LP'ing** — A retail user depositing $10,000 into a Uniswap v3 ETH/USDC pool is unlikely to be classified as a swap dealer, even if the LP position has derivative characteristics. The de minimis threshold ($8 billion in aggregate gross notional) and the "regular business" requirement provide substantial protection for small LPs.

**Active, large-scale LP'ing** — An entity that deploys millions in capital across multiple perp protocol pools, actively manages position ranges, and derives a majority of its revenue from LP fees is performing a swap dealer function in substance. If the entity's market-making activity in derivative instruments exceeds the de minimis threshold, swap dealer registration could be required.

**Protocol-native market-making** — Some protocols designate specific LPs or vaults as "designated market makers" with special privileges (priority execution, reduced fees). These designated market makers are the closest analog to traditional swap dealers and face the highest classification risk.

    **Calculating options-equivalent exposure of LP positions:**

    *"I have a Uniswap v3 LP position in the ETH/USDC pool with the following parameters: lower tick price $[Pa], upper tick price $[Pb], liquidity amount $[L], current ETH price $[P]. Calculate: (1) the equivalent options position (put strike, call strike, notional), (2) the implied delta, gamma, and theta of the position, (3) the fee income required to break even against impermanent loss at current implied volatility, and (4) the regulatory classification risk based on the notional equivalent."*

    **What to verify:** AI models can perform the mathematical calculations competently but often struggle with the regulatory classification inference. An AI might correctly calculate that an LP position is equivalent to a $500K notional short straddle but fail to flag that this is a commodity option under CEA §4c(b). Always force the AI to connect the mathematical analysis to the regulatory classification.

## Section Summary

- Concentrated LP positions are mathematically equivalent to short options — this is proven, not speculative
- The narrower the LP range and the larger the capital, the stronger the derivative characterization
- LP positions in perp protocol pools carry the highest regulatory risk — LPs are writing derivatives as market makers
- Large-scale, active LP'ing in derivatives protocols may constitute unregistered swap dealing
- The de minimis threshold provides protection for small retail LPs but not for institutional-scale market-making
