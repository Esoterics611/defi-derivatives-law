# Part V: Compliance & Enforcement

You now understand the law. You understand the instruments — swaps, futures, options, perpetuals, structured products. You understand the contracts — ISDA Master Agreements, Credit Support Annexes, close-out netting. You understand where DeFi protocols fit within this regulatory architecture and where the gaps are.

Part V teaches you how to **comply** — and what happens when you don't.

Compliance is not a checkbox exercise. For derivatives market participants, the CFTC's regulatory framework demands affirmative programs: written policies, designated officers, real-time surveillance, and documented testing. For DeFi protocols operating in or accessible from the United States, these requirements apply whether the protocol acknowledges them or not. Ignorance of the regulatory framework is not a defense — as multiple DeFi founders have learned through enforcement actions.

Chapter 16 builds the compliance architecture. We start with the four pillars of a derivatives compliance program under CFTC Rules 23.600 and 3.3, then translate each pillar into DeFi-native implementations. The chapter provides a practical blueprint: if you operate a DeFi derivatives protocol, this is the minimum compliance infrastructure you need.

Chapter 17 covers the CFTC's most aggressively enforced domain: market manipulation. The CEA's anti-manipulation provisions — §9(a)(2), §6(c)(1), Rule 180.1, and the Dodd-Frank expansion — are powerful tools that the CFTC has already deployed against DeFi participants. We cover traditional manipulation (spoofing, wash trading), DeFi-specific vectors (MEV extraction, oracle manipulation, governance attacks), and the emerging on-chain surveillance toolkit. The Mango Markets case study — the first successful criminal prosecution for DeFi market manipulation — demonstrates that these provisions have real teeth.

The message of Part V: the regulatory framework exists, enforcement is active, and the cost of non-compliance is measured in disgorgement, penalties, trading bans, and prison time.
