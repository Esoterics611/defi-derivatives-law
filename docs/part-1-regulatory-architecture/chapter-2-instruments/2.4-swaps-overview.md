# 2.4 Swaps: The Backbone of Modern Derivatives

Swaps are the most important derivative instrument class for this book. They represent the largest segment of the OTC derivatives market by notional value, they are the primary instrument regulated under Dodd-Frank Title VII, and they are the legal classification most likely to apply to DeFi derivatives products.

## 2.4.1 Anatomy of a Swap

A swap is an agreement between two counterparties to **exchange cash flows** over a period of time according to a predetermined formula. The simplest example is a plain vanilla interest rate swap:

- **Party A** pays a fixed rate (say, 3.5%) on a notional amount ($100 million)
- **Party B** pays a floating rate (say, SOFR + 50 bps) on the same notional amount
- Payments are exchanged periodically (quarterly, semi-annually) for the swap's term (e.g., 5 years)
- Only the **net difference** is exchanged on each payment date — the notional amount never changes hands

The key elements of any swap:

| Element | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Notional amount** | The reference amount for calculating payments | $100 million |
| **Fixed leg** | The predetermined payment stream | 3.5% per annum |
| **Floating leg** | The variable payment stream tied to a reference rate | SOFR + 50 bps |
| **Payment frequency** | How often payments are exchanged | Quarterly |
| **Reset dates** | When the floating rate is determined | 2 business days before each payment |
| **Day count convention** | How interest accrual is calculated | Actual/360 or 30/360 |
| **Effective date** | When the swap begins accruing | Trade date + 2 business days |
| **Maturity date** | When the swap terminates | 5 years from effective date |

## 2.4.2 Major Swap Types

| Swap Type | What's Exchanged | Market Size | DeFi Equivalent |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Interest Rate Swap (IRS)** | Fixed rate vs. floating rate (SOFR, EURIBOR) | ~$400T notional | Variable/fixed yield swaps (Pendle, Voltz) |
| **Credit Default Swap (CDS)** | Protection premium vs. credit event payment | ~$30T notional | Credit protocols, insurance protocols |
| **Total Return Swap (TRS)** | Total return of an asset vs. a financing rate | ~$5T notional | Synthetic asset exposure (Synthetix) |
| **Commodity Swap** | Fixed commodity price vs. floating commodity price | ~$2.5T notional | Fixed-price token agreements |
| **Equity Swap** | Equity return vs. a financing rate | ~$4T notional | Token-based synthetic equity exposure |
| **FX Swap** | Exchange of currencies at agreed rates | ~$110T notional | Cross-chain token swaps with forward component |

## 2.4.3 Why Swaps Matter for DeFi

The statutory definition of "swap" under CEA §1a(47) is extraordinarily broad. It includes:

> "any agreement, contract, or transaction... that provides for any purchase, sale, payment, or delivery... that is dependent on the occurrence, nonoccurrence, or the extent of the occurrence of an event or contingency associated with a potential financial, economic, or commercial consequence"

This definition is so expansive that many DeFi protocol products — perpetual futures, synthetic assets, yield-bearing positions with variable returns, leveraged token positions — can plausibly be classified as swaps. The CFTC has used this broad definition aggressively in DeFi enforcement actions.

The critical question for any DeFi protocol: **does your product involve the exchange of financial exposures between counterparties based on the value of an underlying asset?** If yes, it may be a swap — regardless of what you call it, how it's structured in code, or whether it uses smart contracts instead of ISDA documentation.

!!! danger ""
    **The Perpetual Futures Problem**: DeFi perpetual futures (dYdX, GMX, Hyperliquid) are not technically "futures" under the CEA because they have no expiration date. But they are almost certainly "swaps" — they involve the exchange of funding rate payments between long and short positions, referenced to the price of an underlying commodity. The CFTC has treated perpetual futures as swaps in multiple enforcement actions. The DeFi industry's preferred terminology ("perps") does not change the legal classification.


## 2.4.4 Regulatory Treatment of Swaps

Dodd-Frank Title VII created an entire regulatory architecture for swaps, which is the subject of Part II of this book. The key obligations:

| Obligation | Requirement | CFTC Rule |
|-----------|------------|-----------|
| **Registration** | Swap dealers and major swap participants must register with the CFTC | Part 23 |
| **Clearing** | Certain standardized swaps must be cleared through a DCO | Part 50 |
| **Exchange trading** | Cleared swaps must be traded on a DCM or SEF | CEA §2(h)(8) |
| **Reporting** | All swaps must be reported to a registered SDR | Parts 43 & 45 |
| **Margin** | Uncleared swaps require initial and variation margin | Part 23, Subpart E |
| **Business conduct** | Swap dealers must comply with external and internal business conduct standards | Part 23, Subparts F & H |

Each of these obligations has direct implications for DeFi protocols that create swap-like instruments. We explore them in depth in Chapters 4–7.
