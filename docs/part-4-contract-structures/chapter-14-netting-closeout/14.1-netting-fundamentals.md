# 14.1 Payment Netting & Close-Out Netting

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain payment netting and its role in reducing operational risk
    - Explain close-out netting and its role in reducing credit risk
    - Describe the legal basis for netting enforceability
    - Quantify the capital impact of netting on derivatives portfolios

---

## Payment Netting

**Payment netting** (ISDA Section 2(c)) is the simplest form of netting: when two counterparties owe each other payments in the same currency on the same date under the same Master Agreement, the payments are offset and only the **net amount** is transferred.

Without payment netting, if Goldman owes JPMorgan $12 million (fixed leg of Swap A) and JPMorgan owes Goldman $10 million (floating leg of Swap A plus fixed leg of Swap B), both parties would send separate wire transfers. With payment netting, Goldman sends a single $2 million payment.

Payment netting reduces operational risk (fewer wire transfers, fewer settlement failures) and intraday liquidity needs (parties need only fund the net amount, not the gross). For major dealers with thousands of transactions under a single Master Agreement, payment netting reduces daily cashflow volumes by orders of magnitude.

## Close-Out Netting

**Close-out netting** is the mechanism that makes the derivatives market systemically manageable. When an Event of Default occurs and the non-defaulting party terminates all outstanding transactions, the individual Close-out Amounts are **netted to a single figure** — one party owes the other a single payment.

The significance is best illustrated by example:

| Transaction | Close-out Amount (to Goldman) |
|------------|------------------------------|
| Swap 1 (IRS) | +$15 million (in-the-money for Goldman) |
| Swap 2 (CDS) | -$8 million (out-of-the-money for Goldman) |
| Swap 3 (Equity swap) | +$3 million |
| Swap 4 (Commodity swap) | -$12 million |
| Swap 5 (FX option) | +$6 million |
| **Net Close-out Amount** | **+$4 million** (JPMorgan owes Goldman $4M) |

Without close-out netting, Goldman would be an unsecured creditor of JPMorgan for $24 million (the sum of its in-the-money positions) while simultaneously owing JPMorgan $20 million on its out-of-the-money positions. In a bankruptcy, Goldman might recover only cents on the dollar for the $24 million while being required to pay the $20 million in full. Close-out netting transforms this asymmetric exposure into a single $4 million claim.

## Legal Basis: The Single Agreement Concept

Close-out netting depends entirely on the ISDA Master Agreement's **single agreement concept** (Section 1(c)). If each swap were a separate contract, a bankruptcy trustee could cherry-pick: enforce the swaps that are profitable for the bankrupt estate while repudiating those that are unprofitable. The single agreement concept prevents this by treating all transactions as components of one integrated agreement.

## Netting Legislation

For close-out netting to work, the legal system must **enforce** it — even in bankruptcy, where the automatic stay would normally prevent creditors from terminating contracts. Approximately 80 jurisdictions have enacted netting legislation that ensures the enforceability of close-out netting for qualifying financial contracts. ISDA maintains a **netting opinion** program: legal opinions in each jurisdiction confirming that close-out netting under the ISDA Master Agreement is enforceable. Counterparties rely on these opinions for capital calculations.

| Netting Type | Mechanism | Legal Basis | Capital Benefit | DeFi Equivalent |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Payment netting** | Offset same-currency, same-date payments | ISDA Section 2(c) | Reduces operational risk and liquidity needs | Atomic settlement (single transaction, no gross cashflows) |
| **Close-out netting** | Upon default, net all transactions to a single amount | ISDA Section 6(e), single agreement concept | Reduces credit exposure by 85-90%; critical for regulatory capital | No equivalent — each DeFi position is independent; no cross-position netting |
| **Novation netting** | Replacing multiple trades with a single trade at the net economic terms | Separate novation agreement | Reduces outstanding transaction count | Protocol-level position consolidation (some protocols allow merging positions) |

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note"
    **Calculating net exposure across a portfolio of swaps:**

    *"Given the following portfolio of swaps between Party A and Party B under a single ISDA Master Agreement: [list transactions with current mark-to-market values]. Calculate: (1) Gross positive exposure (sum of in-the-money positions), (2) Gross negative exposure (sum of out-of-the-money positions), (3) Net exposure (gross positive minus gross negative), (4) Netting benefit ratio (1 − net/gross positive). Then assess: if Party B defaults, what is Party A's actual credit exposure with and without close-out netting?"*

    **What to verify:** AI models perform the arithmetic correctly but may not account for cross-currency netting complications (positions in different currencies require FX conversion before netting) or the fact that netting enforceability depends on the counterparty's jurisdiction. A netting calculation is meaningless if the counterparty is in a jurisdiction where ISDA netting is not legally enforceable.

---

## Section Summary

- Payment netting reduces operational risk by offsetting same-day, same-currency cashflows
- Close-out netting reduces credit risk by 85-90%, converting gross exposure to a single net amount
- The single agreement concept and jurisdiction-specific netting legislation are the legal foundations
- DeFi achieves per-transaction settlement through atomic execution but lacks cross-position netting
