# Review Questions — Chapter 11: DAO Governance & Regulatory Liability

---

**1.** Explain why the CFTC classified Ooki DAO as an "unincorporated association" rather than attempting to classify it as a corporation, partnership, or other entity type. What elements of the unincorporated association definition did the DAO satisfy?

**2.** A DeFi perpetual futures protocol is governed by a DAO with 15,000 token holders. Of these, 800 have ever cast a governance vote, 50 serve as delegates with voting power delegated by others, and 5 are core contributors who deployed the original smart contracts. Rank these four participant classes by liability exposure and explain your reasoning using the *Ooki DAO* precedent.

**3.** Compare the liability protection provided by a Cayman Islands Foundation Company versus a Wyoming DAO LLC for a DeFi derivatives protocol. Under what circumstances would the CFTC be able to pierce through each structure to reach individual participants?

**4.** A protocol founder argues: "We deployed the smart contracts 18 months ago, renounced all admin keys, and burned the governance tokens. The protocol is now immutable — no human can change or stop it. Therefore, no person is 'operating' it." Evaluate this argument under the CEA. Could the CFTC still bring enforcement? Against whom?

**5.** Describe the "chilling effect" of the *Ooki DAO* precedent on DAO governance participation. How does the threat of personal liability create a paradox: governance participation is riskier than passive holding, but passive holding may contribute to the concentration of power that regulators target?

**6.** A DAO is considering three legal wrapper options: (a) Cayman Foundation Company, (b) Marshall Islands DAO LLC, and (c) no wrapper at all. The protocol serves users in 40 countries including the United States. For each option, analyze: the liability exposure of token holders, the CFTC's ability to enforce, and the practical costs and benefits. Which would you recommend and why?

**7.** Explain why the bZeroX-to-Ooki-DAO transfer was described as a "shell game" by the CFTC. How did the transfer actually **increase** the total number of persons facing liability rather than decreasing it?

**8.** The "sufficient decentralization" framework suggests that once a network is sufficiently decentralized, certain regulatory requirements may not apply. Critique this framework as applied to derivatives regulation specifically. Does the CEA contain any provision that would support a decentralization exemption? How does the *Ooki DAO* precedent affect this analysis?

**9.** You are advising a new DeFi options protocol that wants to operate compliantly. The team proposes the following structure: (a) a Cayman Foundation holds the IP, (b) the protocol offers permissioned access via KYC'd front-end only, (c) governance token holders vote on protocol parameters but a compliance committee (appointed by the Foundation) has veto power over proposals that would create regulatory violations. Analyze the strengths and weaknesses of this structure against the liability frameworks discussed in this chapter.

**10.** Using the AI Practitioner Note prompt from Section 11.1, analyze a real DeFi DAO (your choice) for governance liability exposure. Run the analysis through an AI model, then critically evaluate: did the AI correctly distinguish between voting and passive holding? Did it account for the delegate's elevated liability position? Did it analyze whether the DAO's legal wrapper (if any) provides meaningful protection?
