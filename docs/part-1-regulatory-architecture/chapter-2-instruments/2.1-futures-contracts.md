# 2.1 Futures Contracts: Mechanics & Regulation

A futures contract is the oldest and most straightforward derivative instrument. It is a **standardized agreement** to buy or sell a specified quantity of a commodity (or financial instrument) at a predetermined price, on a specific future date, on a regulated exchange.

## 2.1.1 The Mechanics

Every futures contract specifies five elements:

| Element | Description | Example: CME Bitcoin Futures (BTC) |
|---------|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Underlying** | The commodity or instrument being traded | Bitcoin |
| **Contract Size** | The quantity per contract | 5 BTC per contract |
| **Delivery/Settlement** | Physical delivery or cash settlement | Cash-settled (USD) |
| **Expiry** | When the contract expires | Monthly (last Friday of contract month) |
| **Tick Size** | Minimum price movement | $5 per BTC ($25 per contract) |

Futures are **marked-to-market daily**: at the end of each trading day, the exchange calculates the difference between the settlement price and the previous day's settlement price, and credits or debits each position holder's margin account accordingly. This daily settlement eliminates the buildup of unrealized losses that created systemic risk in OTC markets.

The clearinghouse — typically the exchange's clearing division — serves as the **central counterparty** to every trade. When Trader A sells a futures contract to Trader B, the clearinghouse interposes itself: it becomes the buyer to Trader A and the seller to Trader B. This structure means that neither party bears the credit risk of the other — only the credit risk of the clearinghouse.

## 2.1.2 Margin and Leverage

Futures are inherently leveraged instruments. Traders don't pay the full contract value upfront. They post **initial margin** — typically 3–15% of the contract's notional value — and must maintain a **maintenance margin** level. If losses cause the margin account to fall below maintenance levels, the trader receives a **margin call** and must deposit additional funds.

| Margin Type | Purpose | Typical Range |
|-------------|---------|---------------|
| **Initial Margin** | Collateral posted to open a position | 3–15% of notional |
| **Maintenance Margin** | Minimum account balance to keep the position open | ~75% of initial margin |
| **Variation Margin** | Daily mark-to-market settlement (credits/debits) | Varies daily |

For CME Bitcoin futures: initial margin has historically ranged from 40–50% of notional — far higher than traditional commodities — reflecting Bitcoin's volatility.

## 2.1.3 Crypto Futures: The TradFi-DeFi Bridge

The launch of regulated crypto futures created the bridge between traditional derivatives markets and the digital asset ecosystem:

| Contract | Exchange | Launch Date | Settlement | Contract Size |
|----------|----------|-------------|------------|---------------|
| Bitcoin Futures | CME | Dec 2017 | Cash (USD) | 5 BTC |
| Micro Bitcoin Futures | CME | May 2021 | Cash (USD) | 0.1 BTC |
| Ether Futures | CME | Feb 2021 | Cash (USD) | 50 ETH |
| Micro Ether Futures | CME | Dec 2021 | Cash (USD) | 0.1 ETH |

These contracts are fully regulated, traded on a DCM, cleared through CME Clearing, and reported to the CFTC. They serve as the regulatory benchmark against which DeFi derivatives protocols are measured — and found lacking in compliance.

## 2.1.4 Regulatory Treatment

Futures are regulated under CEA §2(a)(1)(A), which grants the CFTC exclusive jurisdiction over "accounts, agreements... and transactions involving swaps or contracts of sale of a commodity for future delivery." Futures must be traded on or subject to the rules of a DCM (CEA §4(a)) — this is the **exchange-trading requirement** that makes unregistered DeFi futures platforms illegal for U.S. persons.

!!! danger ""
    **The DeFi Problem**: When a DeFi protocol offers a product that is economically equivalent to a futures contract — specified underlying, standardized terms, cash settlement, daily funding — the CFTC will argue it is either a future (requiring DCM registration) or a swap (requiring SEF registration and trade reporting). "But it's a smart contract, not a futures contract" is not a legal defense.
