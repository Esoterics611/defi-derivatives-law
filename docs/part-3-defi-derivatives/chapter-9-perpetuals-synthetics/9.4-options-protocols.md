## On-Chain Options Landscape

Options — the right but not the obligation to buy (call) or sell (put) an underlying asset at a specified price — are among the oldest derivative instruments. Under the CEA, options on commodities are regulated derivatives that must be traded on registered exchanges. CEA §4c(b) makes it unlawful to offer or enter into commodity options except on or subject to the rules of a registered DCM.

DeFi has created several approaches to on-chain options:

**Opyn (Squeeth / Crab Strategy):** Opyn's signature innovation is **Squeeth** — a "squared perpetual" that tracks ETH². Unlike a traditional option, Squeeth has no strike price and no expiry. Its payoff is the square of ETH's price movement, funded through a funding rate mechanism similar to perps. This creates a novel derivative instrument: a perpetual convexity exposure. Opyn also offers the "Crab Strategy" vault, which automates a delta-neutral strategy using Squeeth to earn funding rate income.

**Lyra (now part of Derive):** An options AMM that prices options using a modified Black-Scholes model. Lyra provides on-chain options with traditional strike prices and expiry dates. Market makers provide liquidity, and the protocol dynamically adjusts pricing based on supply, demand, and volatility.

**Hegic:** An on-chain options protocol where LPs pool capital to collectively write options. Buyers purchase options at algorithmically determined premiums. The pool acts as the collective options writer.

**Dopex (now Stryke):** Offered options vaults and "Atlantic Options" — options that allow the collateral to be used simultaneously for other DeFi strategies. Created novel option structures not available in TradFi.

## DeFi Options Vaults (DOVs)

DeFi Options Vaults automate options-writing strategies. A typical DOV:

1. Users deposit collateral (e.g., ETH)
2. The vault automatically writes covered call options each week
3. Options are auctioned to market makers
4. If the option expires worthless (ETH stays below the strike), depositors earn the premium
5. If the option is exercised (ETH rises above the strike), depositors lose the upside above the strike

Ribbon Finance pioneered this model. DOVs are attractive to yield-seeking depositors because they generate consistent premium income in sideways markets. But the regulatory implications are significant: **the vault is an automated options-writing engine that creates regulated derivative instruments.**

| Options Protocol | Architecture | Strategy | Regulatory Classification |
|-----------------|-------------|----------|--------------------------|
| **Opyn (Squeeth)** | Power perpetual via funding rate | Long/short ETH² convexity | Novel derivative — likely a swap under §1a(47)(A)(iii) (payment based on value of commodity) |
| **Lyra / Derive** | Options AMM with Black-Scholes pricing | Standard puts and calls with strike/expiry | Commodity options under CEA §4c(b) — must be traded on registered DCM |
| **Hegic** | Pooled options writing | Simple put/call purchase | Commodity options under CEA §4c(b) |
| **Ribbon (now Aevo)** | Automated covered call/put vaults | Yield via premium collection | The vault writes commodity options — both the vault operator and vault depositors may be offering unregistered options |
| **Dopex / Stryke** | Options vaults with novel structures | Atlantic Options, SSOV | Commodity options with novel features — same regulatory classification |

## The Squeeth Innovation: Power Perpetuals

Opyn's Squeeth deserves special attention because it represents a genuinely novel derivative structure. A traditional option has a strike price and expiry. A perpetual future has no expiry but is linear in the underlying. Squeeth has no expiry **and** a non-linear (quadratic) payoff.

The payoff of a long Squeeth position is proportional to ETH² — the square of ETH's percentage price change. This creates embedded convexity: the position gains more from upward price movements than it loses from equivalent downward movements (before funding costs). The funding rate, paid by longs to shorts, compensates for this embedded optionality.

From a regulatory perspective, Squeeth is unambiguously a derivative — it provides payments based on the value of a commodity (ETH) through a mathematical transformation (squaring). Whether it is classified as a swap, an option, or a novel instrument, it falls within the CEA's jurisdiction. In 2023 the CFTC settled with Opyn over Squeeth itself: it found oSQTH tokens to be swaps and leveraged retail commodity transactions, so the classification question has an agency answer.

!!! danger "The CFTC v. Opyn Precedent"
    In September 2023, the CFTC settled enforcement actions against Opyn, Inc., Deridex, Inc., and ZeroEx, Inc. for offering illegal off-exchange digital asset derivatives. Opyn was charged over oSQTH (Squeeth): the CFTC found the tokens to be swaps and leveraged retail commodity transactions, and found that Opyn ran an unregistered swap execution facility. The settlement: $250,000 civil penalty and a cease-and-desist order. The CFTC looked at economic substance and did not care that Squeeth fits no traditional instrument type.

## Section Summary

- On-chain options protocols create commodity options regulated under CEA §4c(b)
- DOVs automate options-writing strategies, making vault depositors collective options writers
- Power perpetuals (Squeeth) are novel derivatives with quadratic payoffs — clearly within CEA jurisdiction
- The CFTC's 2023 enforcement against Opyn treated Squeeth, a novel power perpetual, as a swap
- Every major options protocol operates without the DCM registration required for commodity options trading
