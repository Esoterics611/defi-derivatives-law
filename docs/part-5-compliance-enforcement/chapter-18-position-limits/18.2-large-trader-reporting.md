# 18.2 Large Trader Reporting

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain the CFTC's large trader reporting framework under Part 18
    - Identify reportable position levels and filing requirements
    - Describe Form 40 (Statement of Reporting Trader) and its disclosure requirements
    - Analyze the compliance infrastructure required for large trader reporting

---

## The Reporting Framework

CFTC Rule Part 18 establishes the **large trader reporting system** — a mandatory reporting regime that requires market participants holding positions above specified thresholds to identify themselves and report their positions to the CFTC.

Large trader reporting serves a different purpose than position limits. Position limits cap how much you can hold. Large trader reporting tells the CFTC who holds large positions — regardless of whether those positions are within the limits. The CFTC uses this data for market surveillance: monitoring concentration, detecting potential manipulation, and understanding market dynamics.

The system operates through two mechanisms:

1. **Routine position reports**: Futures commission merchants (FCMs), clearing members, and foreign brokers submit daily position reports for all accounts exceeding reportable levels
2. **Trader identification**: The CFTC requires traders holding reportable positions to file Form 40, identifying themselves, their trading purpose, and their affiliations

## Reportable Position Levels

The CFTC sets **reportable position levels** for each commodity — thresholds above which positions must be reported. These are well below position limits. The purpose: capture a comprehensive picture of large-position holders, not just those approaching limits.

| Commodity Category | Example Contracts | Typical Reportable Level | Position Limit (Spot Month) | Ratio |
|-------------------|------------------|------------------------|---------------------------|-------|
| Grain | Corn (CBOT) | 250 contracts | 1,200 contracts | ~21% of limit |
| Energy | WTI Crude (NYMEX) | 350 contracts | 6,000 contracts | ~6% of limit |
| Metals | Gold (COMEX) | 200 contracts | 6,000 contracts | ~3% of limit |
| Softs | Sugar (ICE) | 500 contracts | 13,000 contracts | ~4% of limit |
| Financial | E-mini S&P 500 | 1,000 contracts | No federal limit | N/A |

When a trader's position first reaches or exceeds the reportable level, the carrying FCM submits the report. From that point forward, the CFTC tracks the position daily until it falls below the threshold.

## Form 40: Statement of Reporting Trader

**Form 40** is the CFTC's primary tool for identifying who holds large positions. When the CFTC requests it (typically triggered by a position reaching reportable levels), the trader must file Form 40 disclosing:

- **Trader identity**: Full legal name, address, organizational structure
- **Trading purpose**: Commercial (hedging) or non-commercial (speculative)
- **Affiliations**: Other entities under common control or ownership
- **Account controllers**: Who makes trading decisions for the account
- **Principal business**: Nature of the trader's primary business activity

Form 40 is filed with the CFTC's Division of Market Oversight and is confidential — it is not publicly disclosed. The CFTC uses Form 40 data internally for surveillance, enforcement, and to build the Commitment of Traders reports (Section 18.3).

**Filing obligation:** Form 40 must be filed upon CFTC request, typically within one business day. Failure to file — or filing inaccurate information — is a violation of CFTC rules carrying civil penalties. The CFTC treats Form 40 compliance seriously: incomplete or deceptive filings undermine the entire large trader surveillance framework.

## Special Accounts and Omnibus Reporting

The reporting framework addresses complex account structures:

**Special accounts**: Accounts that hold positions at or above reportable levels. The carrying FCM must report each special account's position daily to the CFTC.

**Omnibus accounts**: Accounts where one FCM carries positions on behalf of another FCM's customers. The carrying FCM reports the omnibus account's total position; the originating FCM reports the breakdown by customer. This ensures the CFTC can see through layered account structures.

**Controlled accounts**: When a trading advisor or fund manager controls multiple accounts, each account is reported separately, but Form 40 identifies the common controller — enabling the CFTC to aggregate positions for surveillance purposes.

## The Compliance Burden

For large trading firms — major dealers, hedge funds, commodity trading houses — large trader reporting requires significant compliance infrastructure:

- **Position monitoring systems**: Real-time tracking of positions across all accounts, all exchanges, and all clearing members
- **Threshold alerts**: Automated notifications when positions approach reportable levels
- **Form 40 management**: Maintaining current Form 40 information and filing updates when material information changes
- **Aggregation tracking**: Monitoring positions across affiliated entities to ensure accurate aggregated reporting
- **Record retention**: CFTC Rule 18.05 requires retention of all records relating to large trader reports for at least five years

| Compliance Element | Requirement | System Needed | Cost Factor |
|-------------------|------------|--------------|-------------|
| Daily position reports | FCMs submit positions for all special accounts | Trade capture + reporting system | Integrated with trading infrastructure |
| Form 40 filing | File upon request, update when material changes occur | Compliance management database | Low (manual process) |
| Aggregation monitoring | Track positions across all controlled/affiliated accounts | Cross-entity position system | High for complex organizations |
| Threshold monitoring | Detect when positions reach/exceed reportable levels | Real-time position analytics | Medium (automated alerts) |
| Record retention | 5-year retention of all reporting-related records | Archive system | Medium (storage + retrieval) |

## Section Summary

- CFTC Part 18 requires large trader reporting when positions exceed commodity-specific thresholds — well below position limits
- Form 40 identifies the trader, their purpose (commercial/speculative), affiliations, and account controllers
- The reporting framework works through FCMs, who submit daily position data to the CFTC for special accounts
- Compliance requires real-time position monitoring, threshold alerts, and aggregation tracking across entities
