# 6.5 End-User Exception & Commercial Hedging

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain the statutory basis and policy rationale for the end-user exception
    - Identify the three requirements for claiming the exception
    - Analyze whether a DAO treasury could qualify as an end-user
    - Assess the risks of improperly claiming the exception

---

## The Exception

CEA §2(h)(7) provides that the clearing mandate **does not apply** to a swap if one of the counterparties:

1. **Is not a financial entity** — the counterparty must not be a swap dealer, major swap participant, commodity pool, private fund, or bank holding company regulated by a federal banking agency
2. **Is using the swap to hedge or mitigate commercial risk** — the swap must be related to the counterparty's underlying business, not speculative trading
3. **Has notified the CFTC how it generally meets its financial obligations** — the counterparty's board (or equivalent governing body) must approve a resolution authorizing reliance on the exception and specifying whether the counterparty will exchange margin

The end-user exception is a critical safety valve. Congress recognized that requiring a wheat farmer hedging crop prices or an airline hedging fuel costs to clear their swaps through a DCO would impose costs disproportionate to the systemic risk these transactions create. The exception preserves bilateral trading for genuine hedging by non-financial commercial entities.

## What Counts as "Hedging or Mitigating Commercial Risk"

The CFTC defined this in 17 C.F.R. §50.50(c): a swap hedges or mitigates commercial risk if it is economically appropriate to reduce risks in the conduct and management of a commercial enterprise, where the risks arise from:

- A potential change in the value of assets, liabilities, or services the entity owns, produces, manufactures, processes, merchandises, or anticipates owning
- A potential change in value arising from interest rate, FX, or price movements related to the entity's assets, liabilities, or services
- A fluctuation in revenue, expense, or financing arising from business operations

Speculative trading does not qualify. A swap entered to profit from anticipated price movements — rather than to offset an existing or anticipated commercial exposure — falls outside the exception.

## The DAO Treasury Question

Can a DAO treasury qualify for the end-user exception? This is an open legal question with significant practical implications. The analysis:

| Requirement | DAO Treasury Analysis | Assessment |
|-------------|----------------------|------------|
| **Not a financial entity** | Is the DAO a "commodity pool" or "private fund"? If the DAO collects assets from participants and trades or invests those assets, it may be a commodity pool under CEA §1a(10). Many DeFi treasuries function exactly this way. | **High risk** — many DAOs would be classified as commodity pools or private funds, which are "financial entities" excluded from the exception |
| **Hedging commercial risk** | Does the DAO have "commercial risk" to hedge? A protocol that earns revenue from transaction fees has commercial risk (fee revenue denominated in volatile tokens). A DAO hedging its ETH treasury against USD exposure could arguably be hedging commercial risk. | **Moderate** — arguable for protocol treasuries with genuine commercial operations, weak for investment DAOs |
| **Board/governing body resolution** | Does the DAO have a "governing body" that can pass a resolution? Token-weighted governance could be analogized to a board, but the CFTC has not addressed this. A multisig committee might be a closer analog to a board. | **Uncertain** — no regulatory guidance on DAO governance as a "governing body" |

!!! danger "The Commodity Pool Trap"
    The most likely disqualifier for DAO treasuries is the "financial entity" exclusion. If a DAO collects tokens from participants and manages those assets — even if the primary purpose is protocol development rather than trading — the CFTC could classify it as a commodity pool. Under CFTC regulations, commodity pool operators must register with the NFA, and the pool itself is a "financial entity" ineligible for the end-user exception. This means that even if a DAO wanted to use the clearing exception for legitimate hedging of protocol revenue, its structural classification as a commodity pool could prevent it.

    *"Analyze whether [DAO name] qualifies for the end-user exception under CEA §2(h)(7). The DAO operates as follows: [describe treasury structure, revenue sources, governance]. (2) If not a financial entity, does the DAO have 'commercial risk' arising from its operations that a swap could hedge? (3) Does the DAO's governance structure include a body that could pass the required resolution? Present the analysis as a three-part test with a conclusion."*

    **What to verify:** AI models almost always overlook the commodity pool classification issue. If the AI concludes a DAO qualifies without analyzing whether the DAO is a commodity pool, the analysis is incomplete. Force the AI to address the financial entity threshold first — if the DAO fails that test, the other two requirements are irrelevant.

## Section Summary

- The end-user exception exempts non-financial entities hedging commercial risk from the clearing mandate
- Three requirements: not a financial entity, hedging commercial risk, board resolution authorizing the election
- Most DAO treasuries face a threshold problem: classification as a commodity pool or private fund would make them "financial entities" ineligible for the exception
- Even DAOs with genuine commercial hedging needs may be structurally disqualified
