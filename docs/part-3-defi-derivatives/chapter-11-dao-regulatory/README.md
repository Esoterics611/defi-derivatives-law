# Chapter 11: DAO Governance & Regulatory Liability

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this chapter, you will be able to:

    - Analyze whether a DAO constitutes a "person" subject to CFTC jurisdiction
    - Identify which DAO participants face personal liability for derivatives violations
    - Evaluate legal wrapper structures and their effectiveness against CFTC enforcement
    - Assess progressive decentralization as a compliance strategy
    - Apply liability frameworks to real DAO governance scenarios

!!! warning "Key Terms"
    - **Unincorporated Association**: A voluntary group of persons acting together for a common purpose without forming a legal entity — the CFTC's classification for DAOs lacking a legal wrapper
    - **Joint and Several Liability**: A legal doctrine under which each member of a group can be held individually liable for the full amount of the group's obligations
    - **Legal Wrapper**: A formal legal entity (foundation, LLC, association) that provides a DAO with legal personality, liability protection, and a regulatory interface
    - **Progressive Decentralization**: A strategy of gradually transferring control from a founding team to a distributed token-holder community, with the goal of reducing regulatory liability
    - **Sufficient Decentralization**: The theory (popularized by the SEC's William Hinman and a16z's legal framework) that once a network is sufficiently decentralized, certain regulatory requirements no longer apply to it

---

This chapter addresses the most important legal question in DeFi: **when a DAO violates derivatives law, who goes to jail?**

The question is not hypothetical. In September 2022, the CFTC filed and won an enforcement action against Ooki DAO — not against its founders (who were separately charged), but against the DAO itself as a legal entity. The CFTC obtained a default judgment establishing that a DAO is an "unincorporated association" under California law and thus a "person" subject to the CEA. More importantly, the court held that token holders who voted on governance proposals could be held personally liable for the DAO's regulatory violations.

This case changed the game. Before Ooki DAO, many DeFi participants assumed that decentralization created a regulatory vacuum — that if no single entity controlled a protocol, no single entity could be held responsible. The CFTC's enforcement theory, now backed by judicial precedent, is exactly the opposite: if a group of people collectively control a protocol through governance, they are collectively and individually liable for what that protocol does.

!!! danger "The Personal Liability Reality"
    If you hold governance tokens in a DAO that operates an unregistered derivatives platform, and you vote on proposals that direct the protocol's operations, you may be **personally liable** for the DAO's violations of the Commodity Exchange Act. This is not a theoretical risk — it is established case law after CFTC v. Ooki DAO (N.D. Cal. 2022).

---

## Section Summary

- DAOs can be classified as unincorporated associations, making them "persons" under the CEA
- Token holders who participate in governance face personal liability for the DAO's regulatory violations
- Legal wrappers provide limited but meaningful protection
- Progressive decentralization reduces but does not eliminate regulatory exposure
