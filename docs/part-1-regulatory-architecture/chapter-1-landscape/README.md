# Chapter 1: The Derivatives Regulatory Landscape

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
    - Explain why derivatives regulation exists and the systemic risks it addresses
    - Describe the role of a derivatives lawyer and how it maps to DeFi advisory practice
    - Navigate the CFTC vs. SEC jurisdictional divide for any financial instrument
    - Map the complete regulatory ecosystem: NFA, DCMs, SEFs, SDRs, and DCOs
    - Classify major digital assets as commodities or securities using current legal frameworks
    - Use AI to perform preliminary regulatory classification of DeFi protocols


!!! warning "Key Terms"
    - **Commodity Exchange Act (CEA)**: The foundational federal statute governing derivatives markets, administered by the CFTC
    - **CFTC (Commodity Futures Trading Commission)**: Federal agency regulating commodity futures, options, and swaps
    - **SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission)**: Federal agency regulating securities and security-based swaps
    - **Swap**: A broadly defined derivative contract involving the exchange of cash flows or financial exposures between counterparties
    - **Notional Value**: The reference amount on which derivative payments are calculated — not the amount at risk
    - **Systemic Risk**: The risk that failure of one market participant triggers cascading failures across the financial system
    - **NFA (National Futures Association)**: The self-regulatory organization for the U.S. derivatives industry
    - **DCM (Designated Contract Market)**: A regulated exchange for trading futures and options (e.g., CME Group)
    - **SEF (Swap Execution Facility)**: A post-Dodd-Frank regulated trading venue for swaps


---

The derivatives regulatory landscape is not an abstraction. It is the operating environment for a market with over $600 trillion in notional value — a number so large it defies intuition. To put it in perspective: global GDP is approximately $100 trillion. The derivatives market is six times the size of all economic output on Earth.

This chapter explains why that market requires regulation, who does the regulating, and how DeFi protocols have wandered directly into the crosshairs of agencies that have been perfecting enforcement for decades.
