# Part IV: Contract Structures & Documentation

Parts I through III covered the **regulatory architecture** — the agencies, statutes, and rules that govern derivatives markets, and how DeFi protocols map onto that framework. Part IV shifts from regulation to **contracts** — the actual documentation that governs $600 trillion in outstanding derivatives positions globally, and what their DeFi equivalents look like.

The ISDA Master Agreement is the single most important legal document in global finance. It is the contractual backbone of virtually every bilateral OTC derivative transaction. Understanding its architecture — the three-layer structure of Master Agreement, Schedule, and Confirmation — is essential for anyone who wants to build, advise, or regulate derivatives in any market, including DeFi.

This part covers ISDA documentation and its smart contract equivalents (Chapter 12), collateral management and margin frameworks (Chapter 13), netting and close-out mechanics (Chapter 14), and structured products and their tokenized forms (Chapter 15).

The core insight of Part IV is that **DeFi smart contracts already implement the economic functions of ISDA documentation** — collateral posting, margin calls, netting, and close-out. They just do it differently: automatically, transparently, and without the subjective human judgment that TradFi contracts rely on. Whether this difference is a feature or a bug depends on the scenario.
