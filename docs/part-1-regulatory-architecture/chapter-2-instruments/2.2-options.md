# 2.2 Options: Puts, Calls & Exotic Structures

An option is a contract that gives the holder the **right, but not the obligation**, to buy or sell an underlying asset at a specified price (the strike price) before or on a specified date (the expiration). The seller (writer) of the option has the **obligation** to perform if the buyer exercises.

## 2.2.1 Core Mechanics

| Element | Call Option | Put Option |
|---------|------------|------------|
| **Holder's right** | Buy the underlying at the strike price | Sell the underlying at the strike price |
| **Writer's obligation** | Sell if exercised | Buy if exercised |
| **Profitable when** | Market price > strike + premium | Market price < strike - premium |
| **Maximum loss (holder)** | Premium paid | Premium paid |
| **Maximum loss (writer)** | Unlimited (call) / Strike - premium (put) | Strike - premium |

**American options** can be exercised any time before expiration. **European options** can only be exercised at expiration. Most exchange-traded equity options are American-style; most OTC and DeFi options are European-style.

## 2.2.2 The Greeks: Practitioner-Level Understanding

You don't need to derive the Black-Scholes formula. You need to understand what each sensitivity measure tells you about an option position's risk:

| Greek | Measures | What It Tells You | Range |
|-------|----------|-------------------|-------|
| **Delta (Δ)** | Price sensitivity to underlying movement | How much the option price moves per $1 move in the underlying | Calls: 0 to 1; Puts: -1 to 0 |
| **Gamma (Γ)** | Rate of change of Delta | How quickly Delta changes — high Gamma means the position becomes more sensitive as it approaches the money | Always positive for long options |
| **Theta (Θ)** | Time decay | How much value the option loses each day as expiration approaches | Negative for long options (time erodes value) |
| **Vega (ν)** | Volatility sensitivity | How much the option price changes per 1% change in implied volatility | Positive for long options |
| **Rho (ρ)** | Interest rate sensitivity | How much the option price changes per 1% change in risk-free rate | Minimal for short-dated crypto options |

!!! info ""
    **Why the Greeks Matter for DeFi**: DeFi options protocols (Opyn, Lyra, Hegic, Dopex) must price options correctly or face arbitrage. Understanding the Greeks lets you evaluate whether a protocol's pricing model is sound — or whether it's systematically mispricing risk, which is both a business risk and a potential regulatory concern (inadequate risk management).


## 2.2.3 Exotic Options and DeFi Equivalents

Beyond vanilla puts and calls, the derivatives world includes exotic structures — many of which have emerged in DeFi:

| Exotic Type | Mechanics | DeFi Protocol Example |
|-------------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Barrier Options** | Activated or extinguished when the underlying hits a specified price | Knockout options on Lyra |
| **Binary/Digital Options** | Fixed payout if underlying is above/below strike at expiration | Prediction markets (Polymarket) — the CFTC classified these as event contracts |
| **Asian Options** | Payoff based on average price over a period, not spot price at expiry | TWAP-based settlement in some DeFi protocols |
| **Lookback Options** | Payoff based on the maximum or minimum price during the option's life | No direct DeFi equivalent yet, but programmable |

## 2.2.4 Regulatory Treatment

Commodity options are regulated under CEA §4c(b) and CFTC Part 32. Options on commodities must generally be traded on a DCM, with narrow exemptions for trade options (commercial hedging between eligible counterparties). On-chain puts and calls on digital assets fall under these rules even when they are structured as ERC-20 tokens. The CFTC's 2023 action against **Opyn** (over its Squeeth power perpetual, which it found to be a swap) shows how readily the agency applies the CEA to novel tokenized derivatives.

    ```

    ```
