# 20.2 AI-Assisted Schedule Negotiation

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Identify the key negotiation points in an ISDA Schedule
    - Use AI to compare counterparty schedules and identify differences
    - Generate negotiation memos and redlines with AI assistance
    - Apply the Schedule Negotiation Checklist to structured analysis

---

## What Gets Negotiated

The ISDA Master Agreement is standardized — the same 14 sections for every counterparty relationship. The **Schedule** is where parties customize the relationship. Schedule negotiation is the core legal work in derivatives documentation, and it involves dozens of interconnected provisions.

The negotiation dynamic typically follows a pattern: the dealer (Party A) proposes their standard Schedule, and the buy-side counterparty (Party B — fund, corporate, or non-dealer) negotiates modifications. Each provision has a "dealer standard" and a "buy-side standard," with the negotiated outcome falling somewhere between.

## Key Provisions: The Negotiation Checklist

| # | Provision | Dealer Standard | Buy-Side Standard | Negotiation Strategy | AI Prompt |
|---|----------|----------------|-------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Cross-Default Threshold** | Low ($10M-25M) — easy to trigger against counterparty | High ($100M+) — harder to trigger | Negotiate symmetry — same threshold both ways | "Compare cross-default thresholds for both parties and assess whether they are proportionate to each party's balance sheet" |
| 2 | **Specified Entities** | Broad — includes all counterparty affiliates | Narrow — Party B only | Limit to material affiliates | "List all Specified Entities and identify which trigger cross-default or credit events" |
| 3 | **Automatic Early Termination** | Elected for non-US counterparties | Not elected (prefer optional termination) | Elect only where legally required (non-netting jurisdictions) | "Identify AET elections and assess whether they are appropriate for each party's jurisdiction" |
| 4 | **Threshold Amount (CSA)** | Zero for counterparty (full collateralization) | Positive threshold (reduces operational burden) | Tiered by credit rating | "Extract Threshold Amounts for both parties and map to credit rating triggers" |
| 5 | **Eligible Collateral** | Cash only (USD, EUR) | Broad (cash, treasuries, agencies, equities) | Expand collateral types with appropriate haircuts | "List all Eligible Collateral types with haircuts and compare to market standard for [counterparty type]" |
| 6 | **Transfer Timing** | Same-day or T+1 for counterparty calls | T+1 or T+2 (operational flexibility) | T+1 standard for both | "Identify all transfer timing provisions and flag asymmetries" |
| 7 | **Close-out Methodology** | Close-out Amount (2002 — dealer-favorable flexibility) | Market Quotation (1992 — more constrained) | 2002 Close-out Amount is market standard post-2008 | "Identify which close-out methodology is elected and any modifications to the calculation process" |
| 8 | **Set-off** | Broad set-off rights (can offset across affiliates) | Limited set-off (only under this agreement) | Negotiate scope carefully | "Extract all set-off provisions and identify cross-affiliate rights" |

## AI-Powered Schedule Comparison

The highest-value AI application in Schedule negotiation is **comparative analysis**: given two Schedules (your standard vs. the counterparty's standard), identify every material difference.

**Prompt template:**
```
I am providing two ISDA Schedules:
- Document A: [Your firm's standard Schedule]
- Document B: [Counterparty's proposed Schedule]

Compare every provision and produce a table with columns:
1. Provision (Schedule Part/Section reference)
2. Document A position
3. Document B position
4. Material difference? (Yes/No)
5. Which party does Document B's position favor?
6. Recommended negotiation response

Focus on provisions that affect: credit risk (thresholds, events of default), close-out mechanics (termination, netting, calculation), collateral (CSA terms, eligible collateral, haircuts), and operational provisions (notice, timing, delivery).
```

This comparison — which would take a lawyer 2-3 hours to do manually — takes AI approximately 2 minutes. The output is a structured negotiation map showing every point of difference and its business significance.

## Generating Negotiation Memos

After the comparison, use AI to draft the negotiation memo — the internal document explaining your recommended positions to the business team:

**Prompt template:**
```
Based on the Schedule comparison above, draft a negotiation memo for the derivatives trading desk. For each material difference:
1. State the counterparty's proposed position
2. Explain the risk to our firm if we accept it
3. Recommend our counter-position
4. Identify potential compromises
5. Flag any "must-have" vs. "nice-to-have" positions

Organize by priority: critical issues first, then significant issues, then minor issues. Use plain language that a trader (not a lawyer) can understand.
```

## Redline Generation

AI can generate a redline markup of the counterparty's Schedule against your standard, with explanatory comments for each proposed change:

```
Generate a redline of Document B against Document A. For each difference:
- Show the counterparty's language in [brackets]
- Propose replacement language based on our standard position
- Add a comment explaining the business significance of the change
- Flag provisions where the counterparty's position is reasonable and we should accept it
```
