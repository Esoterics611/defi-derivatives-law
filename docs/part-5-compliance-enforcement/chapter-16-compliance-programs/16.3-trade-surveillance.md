# 16.3 Trade Surveillance

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Distinguish between pre-trade controls and post-trade surveillance
    - Identify CFTC Rule 23.602's diligent supervision requirements
    - Evaluate TradFi surveillance tools and their DeFi equivalents
    - Design a surveillance approach for DeFi derivatives trading

---

## Pre-Trade Controls

Pre-trade controls prevent violations before they happen. They are automated checks embedded in the order management system that block or flag trades that violate defined parameters:

**Order size limits.** Maximum order sizes prevent outsized positions that could move markets or expose the firm to excessive risk. For swap dealers, CFTC rules require position limits monitoring (covered in Chapter 18). Pre-trade controls enforce these limits in real time.

**Price collars.** Orders that deviate significantly from the current market price — typically more than a defined percentage — are held for manual review. Price collars catch "fat-finger" errors (a trader entering $1 million instead of $100,000) and potential manipulation (intentionally disruptive orders).

**Counterparty credit checks.** Before executing a bilateral swap, the system verifies that the counterparty has sufficient credit capacity under the existing credit support annex. This prevents accumulation of un-collateralized exposure.

**Product authorization.** Traders can only execute products they are authorized to trade. An interest rate swaps trader cannot enter into a credit default swap without explicit approval.

**DeFi pre-trade controls:** DeFi protocols implement analogous controls at the smart contract level. Uniswap's slippage tolerance is a price collar. Aave's borrowing caps are position limits. Protocol-level access controls (whitelisting, KYC gates) are counterparty checks. The difference: TradFi pre-trade controls are configured by compliance; DeFi pre-trade controls are hard-coded by developers.

## Post-Trade Surveillance

Post-trade surveillance analyzes completed transactions to detect patterns indicative of manipulation, abuse, or violations. This is where the heavy compliance investment goes — sophisticated pattern recognition across millions of transactions.

Key surveillance patterns:

| Surveillance Function | What It Detects | Detection Method | TradFi Tool | DeFi On-Chain Equivalent |
|----------------------|----------------|-----------------|-------------|------------------------|
| Wash trading detection | Simultaneous buy/sell by the same entity | Matching counterparty IDs, timing analysis, volume-to-OI ratio | NICE Actimize, Nasdaq SMARTS | Chainalysis, Nansen wallet clustering |
| Spoofing detection | Orders placed with intent to cancel | Order-to-trade ratio, time-in-force analysis, pattern matching | Eventus, Trading Technologies | Mempool analysis, reverted transaction monitoring |
| Front-running detection | Trading ahead of known client orders | Timing analysis, information barrier monitoring | Internal surveillance systems | MEV analysis (Flashbots Protect, EigenPhi) |
| Position concentration | Outsized positions relative to market | Position-to-OI ratio, large trader reporting | CFTC large trader system (Form 40) | On-chain position monitoring (Dune, Nansen) |
| Unusual activity review | Trading patterns deviating from historical norms | Behavioral analytics, peer comparison, statistical anomaly detection | Behavox, NICE Actimize | Anomaly detection on wallet activity graphs |
| Cross-market surveillance | Manipulation spanning multiple venues | Cross-venue trade matching, timing correlation | Market-wide consolidated audit trail | Cross-protocol analytics (Arkham Intelligence) |

## CFTC Rule 23.602: Diligent Supervision

Rule 23.602 requires swap dealers and MSPs to establish and maintain a system to supervise activities that is "reasonably designed to achieve compliance with applicable CFTC requirements." The rule demands:

- **Written supervisory procedures**: documenting who supervises what, how supervision is conducted, and how exceptions are escalated
- **Designated supervisors**: named individuals responsible for each business function
- **Review procedures**: regular review of trading activity, correspondence, and complaints

The "reasonably designed" standard is important. The CFTC does not require perfection — it requires that the system be designed with reasonable care and implemented in good faith. When violations occur despite a well-designed surveillance system, the CFTC is significantly more lenient than when violations occur because the system was inadequate.

## Building DeFi Surveillance

On-chain data provides a surveillance advantage that TradFi compliance officers would envy: every transaction is publicly visible, timestamped, and permanently recorded. The challenge is converting raw blockchain data into compliance-relevant intelligence.

**Layer 1: Transaction monitoring.** Real-time ingestion of all protocol transactions. Tools: custom indexers (The Graph subgraphs), Alchemy/Infura webhooks, or protocol-specific event listeners. Every swap, deposit, withdrawal, liquidation, and governance action is captured.

**Layer 2: Wallet clustering.** Identifying wallet addresses that belong to the same entity. Tools: Chainalysis, Nansen, Arkham Intelligence. Wallet clustering is essential for detecting wash trading (same entity on both sides) and position concentration (single entity across multiple wallets).

**Layer 3: Pattern detection.** Applying surveillance algorithms to clustered data. Detect: wash trading patterns, sandwich attacks, unusual position building before governance votes, oracle price manipulation attempts. This layer is where AI models add the most value — pattern recognition across high-dimensional on-chain data.

**Layer 4: Alert generation and investigation.** When patterns exceed defined thresholds, generate alerts for human review. Not every anomaly is a violation — the compliance team must investigate, document findings, and escalate confirmed issues through the procedures defined in Section 16.1.

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note: Building AI-Powered Trade Surveillance"
    For DeFi protocol monitoring, use this prompt structure:

    *"Design a trade surveillance system for [protocol name] that monitors for: (1) wash trading — identify transactions where the same entity (or clustered wallets) appears on both sides, (2) manipulation — detect unusual trading volume or price impact preceding governance votes or oracle updates, (3) position concentration — flag wallets whose aggregate position exceeds [X]% of total protocol TVL, (4) sandwich attacks — identify transactions that front-run and back-run user swaps within the same block. For each detection rule, specify: the on-chain data source, the detection algorithm, the alert threshold, and the expected false positive rate."*

    **What to Verify:** AI-generated surveillance rules will be directionally correct but often set thresholds too aggressively (generating too many alerts) or too loosely (missing genuine violations). Calibrate thresholds against historical data and adjust based on investigation outcomes.

## Section Summary

- Pre-trade controls prevent violations in real time; post-trade surveillance detects them after execution
- CFTC Rule 23.602 requires "reasonably designed" supervisory systems — not perfection, but good-faith design
- TradFi surveillance tools (NICE Actimize, Nasdaq SMARTS) have DeFi equivalents (Chainalysis, Nansen, Arkham)
- DeFi's on-chain transparency provides a surveillance data advantage, but converting data to intelligence requires layered analytical infrastructure
- AI models are particularly effective at pattern detection across high-dimensional on-chain data
