# 3.2 Dodd-Frank Title VII: The Post-2008 Revolution

On July 21, 2010, President Obama signed the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act into law. **Title VII** — the "Wall Street Transparency and Accountability Act" — transformed the regulation of OTC derivatives from a regime of deliberate non-regulation (the CFMA era) into one of the most comprehensive regulatory frameworks in financial history.

## 3.2.1 The Problem Title VII Solved

Before Dodd-Frank, the OTC derivatives market operated in a regulatory shadow. The CFMA of 2000 had deliberately exempted OTC derivatives from CEA regulation based on the theory that sophisticated institutional counterparties didn't need regulatory protection. The 2008 crisis proved this theory catastrophically wrong.

The pre-Dodd-Frank problems:

| Problem | Manifestation | Consequence |
|---------|--------------|-------------|
| **No transparency** | No one — including regulators — knew the total outstanding OTC derivatives positions | Regulators couldn't assess systemic risk |
| **No clearing** | Most OTC swaps were bilateral, with no central counterparty | Counterparty failures cascaded (Lehman, AIG) |
| **No margin requirements** | OTC derivatives could be entered with little or no collateral | Massive leverage accumulated invisibly |
| **No exchange trading** | OTC swaps were negotiated bilaterally, often by phone | No pre-trade price transparency |
| **No reporting** | No requirement to report OTC transactions to anyone | Market surveillance was impossible |
| **No dealer regulation** | Swap dealers operated without registration or conduct requirements | Conflicts of interest were rampant |

## 3.2.2 The Five Pillars of Title VII

Title VII created a comprehensive framework built on five pillars:

### Pillar 1: Mandatory Clearing

Standardized swaps determined by the CFTC to be required to be cleared must be submitted to a registered Derivatives Clearing Organization (DCO). The clearing mandate interposes a central counterparty between swap counterparties, eliminating bilateral counterparty risk.

Currently, the CFTC has mandated clearing for certain **interest rate swaps** (fixed-to-floating in USD, EUR, GBP, JPY) and certain **credit default swaps** (CDS index products). The clearing mandate does not yet extend to digital asset swaps — but this could change as the market matures.

### Pillar 2: Exchange/SEF Trading

Swaps subject to the clearing mandate must also be traded on a DCM or SEF, providing pre-trade price transparency and regulated execution. This pillar directly challenges DeFi derivatives protocols: if a DeFi swap is subject to the trading mandate, it must be traded on a registered venue.

### Pillar 3: Trade Reporting

ALL swaps — cleared and uncleared — must be reported to registered Swap Data Repositories (SDRs). Reporting is divided into:
- **Real-time public reporting** (Part 43): anonymized price and volume data disseminated publicly
- **Regulatory reporting** (Part 45): full transaction data including counterparty identities, reported to the CFTC

### Pillar 4: Dealer Registration & Regulation

Entities that meet the "swap dealer" definition must register with the CFTC and comply with:
- Capital and margin requirements
- Business conduct standards (external and internal)
- Recordkeeping and reporting obligations
- Chief Compliance Officer requirements
- Risk management program requirements

### Pillar 5: Position Limits

The CFTC was directed to establish position limits for swaps that are "economically equivalent" to exchange-traded futures, preventing excessive concentration that could lead to manipulation or systemic risk.

## 3.2.3 The DeFi Implications of Each Pillar

| Title VII Pillar | DeFi Impact | Current Enforcement Status |
|-----------------|-------------|---------------------------|
| **Mandatory Clearing** | DeFi swaps are not currently subject to the clearing mandate, but DeFi protocols function as de facto clearing systems (over-collateralization + liquidation) | Not yet enforced against DeFi |
| **Exchange/SEF Trading** | DeFi protocols that facilitate swap trading for US persons may be operating as unregistered SEFs | Active enforcement (Ooki DAO, Deridex, ZeroEx) |
| **Trade Reporting** | DeFi transactions are on-chain (public) but not reported to registered SDRs in the required format | Not yet enforced, but a likely future requirement |
| **Dealer Registration** | DeFi market makers exceeding the de minimis threshold may technically be unregistered swap dealers | Not yet enforced against individual DeFi actors |
| **Position Limits** | No position limits apply to DeFi derivatives; on-chain analysis could enable this | Not yet enforced |

!!! warning ""
    **The Enforcement Trajectory**: The CFTC has focused first on the lowest-hanging enforcement fruit — unregistered trading platforms (Pillar 2). But each subsequent pillar represents a future enforcement frontier. Expect trade reporting, dealer registration, and eventually clearing requirements to be extended to DeFi as the market grows and regulatory resources increase.


## 3.2.4 Title VII Rulemaking: Where to Find the Rules

Title VII is the statute. The CFTC implements it through regulations published in Title 17 of the Code of Federal Regulations (17 CFR). The key Parts:

| 17 CFR Part | Subject | Key Provisions |
|-------------|---------|----------------|
| Part 1 | General regulations | Definitions, margin for futures |
| Part 23 | Swap dealers and MSPs | Registration, capital, margin, business conduct, reporting |
| Part 37 | Swap execution facilities | Core Principles, registration, operational requirements |
| Part 39 | Derivatives clearing organizations | Core Principles, risk management, financial resources |
| Part 43 | Real-time public reporting | Swap transaction data dissemination |
| Part 45 | Swap data recordkeeping and reporting | Regulatory reporting to SDRs |
| Part 49 | Swap data repositories | SDR registration and operation requirements |
| Part 50 | Clearing requirement | Which swaps must be cleared |
| Part 150 | Limits on positions | Position limits for physical delivery commodity derivatives |
| Part 180 | Prohibition on manipulation | Anti-manipulation rules (Rule 180.1, modeled on SEC Rule 10b-5) |

    When researching CFTC rules, always direct AI to the specific 17 CFR Part. AI performs dramatically better on regulatory questions when given precise rule references.

    **What to Verify:** AI sometimes cites rules that have been amended, reproposed, or vacated. Always cross-reference against the current version on eCFR.gov. CFTC rulemaking is ongoing — a rule that existed in 2023 may have been modified by 2025.
