# 13.3 On-Chain Collateral Management

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Describe how DeFi protocols manage collateral on-chain
    - Identify the major collateral types and their risk profiles
    - Explain rehypothecation chains and recursive leverage in DeFi
    - Assess the systemic risk created by collateral chain dependencies

---

## DeFi Collateral Mechanics

DeFi collateral management operates through a simple but powerful pattern: **deposit, lock, monitor, liquidate.** A user deposits collateral tokens into a smart contract, the contract locks those tokens, continuously monitors the position's health ratio (collateral value relative to borrowed/leveraged value), and automatically triggers liquidation if the ratio falls below a protocol-defined threshold.

This pattern eliminates several TradFi friction points: no counterparty credit risk (collateral is held in a smart contract, not by the counterparty), no margin call delays (monitoring is continuous, not daily), no dispute about collateral valuation (oracle price is deterministic and publicly verifiable), and no custodian risk (the smart contract is the custodian).

But it creates new risks: smart contract risk (bugs or exploits can drain collateral), oracle risk (incorrect price data triggers inappropriate liquidations or fails to trigger necessary ones), and composability risk (collateral tokens may themselves depend on other protocols that can fail).

## Collateral Types and Risk Profiles

| Collateral Type | Examples | Volatility | Liquidation Risk | Rehypothecation Risk | TradFi Equivalent |
|----------------|----------|-----------|-----------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Stablecoins** | USDC, USDT, DAI | Low (peg maintained) | Low | Low (no further leverage) | Cash collateral |
| **Major L1 tokens** | ETH, SOL | High (30-50% drawdowns in days) | Medium-High | Medium (can be staked, lent) | No direct equivalent — high-volatility asset as collateral |
| **Liquid Staking Tokens (LSTs)** | stETH, rETH, cbETH | High (ETH volatility + depeg risk) | High | High (already one layer of protocol dependency) | Agency MBS (collateral whose value depends on another institution's solvency) |
| **LP tokens** | Uniswap LP, Curve LP | High (impermanent loss + underlying volatility) | Very High | Very High (multi-protocol dependency) | Structured product tranches as collateral |
| **Wrapped/bridged assets** | wBTC, bridged USDC | Underlying volatility + bridge risk | Medium-High | Medium (bridge security dependency) | Foreign currency collateral with correspondent bank risk |
| **Governance tokens** | UNI, AAVE, MKR | Very High (protocol-specific risk + market beta) | Very High | Low (limited utility in other protocols) | Equity collateral (most volatile, highest haircuts) |

## Rehypothecation and Collateral Chains

In TradFi, rehypothecation is the practice of a collateral receiver reusing pledged collateral for its own purposes (posting it to another counterparty, selling it short, etc.). Under the English Law CSA (title transfer), rehypothecation is inherent — the receiver owns the collateral. Under the New York Law CSA, rehypothecation must be specifically elected. Under UMR, rehypothecation of initial margin is **prohibited** — IM must be held in segregated third-party custody.

DeFi has no prohibition on rehypothecation — in fact, the composable nature of DeFi tokens makes rehypothecation the default behavior. A typical collateral chain:

1. User stakes ETH → receives stETH (liquid staking token)
2. User deposits stETH into Aave → borrows USDC
3. User deposits USDC into GMX → opens leveraged long on ETH
4. ETH price drops → GMX position liquidated → Aave position approaches liquidation → stETH depeg risk increases

Each step in this chain creates a **dependency**: the health of position 3 depends on position 2, which depends on position 1, which depends on the Lido staking contract and the ETH price. A single failure point — an oracle malfunction, a stETH depeg, a Lido smart contract exploit — cascades through every position in the chain.

!!! danger "Recursive Leverage and CDO-Squared Risk"
    The DeFi collateral chain described above is structurally analogous to the CDO-squared problem that amplified the 2008 financial crisis. A CDO-squared was a CDO backed by tranches of other CDOs — creating layers of leverage where the collapse of underlying mortgages cascaded through multiple layers of structured products. DeFi's composable collateral chains create the same structure: leverage built on leverage, with each layer depending on the solvency of the layer below it.

    The critical difference: in 2008, the collateral chain was opaque — investors in CDO-squared products did not know what mortgages backed the underlying CDOs. In DeFi, the chain is transparent — anyone can trace the dependency path on-chain. Transparency does not prevent cascading failures, but it does enable earlier detection and faster response.

## Systemic Risk Assessment

The systemic risk from DeFi collateral chains has already materialized:

**Terra/Luna (May 2022):** The collapse of UST's peg destroyed the entire Terra ecosystem, but the contagion spread through collateral chains to Anchor (which held UST deposits as collateral for yield), to Celsius and Three Arrows Capital (which held LUNA/UST positions across multiple protocols), and ultimately to centralized lenders and exchanges.

**stETH depeg (June 2022):** When Celsius's solvency was questioned, forced selling of stETH positions widened the stETH-ETH discount. Every protocol accepting stETH as collateral saw position health ratios deteriorate. Aave governance debated emergency measures to reduce stETH exposure.

These events demonstrate that DeFi's collateral chains create system-wide contagion paths that regulators — particularly the CFTC for derivatives-related chains — will eventually address.

## Section Summary

- DeFi collateral management follows a deposit-lock-monitor-liquidate pattern, eliminating counterparty risk but creating smart contract and oracle risk
- Collateral types range from low-risk stablecoins to high-risk LP and governance tokens
- Composable rehypothecation chains create recursive leverage analogous to pre-2008 CDO-squared structures
- The transparency of on-chain collateral chains enables detection but does not prevent cascading failures
