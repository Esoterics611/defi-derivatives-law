# 14.3 Early Termination & Calculation Agent Disputes

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Describe the early termination process under ISDA Section 6
    - Explain the Close-out Amount calculation methodology
    - Identify common Calculation Agent disputes and their resolution
    - Compare ISDA's judgment-based close-out to DeFi's automated liquidation

---

## The Early Termination Process

Once an Event of Default has occurred (and any applicable grace period has expired), the non-defaulting party may elect to terminate all outstanding transactions by designating an **Early Termination Date** under Section 6(a).

The process under the 2002 Master Agreement:

**Step 1 — Designation notice.** The non-defaulting party sends written notice specifying the Early Termination Date (which may be the same day as the notice or a future date, no later than 20 days after the notice).

**Step 2 — Close-out Amount calculation.** The non-defaulting party (or the Calculation Agent, if designated) determines the Close-out Amount for each terminated transaction. The Close-out Amount represents "the amount of the losses or costs of the [determining] party that are or would be incurred under then prevailing circumstances... in replacing, or in providing for the [determining] party the economic equivalent of" the terminated transaction.

**Step 3 — Netting.** The individual Close-out Amounts are aggregated (positive and negative), along with any Unpaid Amounts (amounts due but not paid before termination), to produce a single **Early Termination Amount**. One party owes the other this net figure.

**Step 4 — Payment.** The Early Termination Amount is due within one business day of the calculation being completed and notice being given.

## Close-out Amount: The Valuation Standard

The 2002 Master Agreement's Close-out Amount standard replaced the 1992 version's dual "Market Quotation" and "Loss" methods. The key features:

**Commercially reasonable procedures.** The determining party must use commercially reasonable procedures to calculate the replacement cost. This includes obtaining market quotations from dealers, using internal models, or referencing relevant market data. The standard is flexible — there is no single prescribed methodology.

**Good faith.** The determining party must act in good faith. This is a lower standard than fiduciary duty but higher than pure self-interest. A Calculation Agent that deliberately undervalues the defaulter's in-the-money positions to reduce its own payout would breach the good faith obligation.

**Multiple valuation sources.** The determining party should consider information from third-party dealers, market data, and its own models. Reliance on a single source is permissible but may be challenged if other sources would have produced materially different results.

## Calculation Agent Disputes

Disputes over the Close-out Amount are among the most contentious issues in derivatives practice. Common scenarios:

**Illiquid markets.** When the terminated transaction involves an illiquid or exotic product, dealer quotations may be unavailable or vary widely. The Calculation Agent must exercise judgment about which quotations are reliable and how to value positions for which no market exists.

**Conflict of interest.** When the non-defaulting party is also the Calculation Agent (the most common arrangement), it has an inherent conflict: its close-out valuation determines how much it owes or is owed. This conflict is mitigated by the good faith and commercially reasonable standards but not eliminated.

**Methodology disagreements.** Parties may disagree about the appropriate discount rate, volatility assumption, or model for valuing complex transactions. The 2002 Master Agreement does not prescribe a specific methodology — only that the methodology be commercially reasonable.

Resolution typically follows a path: informal negotiation → formal dispute notice → independent valuation (if agreed) → arbitration or litigation. The entire process can take months or years.

| Close-out Step | ISDA 2002 Process | Timeline | DeFi Equivalent |
|---------------|-------------------|----------|-----------------|
| Trigger identification | Non-defaulting party identifies Event of Default | Hours to days | Oracle price crosses liquidation threshold (milliseconds) |
| Termination notice | Written notice with designated date | 1-20 days | No notice — position immediately eligible for liquidation |
| Valuation | Commercially reasonable procedures; dealer quotations; internal models | 1-10 business days | Oracle price at moment of liquidation (single data point) |
| Netting | Aggregate all Close-out Amounts | Calculated as part of valuation | Per-position (no cross-position netting) |
| Payment | Wire transfer within 1 business day of calculation | T+1 to T+15 depending on complexity | Atomic — collateral transfer in same block as liquidation |
| Dispute resolution | Negotiation → arbitration → litigation | Months to years | None — execution is final |

## Section Summary

- Early termination under Section 6 involves notice, Close-out Amount calculation, netting, and payment
- The Close-out Amount standard requires commercially reasonable procedures and good faith
- Calculation Agent disputes arise from illiquid markets, conflicts of interest, and methodology disagreements
- DeFi replaces the entire judgment-based process with a single oracle price reading and automatic execution
