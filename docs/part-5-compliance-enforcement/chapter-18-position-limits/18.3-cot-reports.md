# 18.3 Commitment of Traders Reports

    - Interpret the CFTC's weekly Commitment of Traders (COT) reports
    - Distinguish between commercial, non-commercial, and nonreportable trader categories
    - Describe the Traders in Financial Futures (TFF) and Disaggregated COT supplements
    - Assess how DeFi protocols could publish equivalent transparency data

## The COT Report

Every Tuesday, the CFTC collects position data from all reporting traders. Every Friday, it publishes the **Commitment of Traders (COT) report** — the most widely followed derivatives market transparency publication in the world.

The COT report aggregates large trader positions into categories, showing how different types of market participants are positioned. Traders, analysts, and regulators use COT data to understand market structure: who is long, who is short, and how positioning is changing week to week.

## Legacy COT Categories

The traditional (legacy) COT report divides positions into three categories:

**Commercial** — traders who use futures to hedge a position in the underlying physical commodity. Identified by Form 40 designation as commercial hedgers. Examples: grain elevators hedging crop inventory, oil refiners hedging crude purchases, airlines hedging jet fuel costs.

**Non-commercial (speculative)** — traders whose positions are speculative, not hedging physical exposure. This category includes hedge funds, managed money, commodity trading advisors, and proprietary trading firms.

**Nonreportable** — positions held by traders below the reportable threshold. The CFTC calculates this category as the residual: total open interest minus commercial minus non-commercial.

| COT Category | Definition | What It Reveals | Trading Signal |
|-------------|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| Commercial (long) | Hedgers with physical commodity exposure, using futures to sell forward | Producers expect lower prices (hedging future sales) | Commercial shorts at extremes may signal price bottoms |
| Commercial (short) | Hedgers buying futures to lock in input costs | Consumers expect higher prices | Commercial longs at extremes may signal price tops |
| Non-commercial (long) | Speculators betting on price increases | Speculative conviction on the long side | Extreme spec longs often precede corrections |
| Non-commercial (short) | Speculators betting on price decreases | Speculative conviction on the short side | Extreme spec shorts often precede rallies |
| Nonreportable | Small traders below reporting thresholds | Retail sentiment | Often contrarian indicator |

## Disaggregated COT

For physical commodities, the CFTC publishes a **Disaggregated COT** that breaks the legacy categories into finer classifications:

- **Producer/Merchant/Processor/User**: entities with physical commodity exposure
- **Swap Dealers**: entities using futures to hedge OTC swap positions
- **Managed Money**: CTAs, hedge funds, and other speculative fund managers
- **Other Reportables**: large traders not fitting the above categories

The disaggregated report is more useful than the legacy version because it separates swap dealer hedging (which can be massive and mechanical) from speculative managed money positioning (which reflects market views).

## Traders in Financial Futures (TFF)

For financial futures (equity indexes, interest rates, currencies), the CFTC publishes the **Traders in Financial Futures (TFF)** report with its own categories:

- **Dealer/Intermediary**: typically sell-side banks hedging client-facing positions
- **Asset Manager/Institutional**: buy-side funds, pension funds, insurance companies
- **Leveraged Funds**: hedge funds and speculative traders
- **Other Reportables**: everyone else above the threshold

## How Traders Use COT Data

COT reports are market intelligence tools. Common analytical frameworks:

**Extreme positioning analysis.** When non-commercial (speculative) net positions reach historical extremes — measured as a percentage of total open interest or compared to multi-year ranges — it often signals an impending reversal. Extreme spec longs precede sell-offs; extreme spec shorts precede rallies. The logic: when everyone who wants to buy has already bought, there's no one left to push prices higher.

**Commercial-speculative divergence.** When commercial and speculative traders are on opposite sides at extreme levels, the commercial position is typically the better predictor. Commercials have information advantages about the physical supply/demand picture.

**Open interest context.** Rising open interest with rising prices suggests new money entering long positions (bullish continuation). Rising open interest with falling prices suggests new short selling (bearish continuation). Falling open interest suggests position unwinding regardless of price direction.

## DeFi Equivalents

DeFi has all the raw data needed to produce COT-equivalent reports — and more. Every position on every protocol is publicly visible on-chain. The challenge is categorization: who is a hedger, who is a speculator, who is a market maker?

**Potential DeFi transparency reports:**
- **Protocol-level position reports**: Aggregate open interest by wallet cluster size (whale, medium, retail), direction (long/short), and collateral type
- **Cross-protocol concentration reports**: Identify entities with large positions across multiple protocols — the DeFi equivalent of aggregate large trader reporting
- **Real-time dashboards**: Unlike the CFTC's weekly publication schedule, DeFi position data can be published in real time. Dune Analytics and Nansen already provide versions of this for specific protocols

The missing piece is trader classification. On-chain analytics can estimate wallet categories (smart money, new money, protocol treasuries, market makers) using behavioral heuristics, but these are probabilistic estimates — not Form 40 self-declarations.

## Section Summary

- COT reports aggregate large trader positions into commercial (hedger) and non-commercial (speculative) categories, published weekly
- Disaggregated COT and TFF supplements provide finer classification for physical and financial commodities respectively
- COT data is a market intelligence tool: extreme positioning often signals reversals
- DeFi has more raw position data than TradFi but lacks the classification framework — categorizing on-chain positions requires behavioral heuristics rather than self-declaration
