# 18.1 Federal Position Limits

    - Distinguish between spot month, single month, and all-months-combined limits
    - Explain the aggregation requirement and common ownership/control tests
    - Describe the bona fide hedging exemption and its conditions
    - Assess DeFi protocol position caps as regulatory analogues

## CFTC Rule Part 150: The Position Limit Framework

CFTC Part 150 establishes **federal position limits** on 25 core physical-delivery commodity futures and options contracts. These limits cap the maximum position any single entity (or group of entities under common control) may hold. The purpose: prevent excessive speculation, market manipulation, and cornering.

The 25 covered contracts include the most economically significant physical commodity markets:

- **Agricultural**: corn, wheat (CBOT and KC), soybeans, soybean oil, soybean meal, oats, cotton, rice, cocoa, coffee, sugar, orange juice
- **Energy**: WTI crude oil, Henry Hub natural gas, RBOB gasoline, heating oil
- **Metals**: gold, silver, copper, platinum, palladium

Position limits are set at three levels:

| Limit Type | Definition | Purpose | Example (WTI Crude) |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Spot month** | Maximum position during the delivery month or last trading days | Prevent squeezes and cornering of deliverable supply | 6,000 contracts (~6M barrels) |
| **Single month** | Maximum position in any one futures month (non-spot) | Prevent concentration in any expiry | ~33,000 contracts |
| **All months combined** | Maximum aggregate position across all expiry months | Prevent overall market dominance | ~33,000 contracts |

Spot month limits are the tightest because the delivery month is when manipulation risk is highest — a trader with a dominant position in the expiring contract can squeeze counterparties who need to deliver or roll their positions.

## The Aggregation Requirement

Position limits apply not just to individual accounts but to all positions **under common ownership or control**. CFTC Rule 150.4 requires aggregation when:

- Two or more accounts have the **same beneficial owner** (direct or indirect)
- A person **controls** the trading of another account (e.g., fund manager + fund)
- **Common ownership**: entities sharing 10% or more ownership must aggregate unless they qualify for an exemption (e.g., independent account controllers operating with separate trading decisions)

The aggregation requirement is critical. Without it, a single entity could circumvent position limits by opening accounts at multiple brokers or through multiple subsidiaries. With aggregation, the CFTC looks through the entity structure to the ultimate beneficial owner.

**DeFi implication:** Aggregation is the position limit concept most fundamentally challenged by DeFi. In TradFi, aggregation works because accounts have named owners, brokers maintain customer records, and the CFTC can subpoena ownership information. In DeFi, wallet addresses are pseudonymous, creating new addresses is free, and no centralized custodian maintains beneficial ownership records. A single entity can hold positions across hundreds of wallets with no obligation to aggregate them.

## Bona Fide Hedging Exemption

CFTC Rule 150.1 defines a **bona fide hedging** position as one that:

1. Represents a substitute for a transaction in the physical commodity market
2. Is economically appropriate to reduce risks in the conduct of a commercial enterprise
3. Arises from the potential change in value of assets, liabilities, or services

Commercial hedgers — producers, processors, merchants, and users of the underlying commodity — can apply for exemptions from position limits. A grain elevator operator hedging its physical inventory with short futures is not speculating; it's managing legitimate commercial risk.

The exemption requires documentation: the applicant must demonstrate the physical market exposure, the relationship between the hedge and the commercial risk, and that the position doesn't exceed the hedging need.

| Exemption Element | Requirement | Documentation | DeFi Parallel |
|------------------|------------|--------------|---------------|
| Commercial enterprise | Hedger must be engaged in the physical commodity business | Business records, physical inventory data | Protocol treasury hedging? Debatable — most DeFi actors are speculators |
| Economic appropriateness | Hedge must be reasonably correlated to the commercial risk | Risk analysis showing hedge-physical correlation | On-chain correlation analysis between protocol exposure and hedge position |
| Not exceeding need | Position cannot exceed the hedging requirement | Physical market position vs. derivatives position comparison | Smart contract-enforced position caps based on treasury exposure |

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note: Monitoring Multi-Wallet Position Concentration"
    For DeFi protocols, position limit monitoring requires wallet clustering:

    *"Design a position concentration monitoring system for [protocol name] that: (1) ingests all open positions from on-chain data, (2) applies wallet clustering algorithms to group addresses by funding source, behavioral patterns, and transaction correlation, (3) calculates aggregate position size per entity cluster, (4) flags clusters exceeding [X]% of total open interest or [Y] notional value, (5) generates daily position concentration reports comparable to CFTC COT data. Specify the clustering methodology, confidence thresholds for entity identification, and alert criteria."*

    **What to Verify:** AI-generated clustering approaches will be conceptually sound but overoptimistic about accuracy. Wallet clustering produces probabilistic entity identification, not certainty. Set confidence thresholds conservatively — it's better to investigate 10 false positives than miss one concentrated position.

## Section Summary

- CFTC Part 150 imposes federal position limits on 25 core physical-delivery commodity contracts at three levels: spot month, single month, all months combined
- The aggregation requirement combines positions under common ownership or control — the mechanism that prevents circumvention through multiple accounts
- Bona fide hedging exemptions exist for commercial hedgers managing physical commodity risk
- DeFi's pseudonymous multi-wallet architecture fundamentally challenges the aggregation framework
