# 5.1 Swap Dealer Registration & De Minimis Threshold

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:
    - Apply the four-prong swap dealer test to any entity
    - Calculate whether an entity exceeds the de minimis threshold
    - Explain what counts (and what doesn't) toward the de minimis calculation
    - Analyze when DeFi market makers or protocol treasury operations might trigger SD registration


---

## The Four-Prong Swap Dealer Test

Under CEA §1a(49) and CFTC Regulation 1.3, an entity is a "swap dealer" if it meets **any one** of four tests:

### Prong 1: Holds Itself Out as a Dealer

The entity represents to the market that it is available to enter swaps on demand. This includes advertising swap-making services, publishing indicative swap quotes, or positioning itself as a provider of swap liquidity.

**DeFi question:** Does a DeFi protocol that publishes available liquidity and pricing for derivatives positions "hold itself out" as a dealer? The protocol's smart contract is continuously available to counterparties, publishes pricing, and accepts trades on demand — structurally similar to how a traditional swap dealer operates.

### Prong 2: Makes a Market in Swaps

The entity routinely makes itself available to enter swaps at quoted prices or on request, providing two-sided liquidity (willing to take both sides of the trade).

**DeFi question:** An AMM-based perpetual futures protocol provides two-sided liquidity by design — liquidity providers collectively make a market. Does the protocol (or its liquidity providers) satisfy this prong?

### Prong 3: Regularly Enters Swaps as Ordinary Course of Business

The entity enters swaps with counterparties as a regular business activity, not as an occasional participant. This is the broadest prong and the one most likely to capture active DeFi participants.

### Prong 4: Commonly Known as a Dealer

An entity that the market recognizes as a swap dealer, regardless of whether it formally describes itself as one.

!!! warning "The "Dealing" vs. "Trading" Distinction"
    Not everyone who enters swaps is a dealer. The CFTC distinguishes between **dealing** (entering swaps to accommodate counterparty demand, earning bid-ask spread) and **trading** (entering swaps to express a view or hedge, earning from position returns). Dealing = swap dealer. Trading = not a swap dealer, regardless of volume.

    The key factors for distinguishing dealing from trading:
    - Does the entity profit primarily from bid-ask spread or from position appreciation?
    - Does the entity accommodate the counterparty's needs or pursue its own objectives?
    - Does the entity routinely stand ready to enter transactions or enter selectively?
    - Does the entity provide liquidity to the market or consume it?


## The De Minimis Threshold

Even if an entity meets one of the four prongs, it is exempt from registration if its swap dealing activity falls below the **de minimis threshold**:

| Parameter | Current Rule |
|-----------|-------------|
| Threshold level | **$8 billion** aggregate gross notional amount |
| Measurement period | Preceding 12 months |
| Scope | All swaps where the entity acts in a dealing capacity |
| Originally set at | $3 billion (raised to $8 billion by CFTC order, made permanent in 2018) |

### What Counts Toward De Minimis

The calculation includes the **aggregate gross notional amount** of all swaps in which the entity acts as a dealer. Key counting rules:

| Included in Calculation | Excluded from Calculation |
|------------------------|--------------------------|
| All swaps entered in a dealing capacity | Swaps entered for hedging own risk |
| Swaps with non-affiliated counterparties | Swaps between affiliates under common control |
| Swaps in all asset classes | Security-based swaps (SEC jurisdiction) |
| Both cleared and uncleared swaps | Physical forward contracts (excluded from swap definition) |

!!! success "Worked Example"
    A crypto market-making firm provides OTC derivative quotes to institutional clients:
    - Q1: $500M notional in BTC options, $200M in ETH perpetual equivalents
    - Q2: $600M notional in BTC options, $300M in stablecoin interest rate swaps
    - Q3: $700M notional in BTC options, $400M in ETH options
    - Q4: $800M notional in BTC options, $500M in structured products

    **12-month total:** $500M + $200M + $600M + $300M + $700M + $400M + $800M + $500M = **$4.0 billion**

    This is **below** the $8 billion threshold. The firm is currently exempt from SD registration — but if volume grows 50% next year, it will cross the threshold and must register.


## The Registration Process

If the de minimis threshold is exceeded, the entity must:

1. **File CFTC Form 7-R** with the NFA (online registration)
2. **Join the NFA** as a swap dealer member (annual dues, examination fees)
3. **Designate a Chief Compliance Officer** (17 C.F.R. §3.3)
4. **Implement all Part 23 requirements** within the compliance timeline
5. **File annual compliance report** signed by the CCO

| Registration Requirement | Timeline | Estimated Cost |
|-------------------------|----------|----------------|
| NFA membership application | 60–90 days | $5,000–10,000 |
| Initial compliance build-out | 6–12 months | $2–10 million |
| Annual compliance operations | Ongoing | $10–50 million/year |
| Annual CCO report | Annual | Internal cost (significant) |

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note"
    Use AI to monitor de minimis threshold exposure across a portfolio of DeFi protocol interactions:

    *"I am a market-making firm that provides liquidity across the following DeFi derivatives protocols: [list protocols, estimated monthly notional on each]. Calculate my aggregate gross notional dealing exposure over the trailing 12 months. Flag if I am within 50% of the $8 billion de minimis threshold. For each protocol interaction, analyze whether my activity constitutes 'dealing' or 'trading' under CFTC interpretive guidance — specifically, am I accommodating counterparty demand (dealing) or expressing my own directional views (trading)?"*

    **What to verify:** AI will calculate the arithmetic correctly but may struggle with the dealing vs. trading classification. Always independently assess whether your activity looks like market-making (dealing) or position-taking (trading) — this distinction determines whether the notional counts toward your threshold.


---

## Section Summary

- The four-prong SD test captures entities that hold out, make markets, regularly deal, or are commonly known as dealers
- The dealing vs. trading distinction is critical — only dealing activity triggers registration
- The $8 billion de minimis threshold provides a safe harbor for smaller participants
- DeFi protocol operators and large liquidity providers face unresolved questions under all four prongs
- Registration costs $10-50 million annually in compliance — a potentially existential burden for DeFi entities
