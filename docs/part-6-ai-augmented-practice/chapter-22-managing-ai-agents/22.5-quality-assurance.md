# 22.5 Quality Assurance for AI Legal Output

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Define and measure accuracy, completeness, and consistency metrics for AI legal output
    - Build a QA pipeline from automated checks through human spot-checks
    - Construct evaluation datasets using known-good legal analyses as benchmarks
    - Design feedback loops that improve prompt quality based on QA results
    - Understand liability allocation when AI-assisted legal work contains errors

---

## QA Frameworks for AI Legal Output

AI-augmented legal work requires the same quality assurance rigor as any professional service — but the QA methods are different. You cannot proofread an AI's reasoning the way you proofread a human associate's memo. You need systematic, measurable quality controls.

### Accuracy Metrics

| Metric | What It Measures | How to Measure | Target Threshold |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Citation correctness** | % of citations that reference real, correctly-cited authorities | Automated database cross-check + human spot-check | ≥ 95% verified; 0% fabricated |
| **Legal reasoning validity** | Whether the analysis logically follows from cited authorities | Senior human review of reasoning chain | ≥ 90% logically sound |
| **Factual accuracy** | Whether factual statements about entities, dates, amounts are correct | Source document cross-reference | ≥ 98% for extracted facts |
| **Classification accuracy** | Whether regulatory classifications match expert assessment | Comparison against expert-reviewed benchmarks | ≥ 85% agreement with expert |

### Completeness Metrics

| Metric | What It Measures | How to Measure | Target Threshold |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Issue identification** | % of relevant issues identified vs. total issues that exist | Compare AI output against expert-identified issue set | ≥ 80% issue capture |
| **Authority coverage** | % of relevant authorities cited vs. all applicable authorities | Expert review of cited vs. missing authorities | ≥ 75% authority coverage |
| **Counterargument identification** | Whether opposing arguments are acknowledged | Review for one-sidedness in analysis | At least 1 counterargument per major conclusion |
| **Risk factor coverage** | Whether all material risks are identified | Compare against comprehensive risk checklist | ≥ 85% risk identification |

### Consistency Metrics

| Metric | What It Measures | How to Measure | Target Threshold |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Cross-run consistency** | Same question produces same analysis across runs | Run identical queries 5 times, compare outputs | ≥ 90% substantive consistency |
| **Cross-document consistency** | Analysis of related documents yields consistent conclusions | Compare analyses of related ISDA documents | No contradictions |
| **Temporal consistency** | Same facts analyzed at different times yield same results | Periodic re-analysis of benchmark cases | ≥ 95% consistency (except when law changes) |

## The QA Pipeline

Every piece of AI-generated legal output should pass through a structured QA pipeline before it reaches a client, counterparty, or regulator:

```
Stage 1: AUTOMATED CHECKS
  - Citation format validation (do citations follow expected patterns?)
  - Completeness checks (does output contain all required sections?)
  - Internal consistency (do citations match the propositions they support?)
  - Confidence score check (does any section fall below confidence threshold?)
  Time: 1-2 minutes (automated)

Stage 2: PEER AI REVIEW
  - Adversarial reviewer agent critiques the output (see Section 22.2)
  - Verification agent checks citations against databases
  - Second analysis agent independently answers the same question for comparison
  Time: 5-10 minutes (automated)

Stage 3: HUMAN SPOT-CHECK
  - Senior reviewer examines flagged items from Stages 1-2
  - Spot-checks 10-20% of unflagged citations and conclusions
  - Evaluates overall quality: reasoning sound, analysis complete, tone appropriate
  Time: 15-45 minutes (human)

Stage 4: CLIENT DELIVERY
  - Human adds professional judgment, context, and recommendations
  - Human takes responsibility for the final work product
  - AI contribution documented in work product metadata (for transparency)
  Time: varies (human)
```

## Building Evaluation Datasets

To measure QA metrics, you need benchmark datasets — known-good legal analyses you can compare AI output against.

**Sources of benchmark data:**

1. **Previously completed human-reviewed work product**: Legal memos, compliance assessments, and regulatory analyses that were reviewed and approved by experienced practitioners
2. **Published enforcement orders**: CFTC enforcement actions with known outcomes — the AI's analysis should reach conclusions consistent with the CFTC's findings
3. **Textbook examples**: Standard regulatory classification scenarios with established correct answers
4. **Expert-created test sets**: Deliberately constructed fact patterns with expert-determined correct analyses

**Building your evaluation set:**

| Category | Number of Examples | What It Tests | Source |
|----------|-------------------|---------------|--------|
| Regulatory classification | 20-30 | Can the AI correctly classify instruments under CEA definitions? | Protocol descriptions + expert classifications |
| ISDA extraction | 15-20 | Can the AI accurately extract elections from ISDA Schedules? | Redacted ISDA Schedules + manually verified extractions |
| Enforcement analysis | 10-15 | Can the AI correctly parse and analyze enforcement orders? | Published CFTC orders + expert analysis |
| Compliance assessment | 10-15 | Can the AI identify compliance gaps? | Protocol specs + known compliance issues |
| Legal reasoning | 15-20 | Can the AI apply legal tests to novel fact patterns? | Constructed scenarios + expert-determined answers |

## QA Scorecard

Track quality over time with a standardized scorecard:

| QA Dimension | Metric | Measurement Method | Acceptable Threshold | Remediation if Below |
|-------------|--------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Citation accuracy** | % verified citations | Database cross-check | ≥ 95% | Add citation verification step; refine prompts to prefer statutes over cases |
| **Reasoning quality** | Expert rating (1-5) | Senior reviewer scoring | ≥ 4.0 average | Refine chain-of-thought prompting; add reasoning validation step |
| **Completeness** | % issues captured | Comparison to expert benchmark | ≥ 80% | Expand system prompt with issue checklist; add completeness verification agent |
| **Consistency** | % agreement across runs | Multi-run comparison | ≥ 90% | Reduce temperature; add structured output constraints; pin model version |
| **Timeliness** | Average pipeline duration | Time tracking | < 2 hours for standard analyses | Optimize pipeline; parallelize agent execution |
| **Human revision rate** | % of output requiring substantive human edits | Track revisions in review | ≤ 30% | Analyze common revision patterns; update prompts to address |

## When AI Output Fails QA

Failed QA should trigger a systematic feedback loop:

1. **Identify the failure mode** — Which QA dimension failed? What specific error occurred?
2. **Trace to root cause** — Was it a prompt issue, a model limitation, a tool failure, or an evaluation error?
3. **Update the relevant component** — Refine the prompt, add a verification step, adjust the pipeline, or update the evaluation criteria
4. **Re-run against benchmarks** — Verify the fix improves the specific metric without degrading others
5. **Document the pattern** — Record the failure mode and fix in a "lessons learned" database for prompt engineering reference

## Liability Considerations

!!! danger "AI Limitation Warning"
    **Who is responsible when AI-assisted legal work contains errors?**

    The current legal framework is unambiguous: **the human professional who delivers the work product bears responsibility.** AI tools do not practice law, do not owe duties to clients, and cannot be held professionally liable. The attorney, compliance officer, or advisor who relies on AI output and delivers it to a client or regulator is fully responsible for its accuracy.

    This means:

    - **No AI tool eliminates the duty to verify.** "The AI said so" is not a defense to a malpractice claim or a regulatory enforcement action.
    - **Disclosure may be required.** Some jurisdictions and ethical rules require disclosure of AI use in legal work product. Track evolving bar association guidance.
    - **Supervisory obligations apply.** If you manage a team using AI tools, you have the same supervisory duty as you would over junior associates — you must ensure the quality of their output, whether human-generated or AI-assisted.
    - **The QA framework is your protection.** A documented, systematic QA process demonstrates reasonable professional care — even if an error ultimately slips through.

!!! tip "AI Practitioner Note"
    **How to build a legal AI QA scorecard and track quality over time:**

    1. **Define your metrics** using the tables above — select the metrics most relevant to your practice
    2. **Build your benchmark set** — start with 50 examples across the categories listed above; expand as you accumulate reviewed work product
    3. **Score monthly** — run your AI pipeline against the benchmark set once per month and record scores
    4. **Track trends** — are scores improving, stable, or declining? Model updates, prompt changes, and pipeline modifications all affect quality
    5. **Set alerts** — if any metric drops below threshold, pause AI-assisted work on that task type until the issue is diagnosed and resolved
    6. **Share results** — QA transparency builds trust with colleagues, clients, and regulators who question AI use in legal work

    The scorecard is not overhead. It is the evidence that your AI-augmented practice meets professional standards.
