# Chapter 19: Enforcement Actions & Case Law

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this chapter, you will be able to:

    - Describe the CFTC's enforcement authority, penalty powers, and criminal referral process
    - Analyze landmark TradFi enforcement cases and their precedential significance
    - Evaluate the growing body of DeFi-specific CFTC enforcement actions
    - Read and interpret CFTC enforcement orders to extract actionable legal intelligence
    - Apply enforcement pattern analysis to assess regulatory risk for DeFi protocols

!!! warning "Key Terms"
    - **Civil Monetary Penalty (CMP)**: Fines imposed by the CFTC in administrative proceedings or federal court actions — up to $190,132 per violation (adjusted annually for inflation)
    - **Disgorgement**: A remedy requiring the defendant to surrender ill-gotten gains — calculated as the profit derived from the violation
    - **Consent Order**: A settlement agreement where the respondent agrees to findings and remedies without admitting or denying the allegations
    - **Default Judgment**: A judgment entered when the defendant fails to appear or respond — the remedy the CFTC obtained against Ooki DAO
    - **Whistleblower Award**: Payment of 10-30% of monetary sanctions exceeding $1 million to individuals who provide original information leading to successful enforcement

---

This is the most practically important chapter in the book. Theory tells you what the law says. Enforcement tells you what the law **does**.

Every CFTC enforcement order is a data point in a pattern. The violations charged, the penalties imposed, the legal theories advanced, and — critically — the violations that could have been charged but weren't: all of this reveals the CFTC's strategic priorities, resource allocation, and risk tolerance. Reading enforcement orders is not legal archaeology. It's strategic intelligence.

For DeFi operators, enforcement analysis answers the questions that matter most: What behavior triggers CFTC attention? How do they build a case? What penalties do they impose? How do they treat cooperation vs. resistance? And what does the absence of enforcement in certain areas (MEV, vault operations) tell you about the agency's current priorities?

This chapter covers the CFTC's enforcement toolkit (Section 19.1), landmark TradFi cases that established the precedents now being applied to DeFi (Section 19.2), the growing catalog of DeFi-specific enforcement actions (Section 19.3), and a practical guide to reading enforcement orders (Section 19.4). Two case studies — Ooki DAO and Opyn — provide detailed chronological analysis of the most consequential DeFi enforcement actions to date.

The lesson across all of these cases: the CFTC is methodical, patient, and increasingly sophisticated about DeFi. They build precedent incrementally — starting with the clearest violations (unregistered platforms) and expanding outward. If your protocol operates in a space they haven't yet enforced, that's a timeline, not a safe harbor.
