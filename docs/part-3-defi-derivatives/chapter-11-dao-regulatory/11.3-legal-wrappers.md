# 11.3 Legal Wrappers: Foundations, LLCs & Offshore Structures

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Compare the major legal wrapper structures used by DAOs
    - Assess the liability protection each structure provides against CFTC enforcement
    - Evaluate jurisdictional considerations for wrapper selection
    - Identify the limitations of legal wrappers as a regulatory strategy

---

## Why Legal Wrappers Exist

The *Ooki DAO* precedent established that a DAO without a legal wrapper is an unincorporated association whose members face personal liability. Legal wrappers exist to solve this problem: by incorporating the DAO as a formal legal entity, members can interpose a corporate shield between themselves and the DAO's regulatory obligations.

But the protection is not absolute. A legal wrapper gives the DAO legal personality — meaning the entity, not its members, is the regulated "person." However, CFTC enforcement can pierce through to individuals who control or direct the entity's illegal conduct, particularly under the CEA's aiding and abetting provisions (CEA §13(a)) and the controlling person doctrine (CEA §13(b)).

## The Major Wrapper Structures

### Cayman Islands Foundation Company

The most popular structure for large DeFi protocols. A Cayman Foundation Company is a legal entity with no shareholders or members — it exists for a defined purpose (the protocol's governance and development) and is managed by a board of directors (or a supervisor/guardian structure).

**How it works for DAOs:** The foundation holds the protocol's intellectual property, treasury, and governance rights. Token holders participate in governance through the foundation's constitutional documents (memorandum and articles), which can incorporate on-chain voting mechanisms. Directors manage the foundation's legal obligations and regulatory compliance.

**Examples:** dYdX Foundation, Uniswap Foundation, Lido Foundation

**Advantages:** No members to bear personal liability; established Cayman legal infrastructure; tax neutrality; flexibility in governance design.

**Limitations:** Directors face fiduciary duties and personal liability for breaches; the CFTC can serve process on a Cayman entity doing business in the U.S.; if the foundation is deemed to control the protocol, the foundation itself (and its directors) face enforcement.

### Wyoming DAO LLC

Wyoming's DAO LLC statute (W.S. §17-31-101 et seq., effective July 2021) was the first U.S. statute to provide a formal legal structure for DAOs. A Wyoming DAO LLC is a limited liability company whose operating agreement can incorporate smart contract governance.

**How it works:** The DAO registers as an LLC in Wyoming, files articles of organization specifying that it is a DAO LLC, and adopts an operating agreement that may be partly or entirely managed by smart contracts. Members' liability is limited to their capital contribution.

**Advantages:** U.S. legal recognition; member liability limited to investment; clear legal personality for contracting and regulatory purposes; ability to open bank accounts and interface with TradFi.

**Limitations:** Requires a registered agent in Wyoming; all members must be identified (undermining pseudonymity); the operating agreement must address real-world governance (dispute resolution, fiduciary duties); being a U.S. entity makes CFTC jurisdiction automatic.

### Marshall Islands DAO LLC

The Republic of the Marshall Islands enacted a DAO LLC Act (2022) modeled on Wyoming's statute but with offshore advantages. Several DeFi protocols have adopted this structure.

**How it works:** Similar to Wyoming but with Marshall Islands incorporation. The entity is recognized as a legal person under Marshall Islands law and benefits from the jurisdiction's privacy protections and limited regulatory infrastructure.

**Advantages:** Offshore jurisdiction with limited regulatory apparatus; privacy protections; member liability limited; designed specifically for crypto-native entities.

**Limitations:** Untested in U.S. courts; a Marshall Islands entity operating in U.S. markets likely subjects itself to U.S. jurisdiction; enforcement cooperation between Marshall Islands and U.S. is uncertain but growing.

### Swiss Association (Verein)

Swiss law permits the formation of associations (*Verein*) under Articles 60-79 of the Swiss Civil Code. Several DeFi and blockchain projects have used this structure, including Ethereum Foundation.

**How it works:** A *Verein* is a membership-based entity with legal personality separate from its members. Governance is conducted through a general assembly of members. The association can hold assets, enter contracts, and be a party to legal proceedings.

**Advantages:** Established Swiss legal framework; association has separate legal personality; members' personal liability is generally limited to the association's assets; Swiss regulatory environment (FINMA) is relatively crypto-friendly.

**Limitations:** Requires physical presence in Switzerland; Swiss AML/KYC requirements apply; if the association offers derivatives to U.S. persons, CFTC cross-border jurisdiction applies regardless of Swiss incorporation.

## Comparative Analysis

| Feature | Cayman Foundation | Wyoming DAO LLC | Marshall Islands DAO LLC | Swiss Association |
|---------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| **Liability protection** | High (no members) | Medium (limited to investment) | Medium (limited to investment) | Medium-High (separate personality) |
| **CFTC enforceability** | Medium — requires cross-border process | High — U.S. entity, automatic jurisdiction | Low-Medium — offshore, enforcement cooperation uncertain | Medium — cross-border, but Swiss cooperation strong |
| **Pseudonymity** | Partial (directors identified, token holders not) | Low (members must be identified) | Medium (privacy protections) | Medium (officers identified, members less so) |
| **Cost** | $15,000–$50,000 setup + ongoing | $2,000–$10,000 setup + ongoing | $5,000–$15,000 setup + ongoing | $10,000–$30,000 setup + ongoing |
| **Governance flexibility** | High (bespoke constitutional docs) | Medium (must comply with LLC Act) | Medium-High (DAO-specific statute) | Medium (Civil Code constraints) |
| **Track record** | Strong (dYdX, Uniswap, Lido) | Limited (few high-profile adoptions) | Emerging | Established (Ethereum Foundation) |

!!! danger "Critical Assessment: Do Legal Wrappers Actually Protect Against CFTC Enforcement?"
    The honest answer is: **partially, at best.** A legal wrapper protects individual token holders from the *Ooki DAO* unincorporated association theory — but it does not protect the entity itself from CFTC enforcement. If a Cayman Foundation operates a protocol that offers unregistered derivatives to U.S. persons, the CFTC can and will bring enforcement against the Foundation. The Foundation's directors face personal liability under aiding and abetting theories. And if the CFTC can demonstrate that specific individuals (founders, core developers) control the Foundation and direct its illegal conduct, those individuals face personal enforcement regardless of the corporate form.

    Legal wrappers are a **necessary but insufficient** component of a regulatory strategy. They protect passive token holders and create a clear regulatory interface — but they do not make an unregistered derivatives platform legal.

---

## Section Summary

- Legal wrappers interpose a corporate entity between token holders and the DAO's regulatory obligations
- The Cayman Foundation is the most popular structure for large protocols, offering no-member liability and governance flexibility
- Wyoming and Marshall Islands DAO LLCs provide U.S. and offshore alternatives with member liability limitations
- No wrapper structure eliminates CFTC enforcement risk — wrappers protect individual token holders but not the entity or its directors from enforcement for operating unregistered derivatives platforms
