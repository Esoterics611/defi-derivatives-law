# 13.4 Liquidation Mechanics: TradFi Close-Out vs. DeFi Liquidation

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Describe the ISDA close-out process and the Calculation Agent's role
    - Explain DeFi liquidation mechanics and the role of liquidator bots
    - Compare the two approaches on speed, fairness, and systemic risk
    - Analyze liquidation cascades and their regulatory implications

---

## TradFi Close-Out Under ISDA

When an Event of Default occurs under the ISDA Master Agreement (Section 5(a)), the non-defaulting party may designate an **Early Termination Date** (Section 6(a)) and calculate a **Close-out Amount** (Section 6(e)) for each terminated transaction.

The close-out process is deliberate and judgment-intensive:

**Step 1 — Event of Default identified.** The non-defaulting party becomes aware of a default (failure to pay, breach of covenant, bankruptcy filing, cross-default). This may take hours or days to confirm.

**Step 2 — Notice.** The non-defaulting party delivers a notice of default to the defaulting party. Under Section 6(a), Early Termination can be automatic (if elected in the Schedule for bankruptcy events) or at the option of the non-defaulting party.

**Step 3 — Close-out Amount calculation.** The Calculation Agent determines the Close-out Amount for each terminated transaction using "commercially reasonable procedures." This involves obtaining market quotations, analyzing replacement costs, and applying professional judgment. The process typically takes 1-5 business days; for complex portfolios, it can take weeks.

**Step 4 — Net payment.** The individual Close-out Amounts are netted to produce a single net payment amount owed by one party to the other. If the net payment favors the defaulting party, the non-defaulting party must still pay.

## DeFi Liquidation

DeFi liquidation is mechanical and instantaneous:

**Step 1 — Threshold breach detected.** An oracle price update causes a position's LTV ratio to cross the liquidation threshold. Detection is automatic — the smart contract's state makes the position eligible for liquidation the moment the oracle price is written to the blockchain.

**Step 2 — Liquidator execution.** Third-party liquidator bots (MEV searchers, dedicated liquidation services) monitor the blockchain for liquidatable positions. When they detect one, they submit a liquidation transaction, repaying part or all of the borrower's debt and receiving the collateral at a discount (the liquidation bonus, typically 5-15%).

**Step 3 — Settlement.** The liquidation is atomic — collateral transfer, debt repayment, and fee distribution all occur in a single transaction within a single block (~12 seconds on Ethereum). There is no multi-day process, no judgment call, and no possibility of renegotiation.

## Comparison

| Feature | ISDA Close-Out | DeFi Liquidation |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Trigger** | Event of Default (human determination) | LTV threshold breach (oracle price-based, automatic) |
| **Decision-maker** | Non-defaulting party (discretionary) | Smart contract + liquidator bot (automated) |
| **Speed** | Days to weeks | Seconds (single block) |
| **Valuation method** | "Commercially reasonable procedures" — quotations, models, judgment | Oracle price at moment of liquidation — no judgment |
| **Netting** | Full portfolio netting across all transactions | Per-position (no cross-position netting unless protocol specifically implements it) |
| **Forbearance** | Non-defaulting party can choose NOT to terminate, giving defaulting party time to cure | No forbearance — liquidation is automatic and irrevocable |
| **Dispute resolution** | Calculation Agent determination can be challenged | No dispute mechanism — executed transactions are final |
| **Partial termination** | Possible — non-defaulting party can terminate specific transactions | Possible — partial liquidations are common in lending protocols |
| **Pro-cyclicality** | Moderated by human judgment and time delays | Amplified — cascading liquidations occur in real-time |
| **MEV extraction** | N/A | Liquidator bots compete for profit, extracting value from liquidated positions |
| **Cost to defaulting party** | Close-out amount may or may not include a penalty | Liquidation bonus (5-15%) is always paid from borrower's collateral |

## Liquidation Cascades

The most dangerous feature of DeFi liquidation mechanics is the **cascade**: a sequence of liquidations where each liquidation drives down asset prices, triggering further liquidations, in a self-reinforcing spiral.

The mechanics are straightforward:

1. ETH price drops → positions with highest LTV ratios are liquidated
2. Liquidators sell the seized ETH collateral for stablecoins → selling pressure pushes ETH lower
3. The lower ETH price triggers liquidation of positions that were previously above threshold
4. More ETH sold → price drops further → more liquidations
5. The cascade continues until either: (a) ETH price stabilizes, (b) all under-collateralized positions are liquidated, or (c) the protocol's insurance fund is exhausted and losses are socialized

!!! danger "The March 2020 Precedent"
    On March 12, 2020, ETH dropped 43% in 24 hours. MakerDAO experienced a catastrophic liquidation cascade: $8.32 million in positions were liquidated with zero bids in collateral auctions (liquidators bid $0 and won), the DAI peg broke above $1.00 as demand for DAI spiked during liquidations, and Ethereum network congestion made it impossible for additional liquidators to participate. This single event demonstrated every systemic risk that derivatives regulators worry about — and it occurred in a market worth a fraction of traditional derivatives markets.

The CFTC has not yet addressed DeFi liquidation cascades in its regulatory framework. But the functional parallel to TradFi margin calls during market stress (which the CFTC extensively regulates through position limits, margin requirements, and circuit breakers) makes eventual regulatory attention inevitable.

---

## Section Summary

- ISDA close-out is deliberate, judgment-intensive, and takes days to weeks
- DeFi liquidation is automatic, mechanical, and settles in seconds
- TradFi's approach allows forbearance and dispute resolution; DeFi's approach eliminates human discretion
- Liquidation cascades are DeFi's most significant systemic risk, amplified by the speed and automaticity of on-chain execution
