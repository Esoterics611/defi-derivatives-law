# 12.2 The Schedule: Customization & Elections

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Identify the five parts of a standard ISDA Schedule
    - Explain the key elections and their commercial significance
    - Describe the negotiation dynamics between dealer and buy-side counterparties
    - Map Schedule elections to DeFi protocol parameter equivalents

---

## The Schedule as Customization Layer

The ISDA Master Agreement is a pre-printed standard form — the same document for every counterparty relationship. The **Schedule** is where the relationship becomes specific. It is a negotiated document, typically 15-40 pages, that modifies the Master Agreement's default provisions through elections, amendments, and additional terms.

Think of the Master Agreement as a smart contract's immutable core logic and the Schedule as the governance-configurable parameters. The logic is fixed; the parameters vary by relationship.

## The Five Parts

| Part | Content | Key Negotiations |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Part 1: Termination Provisions** | Elections relating to events of default and termination events: cross-default thresholds, specified entities, credit event upon merger provisions | Whether cross-default applies; the dollar threshold that triggers it; which affiliates are captured |
| **Part 2: Tax Representations** | Tax status of each party, withholding tax provisions, tax event definitions | Payer tax representations; gross-up obligations; FATCA compliance |
| **Part 3: Agreement to Deliver Documents** | List of documents each party must provide (legal opinions, annual reports, credit support documents) | Timing and conditions for document delivery |
| **Part 4: Miscellaneous** | Governing law, netting, process agent, calculation agent, set-off, additional representations | Governing law (English vs. New York); calculation agent identity; additional reps |
| **Part 5: Other Provisions** | Bespoke additions: additional events of default, additional termination events, amendments to standard clauses, regulatory protocol adherence | NAV trigger events for funds; key person provisions; regulatory amendments |

## Critical Elections

Several Schedule elections are particularly significant and heavily negotiated:

**Cross-Default (Section 5(a)(vi)).** The Master Agreement allows parties to elect whether a default under another agreement (e.g., a loan facility or another ISDA agreement) triggers an event of default under this Master Agreement. The key negotiation: the **Threshold Amount** — the dollar amount of cross-defaulted obligations below which no event of default is triggered. Dealers typically propose high thresholds ($100M+); buy-side counterparties want lower thresholds.

**Specified Entities.** Parties can extend events of default and termination events to affiliated entities. If Goldman Sachs' London subsidiary defaults on a separate obligation, should that trigger termination of the New York entity's ISDA? The Schedule defines which affiliates are captured.

**Calculation Agent.** Section 6 of the 2002 Master Agreement requires a Calculation Agent to determine Close-out Amounts upon early termination. In dealer-to-client relationships, the dealer is almost always the Calculation Agent — a significant source of potential conflict. In inter-dealer relationships, the parties may designate a neutral third party.

**Governing Law.** Parties elect either English law or New York law. The choice affects interpretation of the agreement, enforcement procedures, and the applicability of netting and insolvency safe harbors. English law is standard for European counterparties; New York law for U.S. counterparties.

## Schedule Elections vs. DeFi Parameters

| Schedule Election | Typical Dealer Position | Typical Buy-Side Position | DeFi Protocol Equivalent |
|-------------------|------------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Cross-Default Threshold** | $100M+ (high) | $1M–$10M (low) | No cross-default concept — each protocol position is independent |
| **Calculation Agent** | Dealer is Calc Agent | Request for neutral third party | Oracle feed + automated calculation (no human agent) |
| **Governing Law** | New York or English law | Follows dealer's preference | "Code is law" (no governing law); legal wrapper jurisdiction if applicable |
| **Netting** | Multi-branch netting elected | Same | Atomic settlement eliminates netting need for individual transactions |
| **Credit Event Upon Merger** | Applies to counterparty | Applies to both parties | No equivalent — smart contracts don't monitor corporate events |
| **Additional Termination Events** | NAV decline triggers for fund counterparties | Resist fund-specific triggers | Liquidation threshold (LTV ratio) serves similar function |

    **Comparing two ISDA Schedules and identifying material differences:**

    *"I have two ISDA Schedules. Schedule A is between [Dealer] and [Fund Client]. Schedule B is between [Dealer] and [Corporate Client]. Compare the following provisions: (1) Cross-Default: is it elected? What are the threshold amounts? (2) Specified Entities: which affiliates are captured? (3) Calculation Agent: who is designated? (4) Additional Termination Events: what triggers exist in each? (5) Governing Law and jurisdiction. Present the comparison as a table with materiality flags (Red = significant difference, Yellow = moderate, Green = standard)."*

    **What to verify:** AI models can parse ISDA Schedules effectively for structural comparisons but frequently miss embedded cross-references — e.g., a Schedule that modifies Section 5(a)(vi) by reference to a separately defined "Specified Indebtedness" threshold. Always verify that the AI has followed all cross-references and has not treated a blank field as "not applicable" when it actually means "default provision applies."

## Section Summary

- The Schedule customizes the Master Agreement through five parts covering termination, tax, documents, miscellaneous, and bespoke provisions
- Critical elections include cross-default thresholds, Calculation Agent, governing law, and additional termination events
- Dealer-side and buy-side negotiate from different positions reflecting their different risk profiles
- DeFi protocols implement many Schedule functions through configurable parameters, but lack concepts like cross-default and credit event monitoring
