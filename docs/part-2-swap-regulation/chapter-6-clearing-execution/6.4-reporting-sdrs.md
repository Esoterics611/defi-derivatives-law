# 6.4 Real-Time Reporting & Swap Data Repositories

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Distinguish between real-time public reporting (Part 43) and regulatory reporting (Part 45)
    - Identify the data fields required for swap reporting
    - Explain the role of Swap Data Repositories (SDRs) in the regulatory infrastructure
    - Compare CFTC reporting requirements to DeFi's on-chain transparency

---

## Two Reporting Regimes

Dodd-Frank created two distinct but related reporting obligations for swaps, each serving a different purpose:

**Part 43 — Real-Time Public Reporting:** Trade-level data published to the market with time delays and caps on notional amounts. The purpose is **price transparency** — giving market participants access to transaction data so they can assess market conditions and price their own trades. Think of it as the swap market's equivalent of a stock ticker, but delayed and anonymized.

**Part 45 — Regulatory Reporting to SDRs:** Comprehensive position and transaction data reported to registered Swap Data Repositories and available to the CFTC. The purpose is **regulatory surveillance** — giving the CFTC the data it needs to monitor systemic risk, detect manipulation, and oversee market participants. This data is not public.

| Feature | Part 43 (Public) | Part 45 (Regulatory) |
|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Audience** | Market participants, public | CFTC, prudential regulators |
| **Data scope** | Price, notional (capped), tenor, asset class | Full transaction details, counterparty identity, lifecycle events |
| **Timing** | "As soon as technologically practicable" (typically within 15 minutes for on-facility trades) | By end of next business day (T+1) |
| **Anonymity** | Counterparty identities not disclosed | Full LEI identification of both counterparties |
| **Notional caps** | Large trades reported with capped notional to prevent information leakage | Actual notional reported |

## Swap Data Repositories (SDRs)

SDRs are registered entities under CEA §21 that collect, maintain, and make available swap transaction data. They are the data infrastructure of the post-Dodd-Frank swap market. Currently registered SDRs include DTCC Data Repository, ICE Trade Vault, CME Swap Data Repository, and Bloomberg SDR.

SDRs must comply with their own set of requirements including data standards, system safeguards, governance, and access provisions for the CFTC and other regulators. The CFTC's 2020 amendments to Part 45 (the "Rewrite") significantly overhauled reporting requirements, harmonizing data fields and establishing the **Unique Product Identifier (UPI)** system alongside the existing **Unique Swap Identifier (USI)** and **Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)** requirements.

**USI (Unique Swap Identifier):** A unique alphanumeric code assigned to each swap transaction at execution. The reporting counterparty is responsible for generating and transmitting the USI.

**UPI (Unique Product Identifier):** A standardized code identifying the product type. Managed by the Derivatives Service Bureau (DSB) under an ISO standard. Rolled out beginning January 2024.

**LEI (Legal Entity Identifier):** A 20-character code uniquely identifying each legal entity that is a counterparty to a swap. Administered by the Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation (GLEIF). Both counterparties must have LEIs.

## The DeFi Transparency Paradox

DeFi operates with a paradox that the CFTC has not yet fully confronted: **on-chain derivatives markets are simultaneously more transparent and less compliant than traditional markets.**

More transparent because every transaction is recorded on a public, immutable blockchain. Anyone can query the transaction history of any protocol, any address, any position. The data is richer, more granular, and more accessible than anything the CFTC receives through SDRs.

Less compliant because this transparency is in the wrong format, using the wrong identifiers, and available through the wrong channels. The CFTC's reporting infrastructure requires specific data fields (USI, UPI, LEI), specific formats (FpML, FIXML), specific timing requirements, and specific registered intermediaries (SDRs). On-chain transaction data satisfies none of these requirements.

| CFTC Requirement | DeFi Equivalent | Compliance Gap |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| USI per transaction | Transaction hash | Different identifier system; no USI generation |
| UPI per product | No standardized product codes | Products identified by contract address, not UPI |
| LEI per counterparty | Wallet address (pseudonymous) | No identity linkage; pseudonymous by design |
| Report to registered SDR | Data on public blockchain | No SDR submission; data available but not in required format |
| Part 43 public dissemination with caps | All data public, no caps | Over-transparency: no notional caps, no delays, full counterparty exposure visible |
| Part 45 lifecycle reporting | On-chain state changes | Events recorded but not in regulatory format |

!!! warning "The Over-Transparency Problem"
    DeFi's radical transparency creates a problem that TradFi doesn't have: **information leakage**. Part 43's notional caps exist specifically to prevent large trades from moving markets against the trader before execution is complete. In DeFi, pending transactions are visible in the mempool before execution, and position sizes are visible on-chain at all times. This creates MEV extraction opportunities and enables front-running that Part 43 was designed to prevent. DeFi is simultaneously non-compliant with reporting requirements and suffering from the exact harms those requirements address.

---

## Section Summary

- Part 43 provides real-time public transparency; Part 45 provides comprehensive regulatory data to the CFTC
- SDRs are the registered data infrastructure, receiving and maintaining swap transaction records
- USI, UPI, and LEI form the identifier framework for the swap reporting system
- DeFi's on-chain transparency provides more data than TradFi reporting but in incompatible formats
- The over-transparency problem means DeFi suffers from information leakage that TradFi reporting rules are designed to prevent
