# 20.4 AI-Assisted Compliance Drafting

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Use AI to draft compliance policies, risk disclosures, and regulatory filings
    - Apply the "first draft" workflow: AI generates 80%, human refines 20%
    - Identify which compliance documents AI drafts well and which require heavy human editing
    - Assess quality and liability risks of AI-drafted regulatory filings

---

## The First Draft Workflow

The most productive AI workflow for compliance drafting is the **80/20 model**: AI generates a structurally complete first draft (roughly 80% of the final product), and a human expert refines, corrects, and customizes it (the remaining 20%). This is dramatically faster than drafting from scratch — the human works from a structured document rather than a blank page.

The key insight: AI is excellent at structure, comprehensiveness, and regulatory citation. It is weak at firm-specific customization, nuanced judgment calls, and ensuring accuracy of cited rules. The human's 20% is disproportionately important — it's the difference between a generic template and a usable compliance document.

## Document Types and AI Effectiveness

| Document Type | CFTC Requirement | AI Draft Quality | Human Editing Intensity | Time Savings |
|-------------- |-----------------|-----------------|------------------------|-------------|
| **Compliance policies & procedures manual** | Rule 23.600 (SD/MSP risk management) | High — AI produces comprehensive, well-structured policy documents | Medium — firm-specific procedures, approval workflows, organizational details | 60-70% |
| **CCO annual report** | Rule 3.3(e) | Medium — AI structures the report well but lacks firm-specific compliance data | High — requires real assessment data, incident reports, testing results | 40-50% |
| **Risk disclosure document** | Various (Rule 23.431 for SDs) | High — AI produces thorough risk disclosures covering standard categories | Medium — protocol-specific risks, custom language, legal review | 60-70% |
| **Trade reporting procedures** | Parts 43, 45 (real-time and regulatory reporting) | Medium — AI understands the reporting framework but technical implementation details require expertise | High — data field mapping, system specifications, exception handling | 30-40% |
| **Business conduct documentation** | Rules 23.400-23.451 | High — disclosure and fair dealing standards are well-documented and AI reproduces them accurately | Medium — counterparty-specific disclosures, scenario-based procedures | 50-60% |
| **Registration application** | NFA Form 7-R, CFTC Form 7-W | Low-Medium — forms have specific requirements that AI may not capture precisely | High — entity-specific information, principal details, regulatory history | 20-30% |

## Workflow: Drafting a Compliance Manual

**Step 1: Provide context and scope.**
```
Draft a derivatives compliance manual for a [DeFi perpetual futures protocol / swap dealer / FCM] that covers:
1. Organizational structure and compliance reporting lines
2. Trading policies (authorized products, position limits, approved counterparties)
3. Risk management program per CFTC Rule 23.600
4. Business conduct standards per CFTC Rules 23.400-23.451
5. Trade surveillance procedures per CFTC Rule 23.602
6. Recordkeeping requirements per CFTC Rules 23.201-23.203
7. Escalation and remediation procedures
8. Annual review and testing protocols

For each section: cite the specific CFTC rule, state the requirement, and provide an implementation procedure. Include placeholder brackets [FIRM-SPECIFIC] where entity-specific information is needed.
```

**Step 2: AI generates the first draft.** The output will be a 15-25 page compliance manual with all required sections, rule citations, and procedural templates. The structure will be comprehensive.

**Step 3: Human refinement.** The compliance officer or legal counsel:
- Fills in `[FIRM-SPECIFIC]` placeholders with actual organizational details
- Reviews and corrects rule citations (AI may cite superseded rules)
- Adjusts procedures to match the firm's actual operations and systems
- Adds firm-specific risk assessments and custom procedures
- Removes any AI-generated content that doesn't apply to the entity type

**Step 4: Legal review.** Counsel reviews the complete document for legal accuracy, ensuring that the policies actually satisfy the regulatory requirements they claim to address.

## Drafting Risk Disclosures for DeFi Protocols

Risk disclosures are particularly well-suited to AI drafting because they follow standard categories:

```
Draft a risk disclosure document for [protocol name], a DeFi [perpetual futures / options / lending] protocol. Cover these risk categories:
1. Smart contract risk (bugs, exploits, upgrade risk)
2. Oracle risk (manipulation, staleness, failure)
3. Liquidation risk (cascade liquidations, MEV, slippage)
4. Regulatory risk (CFTC enforcement, SEC classification, cross-border)
5. Market risk (volatility, liquidity, basis risk)
6. Counterparty/protocol risk (governance attacks, admin key compromise)
7. Systemic risk (composability failures, stablecoin depeg, chain congestion)

For each category: describe the risk in plain language, explain the worst-case scenario, state what protections (if any) the protocol provides, and include a clear warning that users may lose their entire investment. Use the same tone and specificity as a registered broker-dealer's risk disclosure.
```

!!! danger ""
    **AI Limitation Warning:** AI-drafted compliance documents MUST be reviewed by qualified counsel before filing or adoption. Regulatory filings with errors create liability — an inaccurate CCO annual report can trigger personal liability under Rule 3.3(e). An inadequate risk disclosure can be evidence of fraud if losses occur. AI produces excellent first drafts, but the legal responsibility for the final document rests entirely with the human who signs it.

## Section Summary

- The 80/20 workflow (AI drafts, human refines) reduces compliance document drafting time by 40-70%
- AI excels at structure, comprehensiveness, and regulatory citation; humans add firm-specific context, judgment, and accuracy verification
- Compliance manuals and risk disclosures are the highest-value AI drafting targets
- Registration applications and CCO annual reports require heavy human editing due to firm-specific data requirements
- All AI-drafted compliance documents must be reviewed by counsel — the signer bears full legal responsibility
