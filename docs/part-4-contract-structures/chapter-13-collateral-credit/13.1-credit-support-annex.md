# 13.1 The Credit Support Annex (CSA)

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain the role of the CSA as the fourth pillar of ISDA documentation
    - Distinguish between English law and New York law CSA variants
    - Identify the key commercial terms in a CSA
    - Map CSA terms to DeFi collateral parameters

---

## The CSA's Role

The Credit Support Annex is the ISDA document that governs how counterparties **post and return collateral** to manage the credit exposure created by their derivatives positions. While the Master Agreement defines the relationship and the Confirmation defines the trade, the CSA ensures that neither party accumulates excessive uncollateralized exposure to the other.

The CSA operates alongside the Master Agreement (it is technically an annex, not a separate agreement) and its terms are subject to the single agreement concept. Three variants are in common use:

**English Law CSA (1995/2016):** Collateral is transferred by **title transfer** — the posting party transfers legal ownership of the collateral to the receiving party. The receiving party can rehypothecate (reuse) the collateral. This is the most common variant globally.

**New York Law CSA (1994):** Collateral is posted as a **security interest** — the posting party retains ownership, granting the receiving party a pledge. Rehypothecation is permitted only if specifically elected. More common in U.S. bilateral transactions.

**2016 Credit Support Annex for Variation Margin (VM CSA):** Introduced specifically to comply with the Uncleared Margin Rules, this variant requires daily VM exchange with title transfer and restricts the types of eligible collateral. It is now the standard for VM between covered entities.

## Key CSA Terms

| CSA Term | Definition | Typical Dealer Value | Typical Buy-Side Value | DeFi Equivalent |
|----------|-----------|---------------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Threshold** | The amount of uncollateralized exposure each party tolerates before collateral must be posted | $0 (post-UMR) to $50M (legacy) | $0 to $10M | Collateral ratio (e.g., 150% LTV means effectively zero threshold) |
| **Minimum Transfer Amount (MTA)** | The minimum amount of collateral that must be transferred in a single delivery | $250K–$1M | $100K–$500K | Minimum deposit amount (if any); most protocols have none |
| **Independent Amount (IA)** | Additional collateral required above mark-to-market exposure, similar to initial margin | Varies by counterparty credit quality | Resisted by buy-side | Over-collateralization requirement (the amount above 100% LTV) |
| **Eligible Collateral** | Types of assets accepted as collateral | Cash (USD, EUR, GBP), sovereign bonds (G7), agency MBS | Same, but negotiates for broader list | ETH, USDC, USDT, stETH, wBTC (protocol-specific) |
| **Valuation Percentage (Haircut)** | The discount applied to non-cash collateral | 0% cash; 2-5% sovereign bonds; 5-15% corporate bonds | Same | Protocol-specific haircuts on volatile collateral |
| **Valuation Date** | When collateral positions are marked to market | Each business day | Same | Every block (continuous) |
| **Notification Time** | Deadline for margin calls | 1:00 PM local time | Same | Instant (no notification; automatic liquidation) |
| **Return Timing** | When excess collateral must be returned | Next business day after demand | Same | Instant withdrawal (subject to protocol constraints) |

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note"
    **Analyzing a CSA and flagging non-standard provisions:**

    *"Review the following Credit Support Annex and identify: (1) Threshold amounts for each party (and whether they differ — asymmetric thresholds favor the party with the higher threshold). (2) Eligible collateral and haircuts — flag any collateral types that deviate from G7 sovereign bonds and cash. (3) Independent Amount — is IA required, and if so, is it a fixed amount or a percentage? (4) Rehypothecation rights — can the receiving party reuse posted collateral? (5) Dispute resolution for collateral valuation disagreements. Flag any provisions that deviate from market standard with a materiality assessment."*

    **What to verify:** AI models handle the structured fields (Threshold, MTA, eligible collateral) well but often miss dispute resolution provisions buried in the "Other Provisions" section. Also verify that the AI correctly identifies whether the CSA is English law (title transfer) or New York law (security interest) — this distinction affects rehypothecation rights and insolvency treatment.

## Section Summary

- The CSA governs bilateral collateral posting, operating as the fourth pillar of ISDA documentation
- English law CSA uses title transfer; New York law CSA uses security interest
- Key terms include Threshold, MTA, Independent Amount, Eligible Collateral, and Valuation Percentage
- DeFi collateral systems implement most CSA functions but with continuous valuation and automatic enforcement
