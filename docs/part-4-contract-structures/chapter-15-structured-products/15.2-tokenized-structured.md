# 15.2 Tokenized Structured Products

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Identify key tokenized structured product protocols and their underlying instruments
    - Analyze the dual registration problem (SEC + CFTC) for tokenized products
    - Evaluate the securities and derivatives classification of specific tokenized instruments
    - Assess regulatory gaps in the current framework for tokenized structured products

---

## The Tokenization Wave

Tokenization — representing traditional financial instruments as blockchain tokens — has moved from concept to reality. A growing number of protocols now offer on-chain versions of TradFi structured products, giving DeFi users access to yields and exposures previously confined to institutional markets.

The appeal is clear: 24/7 trading, fractional ownership, instant settlement, global access, and transparency of holdings. The legal problem is equally clear: tokenizing a security doesn't make it stop being a security, and embedding a derivative in a token doesn't make it stop being a derivative.

## Key Protocols and Products

**Ondo Finance** offers tokenized U.S. Treasury exposure through products like OUSG (tokenized short-term Treasuries) and USDY (a yield-bearing stablecoin backed by Treasuries and bank deposits). These are relatively straightforward — the underlying is a fixed-income instrument, and the token represents a share of a fund holding that instrument. The derivatives component is minimal, but the securities classification is clear.

**Backed Finance** issues tokens backed by real-world financial instruments — including structured products. Their tokens track the performance of the underlying instrument, with the token itself being a bearer instrument on-chain. When the underlying is a structured note (bond + embedded derivative), the token inherits the dual regulatory classification.

**Centrifuge** tokenizes real-world credit assets (invoices, real estate loans, revenue-based financing) and structures them into senior and junior tranches. The tranching mechanism creates different risk/return profiles from the same underlying pool — this is, structurally, a CDO. The junior tranche absorbs first losses, the senior tranche has priority. Investors choose their tranche based on risk appetite.

**Matrixdock and OpenEden** offer tokenized T-bill products competing with Ondo, while **Mountain Protocol** offers USDM, a yield-bearing stablecoin backed by U.S. Treasuries. Each of these sits at the intersection of stablecoin regulation, securities law, and (depending on structure) derivatives regulation.

## The Dual Registration Problem

The core regulatory challenge for tokenized structured products is that they may simultaneously be:

1. **Securities** under the Securities Act of 1933 — requiring SEC registration or an exemption (Reg D, Reg S, Reg A+)
2. **Derivatives** under the Commodity Exchange Act — requiring CFTC compliance if the embedded component is a swap, future, or option on a commodity

| Tokenized Product | Underlying | Securities Classification | Derivatives Classification | Regulatory Gap |
|-------------------|-----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|----------------|
| Tokenized Treasury fund (OUSG) | U.S. Treasury bonds | **Security** — investment contract (Howey test) | Minimal — no embedded derivative | SEC registration; most use Reg D exemption for accredited investors |
| Tokenized structured note | Bond + embedded option | **Security** — debt instrument with derivative payoff | **Derivative** — embedded option may be a "swap" if OTC | Dual SEC + CFTC jurisdiction; no clear joint registration path |
| Tokenized CDO tranche (Centrifuge-style) | Pool of credit assets, tranched | **Security** — investment contract with pooled returns | **Possible derivative** — senior/junior waterfall creates synthetic credit exposure | SEC registration + potential CFTC scrutiny of credit tranching |
| Yield-bearing stablecoin (USDM, USDY) | Treasury-backed with yield pass-through | **Likely security** — investment of money with expectation of profit from others' efforts | Minimal if purely Treasury-backed | Stablecoin legislation may create a new category; currently securities law applies |

## Why Dual Regulation Matters

In TradFi, a structured note issued by Goldman Sachs is registered as a security with the SEC. If it contains a commodity-based derivative (e.g., principal linked to oil prices), the embedded derivative may also fall under CFTC jurisdiction. Goldman navigates this through its existing registrations as a broker-dealer (SEC) and swap dealer (CFTC), plus decades of established regulatory guidance.

DeFi protocols have none of this infrastructure. A protocol that tokenizes a structured product is potentially:
- An unregistered **securities issuer** (Securities Act violation)
- An unregistered **broker-dealer** (Exchange Act violation)
- An unregistered **swap dealer** or operating an unregistered **swap execution facility** (CEA violation)
- An unregistered **investment company** (Investment Company Act violation if pooled)

Each violation carries its own enforcement regime, and the CFTC and SEC have demonstrated willingness to bring parallel actions.

!!! warning ""
    **Practical Reality:** Most tokenized structured product protocols currently operate under Reg D exemptions (accredited investors only) or Reg S exemptions (non-U.S. persons only). This limits distribution but doesn't resolve the derivatives classification problem — Reg D/Reg S are securities exemptions, not CFTC exemptions. A product that is both a security and a derivative needs compliance under both regimes.


---

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note: Dual Classification Analysis"
    When evaluating a tokenized product's regulatory status, prompt your AI model with:

    *"Analyze [token/protocol] under both securities law and derivatives law. First, apply the Howey test to determine if this is an investment contract. Second, identify any embedded derivative component (option, swap, forward, futures-like element). Third, classify the derivative under CEA §1a(47) (swap definition) or CEA §1a(31) (future). Fourth, determine which exemptions might apply under each regime. Present the analysis as a dual-track regulatory assessment."*

    **What to Verify:** AI models tend to focus on the securities analysis (Howey test) and underweight the derivatives analysis. Always ask separately about the derivatives classification — the CFTC has independent jurisdiction over commodity-based derivatives regardless of the SEC's securities classification.


---

## Section Summary

- Tokenized structured products represent TradFi instruments on-chain, inheriting their full regulatory classification
- The dual registration problem means tokenized structured products may require compliance under both SEC and CFTC regimes
- Most protocols use Reg D/Reg S exemptions for the securities component but lack any CFTC compliance framework
- The absence of clear joint SEC/CFTC guidance for tokenized structured products creates legal risk for issuers and investors
