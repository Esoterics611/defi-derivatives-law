# 9.2 Synthetic Assets & Mirror Protocols

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Define synthetic assets and explain their mechanics
    - Analyze why the absence of "actual delivery" makes synthetics unambiguously derivatives
    - Classify specific synthetic asset protocols by regulatory risk
    - Assess the regulatory implications of synthetic asset depegging

---

## What Are Synthetic Assets?

A synthetic asset is a token designed to track the price of another asset — a stock, a commodity, a fiat currency, an index — **without holding or delivering the underlying asset**. The holder of a synthetic token has economic exposure to the underlying's price movement without owning the underlying itself.

The mechanics vary by protocol, but the core structure is consistent: a user deposits collateral (typically crypto), and the protocol mints a synthetic token whose value tracks an external reference price via an oracle. The collateral over-collateralizes the synthetic position to ensure solvency. If the collateral ratio falls below a threshold, the position is liquidated.

**Synthetix (now SNX):** The pioneer synthetic asset protocol. Users stake SNX tokens as collateral and mint synthetic assets (sUSD, sBTC, sETH, and historically synthetic stocks and commodities). All synth holders share a pooled debt model — when one synth increases in value, the collective debt increases for all stakers. The protocol transitioned away from individual synthetic stock tracking after regulatory pressure.

**Mirror Protocol (defunct):** Built on Terra, Mirror allowed users to mint and trade "Mirrored Assets" (mAAPL, mTSLA, mGOOG) — synthetic tokens tracking U.S. stock prices. Mirror collapsed with the Terra ecosystem in May 2022. Before its collapse, it represented the most aggressive regulatory boundary push: synthetic versions of SEC-regulated securities, accessible globally without brokerage accounts.

**UMA (Universal Market Access):** A protocol framework for creating custom synthetic assets using an optimistic oracle. UMA allows anyone to create a synthetic tracking any measurable value — a temperature, an election outcome, a gas price — as long as they can define a price identifier and post collateral.

## The "Actual Delivery" Analysis

The CEA's forward contract exclusion exempts from regulation contracts that contemplate "actual delivery" of the underlying commodity. This exclusion is critical for spot crypto transactions — when you buy BTC on an exchange and receive the BTC, that is actual delivery, not a derivative.

Synthetic assets **by definition do not involve actual delivery**. The entire point is that you get price exposure without receiving the underlying. A synthetic Tesla stock does not deliver Tesla shares. A synthetic gold token does not deliver gold. A synthetic BTC does not deliver BTC. This means synthetic assets cannot claim the forward contract exclusion and are unambiguously within the CEA's definition of swap:

- They are "agreements providing for payments based on the value of a commodity" — §1a(47)(A)(iii)
- They do not involve delivery of the underlying — no forward contract exclusion
- If the underlying is a commodity (BTC, ETH, gold, oil): CFTC jurisdiction as a swap
- If the underlying is a security (AAPL, TSLA): SEC jurisdiction as a security-based swap
- If the underlying is a broad index: CFTC jurisdiction

| Synthetic Protocol | Mechanism | Underlying Assets | Regulatory Risk Assessment |
|-------------------|-----------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **Synthetix** | Pooled debt model, SNX collateral, Chainlink oracles | Crypto, FX, commodities | **High** — synthetic commodities are swaps under CFTC jurisdiction |
| **Mirror Protocol** (defunct) | Individual CDP minting, Terra collateral, Band Protocol oracles | U.S. stocks, crypto | **Extreme** — synthetic stocks are security-based swaps under SEC jurisdiction |
| **UMA** | Custom synthetic creation, optimistic oracle, over-collateralized positions | Anything with a definable price | **Variable** — depends on the underlying; framework enables creation of any derivative |
| **Ethena (USDe)** | Delta-neutral synthetic dollar using staked ETH + short perp positions | USD peg via basis trade | **Moderate** — the underlying strategy involves derivatives (short perps), creating layered regulatory exposure |

!!! danger "Synthetic Stocks: The Maximum Risk Zone"
    Mirror Protocol's synthetic stocks represented the highest possible regulatory risk: synthetic versions of SEC-regulated securities, created without any securities registration, sold to anyone globally without brokerage licensing, suitability requirements, or investor protection disclosures. Mirror collapsed before enforcement caught up, but the SEC's charges against Terraform Labs (the parent entity) cited Mirror's synthetic stocks as evidence of unregistered securities activity. Any protocol that launches synthetic versions of regulated securities is operating in the maximum risk zone for both CFTC and SEC enforcement.

---

## Section Summary

- Synthetic assets track underlying prices without actual delivery — making them unambiguously derivatives
- The absence of actual delivery eliminates the forward contract exclusion, confirming swap/security-based swap classification
- Regulatory jurisdiction depends on the underlying: commodities → CFTC, securities → SEC, broad indices → CFTC
- Synthetic stocks are the maximum regulatory risk product — triggering both CFTC and SEC jurisdiction
- The synthetic asset sector has contracted under regulatory pressure, with Mirror defunct and Synthetix narrowing its product set
