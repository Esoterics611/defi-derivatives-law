# Chapter 2: Core Derivative Instruments

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
    - Explain the mechanics, regulation, and economic purpose of futures, options, forwards, and swaps
    - Distinguish between exchange-traded and OTC derivative instruments
    - Map each traditional derivative instrument to its DeFi equivalent
    - Understand the "actual delivery" exemption and its critical importance for DeFi
    - Identify when a DeFi protocol is creating a regulated derivative instrument


!!! warning "Key Terms"
    - **Futures Contract**: A standardized agreement to buy or sell a commodity at a predetermined price on a specific future date, traded on a regulated exchange
    - **Option**: A contract giving the holder the right (but not the obligation) to buy or sell an asset at a specified price before or on a specified date
    - **Forward Contract**: A privately negotiated agreement to buy or sell an asset at a specified price on a future date — the customizable, OTC cousin of a future
    - **Swap**: An agreement between two parties to exchange cash flows over time according to a predetermined formula
    - **Mark-to-Market**: The daily revaluation of a derivatives position based on current market prices, with gains/losses settled daily
    - **Notional Amount**: The reference amount used to calculate derivative payments — not the actual amount exchanged
    - **The Greeks**: Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega, Rho — measures of option price sensitivity to various factors


---

Before you can analyze whether a DeFi protocol creates a regulated derivative, you need to understand what derivatives actually are — not as abstract financial concepts, but as specific instruments with defined mechanics, regulatory treatment, and economic functions. This chapter breaks down the four pillars of the derivatives world: futures, options, forwards, and swaps.

Each instrument has a direct DeFi equivalent. By the end of this chapter, you'll see that DeFi didn't invent new financial instruments — it reimplemented existing ones on new infrastructure. And existing regulation is reaching for them.
