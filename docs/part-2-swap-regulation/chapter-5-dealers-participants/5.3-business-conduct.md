# 5.3 Business Conduct Standards (External & Internal)

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:
    - Distinguish between external business conduct standards (counterparty-facing) and internal standards (organizational)
    - Explain the heightened "Special Entity" protections and who qualifies
    - Identify which business conduct requirements are structurally impossible to satisfy in permissionless DeFi


---

## External Business Conduct Standards

CFTC Part 23, Subpart H (§§23.400–23.451) governs how swap dealers interact with counterparties. These rules establish a **relationship-based regulatory framework** — the dealer owes specific obligations to each counterparty depending on the counterparty's sophistication and status.

### Core Obligations

| Requirement | CFTC Rule | What It Requires |
|------------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Verification of counterparty eligibility** | §23.430 | SD must verify the counterparty is an Eligible Contract Participant (ECP) before entering a swap |
| **Disclosure of material risks** | §23.431 | SD must disclose material risks, characteristics, and incentives of each swap |
| **Disclosure of material information** | §23.431(a)(3) | SD must disclose any material conflicts of interest |
| **Communication in fair and balanced manner** | §23.433 | Communications must not be misleading |
| **Institutional suitability** | §23.434 | SD must have a reasonable basis for recommending a swap (unless counterparty is using an independent representative) |
| **Daily marks** | §23.431(d) | SD must provide daily mid-market marks to counterparty upon request |

!!! danger "The Eligible Contract Participant (ECP) Requirement"
    This is the single most consequential business conduct requirement for DeFi. Under CEA §2(e), it is **unlawful** to enter a swap with a person who is not an ECP unless the swap is traded on a DCM. An ECP is generally an entity or individual with:
    - Total assets exceeding $10 million, or
    - Net worth exceeding $1 million if the swap is entered to manage risk, or
    - A regulated financial institution

    Retail individuals with less than $1 million net worth are **not ECPs**. A DeFi protocol that allows any wallet to enter derivatives positions — without verifying ECP status — is facilitating illegal transactions under §2(e) if any counterparty fails the ECP test. This was one of the violations in the Ooki DAO enforcement action.


### Special Entity Protections

"Special Entities" receive heightened protections. A swap dealer dealing with a Special Entity must:
- Act as a **fiduciary** or use an independent representative for the Special Entity
- Not recommend swaps that are not in the Special Entity's best interests
- Satisfy more rigorous suitability and fair pricing obligations

| Special Entity Category | Examples |
|------------------------|---------|
| Federal agencies | Government departments, agencies |
| State and local governments | Municipal pension funds, state investment pools |
| Employee benefit plans | ERISA-governed pension funds |
| Governmental plans | State and local government employee retirement systems |
| Endowments | University endowments, foundation investment accounts |

## Internal Business Conduct Standards

CFTC Part 23, Subpart J (§§23.600–23.609) requires swap dealers to establish and maintain internal organizational standards:

| Requirement | CFTC Rule | Substance |
|------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Risk management program** | §23.600 | Written policies for market, credit, liquidity, operational, and legal risk |
| **Chief Compliance Officer** | §23.603 | Designated CCO with independence, authority, and annual reporting obligation |
| **Conflicts of interest** | §23.605 | Written policies for identifying and managing conflicts |
| **Supervision** | §23.602 | Diligent supervision of all swap activities |
| **Business continuity** | §23.603 | Disaster recovery and continuity planning |

## The DeFi Gap Analysis

=== "Requirement"
    **Counterparty Eligibility Verification (ECP)**
=== "TradFi Compliance"
    Dealer verifies counterparty identity and ECP status via KYC documentation before onboarding
=== "DeFi Reality"
    Permissionless protocols accept any wallet — no identity verification, no ECP check. **Structurally impossible** to comply without KYC gating.


=== "Requirement"
    **Pre-Trade Risk Disclosure**
=== "TradFi Compliance"
    Dealer provides written disclosure of material risks, scenario analyses, and worst-case outcomes before trade execution
=== "DeFi Reality"
    Terms of service and documentation may exist, but no mechanism to verify counterparty received and understood disclosures before transacting


=== "Requirement"
    **Daily Mid-Market Marks**
=== "TradFi Compliance"
    Dealer provides mark-to-market valuations to counterparty on each business day
=== "DeFi Reality"
    On-chain positions are **continuously** marked to market via oracle prices — arguably exceeding the TradFi requirement. This is one area where DeFi surpasses traditional compliance.


=== "Requirement"
    **Chief Compliance Officer**
=== "TradFi Compliance"
    Named individual with independence, authority, and personal liability for annual compliance report
=== "DeFi Reality"
    No identified individual in many DAOs. DAO governance votes are not equivalent to CCO oversight. This requirement is **incompatible** with fully decentralized governance.


---

## Section Summary

- External business conduct standards impose counterparty-facing obligations: eligibility verification, risk disclosure, suitability, and daily marks
- The ECP requirement makes it illegal to enter swaps with retail individuals — a direct challenge to permissionless DeFi
- Special Entity protections create fiduciary duties toward pension funds, municipalities, and endowments
- Internal standards require risk management programs, a CCO, and conflict management
- Several requirements are structurally incompatible with permissionless, decentralized protocols
