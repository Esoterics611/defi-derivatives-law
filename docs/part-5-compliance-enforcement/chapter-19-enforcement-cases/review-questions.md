# Chapter 19: Review Questions

---

**1.** Distinguish between CFTC administrative proceedings and civil actions in federal court. When does the CFTC choose each pathway? Why have all DeFi enforcement actions been federal court cases rather than administrative proceedings?

**2.** The CFTC's whistleblower program awards 10-30% of sanctions exceeding $1 million. Analyze the implications for a DeFi protocol operating in violation of the CEA. Who might be potential whistleblowers? How does pseudonymity affect the whistleblower calculus?

**3.** Compare the LIBOR manipulation scandal to DeFi oracle manipulation. Identify three structural parallels and two key differences. Why does the CFTC's multi-billion dollar LIBOR enforcement program suggest aggressive pursuit of DeFi oracle manipulation?

**4.** The MF Global case involved misuse of customer segregated funds. Identify the DeFi protocol behavior that most closely parallels MF Global's violation. What regulatory protections exist in TradFi to prevent this? What equivalent protections, if any, exist in DeFi?

**5.** Analyze the CFTC's DeFi enforcement pattern across all six cases (bZeroX, Ooki DAO, Polymarket, Opyn, Deridex, ZeroEx). What violation type does the CFTC target most frequently? What does the penalty range ($100K-$1.4M) reveal about the CFTC's approach? How does cooperation affect outcomes?

**6.** The CFTC served Ooki DAO through the Help Chat Box on its website and a post in its governance forum. Evaluate this novel service method: was it legally sound? What precedent does it set for serving other DAOs? What if the DAO had been governed through a different mechanism (e.g., multisig rather than token voting)?

**7.** A DeFi protocol creates "protection tokens" that pay out if ETH drops below $2,000 before December 31. The protocol markets these as "DeFi insurance," not options. Using CEA §4c(b) and the substance-over-labels reasoning of the Opyn order, analyze whether these protection tokens are commodity options. Does the marketing language ("insurance" vs. "option") affect the CFTC's analysis?

**8.** You receive a CFTC enforcement order to analyze. It charges a DeFi protocol with CEA §4(a) (unregistered DCM) but does NOT charge CEA §4d (unregistered FCM), even though the protocol holds user collateral. What does this omission suggest about the CFTC's enforcement strategy? Why might the CFTC choose not to charge every available violation?

**9.** A DeFi perpetual futures protocol receives a CFTC subpoena (Civil Investigative Demand). The protocol's legal counsel recommends full cooperation. Draft a cooperation strategy that maximizes the protocol's chance of receiving favorable settlement terms. Reference the CFTC's 2017 Advisory on Cooperation and the outcomes in the Opyn and Deridex cases.

**10.** Based on the enforcement pattern analysis in Section 19.3, predict the CFTC's next three DeFi enforcement targets. For each prediction, identify: (a) the protocol type, (b) the CEA violation, (c) the legal precedent the CFTC would establish, and (d) the estimated penalty range. Justify each prediction based on the pattern of prior enforcement.
