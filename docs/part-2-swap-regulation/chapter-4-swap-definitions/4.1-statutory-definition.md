# 4.1 The Statutory Definition of "Swap"

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:
    - Recite the core elements of the CEA §1a(47) swap definition
    - Identify what is included in and excluded from the definition
    - Explain how the CFTC/SEC joint "Further Definition" rulemaking expanded and clarified the statute
    - Analyze why the definition captures most DeFi derivatives by default


---

## The Statutory Text

The Commodity Exchange Act §1a(47) defines "swap" with deliberate breadth. The definition includes (in condensed form):

> Any agreement, contract, or transaction that —
> (i) is a put, call, cap, floor, collar, or similar option of any kind;
> (ii) provides for any purchase, sale, payment, or delivery that is dependent on the occurrence, nonoccurrence, or the extent of an occurrence of an event associated with a potential financial, economic, or commercial consequence;
> (iii) provides on an executory basis for the exchange of one or more payments based on the value or level of one or more rates, commodities, instruments, quantitative measures, or other financial or economic interests

And the catch-all:

> (iv) is, or in the future becomes, commonly known to the trade as a "swap"

That last clause is extraordinary. It is a **forward-looking definition** — capturing not just instruments that exist today, but instruments that the market might create tomorrow. This is the clause that makes the swap definition so relevant to DeFi: novel on-chain instruments that their creators do not call "swaps" may nonetheless meet the definition if they function like swaps.

## The Exclusions

Not everything that looks like a derivative is a "swap." The statute explicitly excludes:

| Excluded Instrument | Statutory Basis | Rationale |
|--------------------|-----------------|-----------| 
| Security futures products | §1a(47)(B)(i) | Regulated under securities law |
| Sales of securities | §1a(47)(B)(ii) | Not derivatives |
| Options on securities (other than security indices) | §1a(47)(B)(iii) | SEC jurisdiction |
| FX swaps and FX forwards (with Treasury determination) | §1a(47)(B)(vi) | Treasury exemption, November 2012 |
| Consumer or commercial transactions | §1a(47)(B)(ii) | Ordinary business |
| Insurance products | §1a(47)(B)(iv) | State insurance regulation |

!!! danger "Critical Distinction: Insurance vs. CDS"
    Credit default swaps are economically similar to insurance (one party pays premiums, the other pays if a credit event occurs), but they are legally **not** insurance. The swap definition specifically excludes insurance products, but the CFTC has consistently held that CDS are swaps, not insurance contracts. Why? Because the protection buyer in a CDS need not own the underlying asset or have an "insurable interest." You can buy a CDS on a company you have no relationship with — that's speculation, not insurance. This matters because it means CDS (and their DeFi equivalents) face derivatives regulation, not state insurance regulation.


## The 2012 Joint Rulemaking: 600 Pages of Clarification

In 2012, the CFTC and SEC jointly published the "Further Definition of 'Swap,' 'Security-Based Swap,' and 'Security-Based Swap Agreement'" — a rulemaking document exceeding 600 pages in the Federal Register (77 Fed. Reg. 48208).

The key clarifications:

**Interest rate swaps** are "swaps" under CFTC jurisdiction. This includes fixed-for-floating, basis swaps, overnight index swaps, forward rate agreements, and caps/floors/collars on interest rates.

**Broad-based equity index swaps** (e.g., swaps on the S&P 500) are "swaps" under CFTC jurisdiction. **Single-name or narrow-based equity swaps** are "security-based swaps" under SEC jurisdiction.

**Single-name credit default swaps** are "security-based swaps" under SEC jurisdiction. **Broad-based index CDS** (e.g., CDX indices) are "swaps" under CFTC jurisdiction.

**Commodity swaps** (energy, metals, agricultural) are "swaps" under CFTC jurisdiction.

**FX instruments** are complex: FX swaps and FX forwards got a Treasury exemption, but FX options, non-deliverable forwards, and currency swaps with principal exchange remain fully regulated swaps.

!!! success ""
    **Key Point**: The joint rulemaking established that classification depends on the **underlying reference** of the instrument, not its structure. A cap on an interest rate is a swap (CFTC). A cap on a single stock price is a security-based swap (SEC). The economic structure is identical — only the reference changes.


## Application to DeFi

The swap definition's breadth means most DeFi derivatives fall within it:

| DeFi Instrument | Statutory Basis for Swap Classification |
|----------------|----------------------------------------|
| Perpetual futures (dYdX, GMX) | Agreement providing payments based on the value of a commodity/asset (§1a(47)(A)(iii)) |
| Synthetic assets (Synthetix) | Agreement providing payments dependent on the price of an underlying (§1a(47)(A)(iii)) |
| Prediction markets (Polymarket) | Agreement dependent on occurrence of an event with economic consequence (§1a(47)(A)(ii)) |
| Interest rate vaults (Pendle, Notional) | Agreement for exchange of payments based on interest rate levels (§1a(47)(A)(iii)) |
| Options vaults (Opyn, Ribbon) | Puts, calls, or options of any kind (§1a(47)(A)(i)) |

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note"
    When analyzing whether a DeFi protocol constitutes a "swap," use this prompt structure:

    *"Analyze the following DeFi protocol mechanism against CEA §1a(47). The protocol works as follows: [describe mechanism]. For each prong of §1a(47)(A), determine whether the protocol's mechanics satisfy the element. Then check each exclusion in §1a(47)(B) to determine if any exemption applies. Present your analysis as a classification matrix."*

    **What to verify:** AI models frequently over-classify instruments as swaps without adequately analyzing the exclusions. Always force the AI to separately analyze each exclusion — especially the forward contract exclusion (actual delivery) and the insurance exclusion — before accepting the classification.


!!! warning "AI Limitation Warning"
    AI models are trained on general legal text, not the 600-page 2012 joint rulemaking. When the AI says "this is a swap," it may be directionally correct but based on incomplete analysis. The Further Definition rulemaking contains critical nuances — interpretive guidance on mixed swaps, titling provisions for cross-jurisdictional instruments, and a detailed analysis of the insurance exclusion — that AI models typically do not fully internalize. Use AI for first-pass analysis, then verify against the actual regulatory text.

## Section Summary

- CEA §1a(47) defines "swap" with extraordinary breadth, including a forward-looking catch-all
- The definition captures puts, calls, options, event contracts, and payment-exchange agreements
- Key exclusions: security futures, FX swaps/forwards (Treasury exemption), insurance, consumer transactions
- The 2012 joint rulemaking (77 Fed. Reg. 48208) is the authoritative classification guide
- Most DeFi derivatives meet the swap definition based on their economic function
