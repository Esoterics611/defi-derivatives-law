# 16.1 Compliance Program Architecture

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Identify the four pillars of a derivatives compliance program
    - Explain CFTC Rule 23.600's risk management program requirements
    - Describe NFA Rule 2-9's supervisory obligations
    - Adapt each compliance pillar for DeFi protocol operations

---

## The Four Pillars

Every derivatives compliance program — whether for a $2 trillion swap dealer or a DeFi protocol with $50 million TVL — rests on four pillars:

1. **Policies & Procedures**: Written documentation of what the rules are and how they apply
2. **Training**: Ensuring all relevant personnel understand their compliance obligations
3. **Monitoring & Testing**: Systematically verifying that the rules are being followed
4. **Escalation & Remediation**: Identifying violations, reporting them, and fixing them

These aren't aspirational goals — they're regulatory requirements. CFTC Rule 23.600 mandates that swap dealers and major swap participants establish and maintain a comprehensive risk management program, and NFA Rule 2-9 requires diligent supervision of all business activities. The CFTC evaluates compliance programs not by whether violations occur (they will), but by whether the program was reasonably designed to prevent, detect, and correct them.

## Pillar 1: Policies & Procedures

Written compliance policies must cover every area of regulatory obligation. For a derivatives market participant, the minimum scope includes:

- **Trading policies**: authorized products, position limits, approved counterparties, order types
- **Risk management**: market risk limits, credit risk limits, liquidity risk procedures, stress testing
- **Recordkeeping**: CFTC Rules 23.201-23.203 require comprehensive records of all swaps, communications, and risk management activities
- **Business conduct**: CFTC Rules 23.400-23.451 cover disclosure, fair dealing, suitability, and conflicts of interest
- **Anti-manipulation**: policies prohibiting wash trading, spoofing, and other manipulative practices (covered in Chapter 17)
- **Privacy**: CFTC Part 160 governs privacy of consumer financial information

CFTC Rule 23.600(c) specifically requires that risk management policies be approved by the governing body (board of directors or senior management) and reviewed at least annually.

**DeFi adaptation:** For a DeFi protocol, "policies and procedures" translates to a combination of smart contract logic (rules encoded in code), governance documentation (rules approved by token holders), and operational procedures (rules followed by the core team). The challenge: who approves the policies in a DAO? Governance token holders are not a "board of directors" in any recognized legal sense.

## Pillar 2: Training

CFTC-registered entities must ensure that all employees and agents involved in swap activities understand their compliance obligations. NFA Rule 2-9 makes this explicit: the member firm is responsible for ensuring adequate knowledge at every level.

Training is also the CFTC's first question after a violation. When an employee engages in manipulation or violates a business conduct rule, the CFTC asks: were they trained? Was the training documented? Was it recent? If the answer to any of these is "no," the compliance program itself is deficient — and the firm faces additional penalties beyond the underlying violation.

**DeFi adaptation:** Protocol contributors, governance delegates, and community moderators all need training on regulatory obligations. The complication: many DAO contributors are pseudonymous, part-time, and distributed across jurisdictions. Building a training program for this workforce requires creative approaches — documentation-as-training, governance proposal templates that embed compliance requirements, and onboarding workflows for new contributors.

## Pillar 3: Monitoring & Testing

A compliance program that writes policies but doesn't verify adherence is a paper program — and the CFTC treats paper programs as worse than having no program at all, because they demonstrate awareness of obligations without follow-through.

Monitoring is continuous: real-time surveillance of trading activity, position monitoring, and exception alerting. Testing is periodic: reviews by compliance staff or independent auditors that assess whether monitoring is working and policies are being followed.

CFTC Rule 23.602 requires "diligent supervision" — the registered entity must establish and maintain a system to supervise the activities of partners, officers, employees, and agents that is reasonably designed to achieve compliance. The standard is not perfection; it's reasonable design and good-faith implementation.

**DeFi adaptation:** On-chain transparency provides a monitoring advantage: every transaction is publicly visible in real time. The challenge is analysis — converting raw blockchain data into compliance-relevant intelligence. Section 16.3 covers trade surveillance tools and approaches in detail.

## Pillar 4: Escalation & Remediation

When monitoring detects a potential violation, the compliance program must have a defined escalation path: who is notified, what investigation occurs, what reporting is required, and how the issue is remediated.

For CFTC-registered entities, certain violations require regulatory reporting — suspicious activity reports, large trader reports, and material event notifications. The CCO (Section 16.2) is responsible for ensuring these reports are filed and that the board or senior management is informed.

Remediation goes beyond fixing the immediate issue. The CFTC expects root cause analysis: why did the violation occur? Was it a policy gap, a training failure, a monitoring blind spot, or intentional misconduct? The answer determines the response — policy revision, additional training, enhanced monitoring, or disciplinary action.

**DeFi adaptation:** Escalation in a DAO is governed by governance processes. A compliance violation might require an emergency governance proposal, a multisig action, or a protocol pause. The absence of a clear chain of command — the defining feature of decentralized governance — is also the greatest compliance challenge. When everyone is responsible for compliance, no one is.

## The Compliance Element Matrix

| Compliance Element | CFTC Requirement | TradFi Implementation | DeFi Adaptation |
|-------------------|-----------------|----------------------|----------------|
| Written policies | Rule 23.600(c): board-approved risk management policies | Compliance manual, policy library, annual review | Governance-approved protocol rules, smart contract parameters, documented operating procedures |
| Training | NFA Rule 2-9: adequate supervision requires trained personnel | Annual compliance training, role-specific modules, documented attendance | Contributor onboarding docs, governance templates, compliance-aware development practices |
| Monitoring | Rule 23.602: diligent supervision systems | Real-time trade surveillance, position monitoring, exception alerts | On-chain analytics, protocol-level monitoring dashboards, automated anomaly detection |
| Testing | Rule 23.600(d): periodic review by independent party | Annual compliance testing, internal audit, external reviews | Smart contract audits, governance process reviews, on-chain behavior analysis |
| Escalation | Rule 3.3: CCO reporting to board and regulators | Escalation matrix, incident response plans, regulatory reporting procedures | Emergency governance proposals, multisig actions, protocol pause mechanisms |
| Remediation | Rule 23.600(e): corrective action for identified deficiencies | Root cause analysis, policy amendments, disciplinary action | Smart contract upgrades, parameter changes, contributor removal |

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note: Drafting a Compliance Manual"
    AI models can significantly accelerate compliance program development. Use this prompt structure:

    *"Draft a derivatives compliance manual for [entity type: swap dealer / DeFi protocol / FCM] covering: (1) trading policies based on CFTC Rules 23.600-23.602, (2) business conduct standards under Rules 23.400-23.451, (3) recordkeeping requirements under Rules 23.201-23.203, (4) risk management procedures, (5) escalation and reporting procedures, and (6) annual review and testing protocols. For each section, cite the specific CFTC rule, state the requirement, and describe the implementation procedure."*

    **What to Verify:** AI-generated compliance manuals will be structurally sound but often miss firm-specific nuances — the particular products traded, the counterparty mix, the operational structure. Treat AI output as a 70% draft that requires substantive customization by someone who knows the business.

    **AI Limitation Warning:** AI models may cite superseded CFTC rules or conflate CFTC requirements with SEC requirements. Always cross-reference rule citations against the current Code of Federal Regulations (Title 17, Chapter I).

## Section Summary

- Derivatives compliance programs rest on four pillars: policies, training, monitoring, and escalation
- CFTC Rule 23.600 mandates comprehensive risk management programs for swap dealers and MSPs
- NFA Rule 2-9 requires diligent supervision — the firm is responsible for its personnel's compliance
- Each pillar can be adapted for DeFi, but decentralized governance creates structural challenges around accountability and authority
- The CFTC evaluates compliance programs on reasonable design, not perfection
