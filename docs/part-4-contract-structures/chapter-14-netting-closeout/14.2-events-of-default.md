# 14.2 Events of Default & Termination Events

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Identify the eight Events of Default under ISDA Section 5(a)
    - Distinguish Events of Default from Termination Events
    - Explain grace periods and cure rights
    - Map ISDA default triggers to DeFi liquidation triggers

---

## Events of Default (Section 5(a))

An Event of Default is a serious breach by one party that gives the other party the right to terminate all transactions. The 2002 ISDA Master Agreement defines eight Events of Default:

| Event of Default | Section | Trigger | Grace Period | DeFi Equivalent |
|-----------------|---------|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Failure to Pay or Deliver** | 5(a)(i) | Party fails to make a required payment or delivery | 1 business day (3 for delivery) | Position fails to maintain minimum collateral (no grace period — immediate liquidation eligibility) |
| **Breach of Agreement** | 5(a)(ii) | Party breaches a covenant or obligation (other than payment) | 30 days after notice | No equivalent — smart contracts don't impose behavioral covenants |
| **Credit Support Default** | 5(a)(iii) | Default under a Credit Support Document (CSA) | Varies (typically 2-3 business days) | Collateral falls below required ratio (no grace period) |
| **Misrepresentation** | 5(a)(iv) | A representation proves to have been false or misleading | None (once discovered) | No equivalent — no representations in permissionless protocols |
| **Default Under Specified Transaction** | 5(a)(v) | Default on another derivatives transaction with the same counterparty or specified entity | As specified in underlying transaction | No cross-transaction default concept in DeFi |
| **Cross-Default** | 5(a)(vi) | Default on other financial obligations exceeding the Threshold Amount (if elected in Schedule) | As specified in underlying obligation | No equivalent — each protocol position is independent |
| **Bankruptcy** | 5(a)(vii) | Insolvency, bankruptcy filing, appointment of receiver, etc. | None for most triggers | Protocol "insolvency" = insurance fund depletion + socialized losses |
| **Merger Without Assumption** | 5(a)(viii) | Party merges or transfers assets and the surviving entity does not assume obligations | None | Token migration where new contract does not honor old positions |

## Termination Events (Section 5(b))

Termination Events are events that are **not the fault of either party** but that nonetheless affect the transaction. They result in partial termination (only affected transactions, not the entire relationship) unless the Schedule provides otherwise:

**Illegality (Section 5(b)(i)):** A change in law makes it illegal for a party to perform its obligations. Partial termination of affected transactions.

**Force Majeure (Section 5(b)(ii)):** An event beyond either party's control prevents performance for an extended period (typically 8 business days). Added in the 2002 version.

**Tax Event (Section 5(b)(iii)):** A change in tax law requires a party to gross up payments or creates adverse tax consequences.

**Credit Event Upon Merger (Section 5(b)(iv)):** A party's creditworthiness deteriorates materially following a merger.

**Additional Termination Events (Section 5(b)(v)):** Custom events added in the Schedule — e.g., NAV decline triggers for hedge fund counterparties, key person events, regulatory changes.

!!! warning "Grace Periods vs. Instant Liquidation"
    The fundamental difference between ISDA Events of Default and DeFi liquidation is **time**. ISDA provides grace periods — 1 to 30 days depending on the event — during which the defaulting party can cure the default. A party that misses a payment by one day can make the payment within the 1-business-day grace period and no Event of Default occurs. In DeFi, there is no grace period. The moment a position's collateral ratio drops below the liquidation threshold, the position is eligible for liquidation. This mechanical immediacy eliminates cure opportunities but also eliminates the exposure that accumulates during TradFi grace periods.

---

## Section Summary

- Eight Events of Default under ISDA Section 5(a) cover payment failure, breach, credit support default, misrepresentation, cross-default, bankruptcy, and merger risk
- Termination Events (Section 5(b)) cover no-fault events: illegality, force majeure, tax events
- Grace periods give defaulting parties time to cure — a mechanism absent in DeFi
- DeFi liquidation triggers are narrower (primarily collateral ratio) but execute instantly
