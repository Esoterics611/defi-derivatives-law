# 11.1 Who Is the "Person" in a DAO? Liability Frameworks

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain why the CEA's "person" definition matters for DAO liability
    - Describe the CFTC's theory that a DAO is an unincorporated association
    - Compare DAO liability structures to general partnerships, joint ventures, and other traditional forms
    - Identify which DAO participants are most exposed to personal liability

---

## The Statutory Framework

The Commodity Exchange Act regulates the conduct of **"persons."** CEA §1a(38) defines "person" to include "an individual, association, partnership, corporation, and trust." This list is broad but finite — and the critical question for DeFi is whether a DAO fits any of these categories.

A DAO is not a corporation (unless it has incorporated). It is not a trust (unless structured as one). It is typically not a formal partnership (no partnership agreement). The CFTC's answer, validated by the court in *CFTC v. Ooki DAO*, is that a DAO is an **association** — specifically, an unincorporated association under state law.

## The Unincorporated Association Theory

An unincorporated association is, under most state laws, a voluntary group of persons joined by mutual consent for a common purpose. It has no separate legal existence from its members unless granted one by statute. The key characteristics:

**No formalities required.** Unlike a corporation or LLC, an unincorporated association does not need to file formation documents. It comes into existence simply when people agree to act together. A group of token holders who collectively govern a protocol through on-chain voting are, in the CFTC's view, an unincorporated association by default.

**Members are personally liable.** Because an unincorporated association has no separate legal personality (in most states), its members bear personal liability for the association's obligations. This is analogous to a general partnership — each partner is jointly and severally liable for the partnership's debts and legal violations.

**It is a "person" under the CEA.** The court in *Ooki DAO* held that an unincorporated association is an "association" under CEA §1a(38) and therefore a "person" subject to CFTC jurisdiction. The DAO's lack of formal legal structure did not protect it — it exposed it.

## Comparison to Traditional Legal Forms

| Legal Form | Formation | Liability Structure | Regulatory Treatment | DAO Parallel |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| **General Partnership** | Agreement (can be informal or implied) | Joint and several liability for all partners | Each partner is a "person"; partnership itself is also a "person" | Most DAOs without legal wrappers — members who participate in governance are treated as general partners |
| **Joint Venture** | Agreement for a specific project or limited purpose | Liability limited to the scope of the venture | Participants in the venture are liable for its acts | DAOs formed for a specific protocol or investment thesis |
| **Unincorporated Association** | Mutual consent for a common purpose | Members liable unless statute limits liability | The association is a "person" under most regulatory statutes | The CFTC's default classification for DAOs |
| **LLC** | State filing (articles of organization) | Members' liability limited to their investment | LLC is a separate "person"; members generally shielded | Wyoming DAO LLC, Marshall Islands DAO LLC |
| **Foundation** | Jurisdiction-specific formation (Cayman, Swiss, etc.) | Foundation is a separate entity; directors have fiduciary duties but limited personal liability | Foundation is the regulated "person" | Cayman Foundation Company (dYdX, Uniswap) |

## Who Is Liable Within a DAO?

Not all DAO participants face equal exposure. The CFTC's enforcement theory, combined with general principles of association law, creates a hierarchy of liability:

**Founders and core developers** face the highest exposure. They created the protocol, often controlled early governance, and may have personally profited from its operation. In the Ooki DAO case, Tom Bean and Kyle Kistner (bZeroX founders) were charged individually alongside the DAO.

**Active governance participants** — token holders who vote on proposals, submit proposals, or serve as delegates — are the next tier. The *Ooki DAO* holding specifically identified governance voting as the conduct that created liability. If you vote to continue operating an unregistered derivatives platform, you are directing the association's illegal activity.

**Passive token holders** who never vote likely have the strongest argument against liability. Merely holding a governance token, without exercising governance rights, may not constitute the "mutual consent" and "common purpose" required for association membership. However, this has not been tested — no court has drawn a clear line between passive holding and active governance.

**Liquidity providers** who deposit assets into a protocol's pools occupy an ambiguous middle ground. They are not governing the protocol, but they are enabling its operation. Whether LP'ing constitutes participation in an unincorporated association is an open question.

!!! danger "AI Limitation Warning"
    The *Ooki DAO* decision is a single district court opinion, not appellate or Supreme Court precedent. AI models may present the holding as more settled law than it actually is. The unincorporated association theory has been applied to DAOs only once in a contested enforcement context, and future courts may adopt different frameworks — including treating DAOs as partnerships, joint ventures, or sui generis entities. Use AI for framework analysis, but do not rely on it for definitive statements about DAO liability law, which remains in early development.

---

## Section Summary

- The CEA regulates "persons," and a DAO is a "person" if classified as an association under §1a(38)
- The CFTC's theory, validated in *Ooki DAO*, is that DAOs are unincorporated associations
- Members of unincorporated associations face joint and several personal liability
- Liability exposure varies by participation level: founders > active voters > delegates > passive holders
- The law is unsettled — *Ooki DAO* is a single district court decision, not binding appellate precedent
