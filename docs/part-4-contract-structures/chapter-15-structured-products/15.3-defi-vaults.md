# 15.3 DeFi Vaults as Structured Products

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Analyze DeFi yield vaults as structured products under securities and derivatives law
    - Identify the embedded derivatives in covered call, delta-neutral, and basis trade vaults
    - Evaluate the regulatory classification of vault operators as investment managers
    - Assess specific protocols' vault strategies and their legal exposure

---

## The Vault = Structured Product Equivalence

A DeFi yield vault operates as follows:

1. **User deposits** collateral (ETH, USDC, wBTC, etc.)
2. **Vault executes a strategy** — selling options, providing liquidity, running arbitrage, or combining multiple DeFi positions
3. **User receives yield** — the strategy's returns, minus fees, distributed proportionally to depositors

Strip away the branding, and this is the definition of a structured product: a packaged investment that combines a deposit with a derivative strategy to generate returns. The vault operator is the structurer. The depositors are the investors. The smart contract is the prospectus — except it's not registered, not reviewed by regulators, and often not even understood by depositors.

## Covered Call Vaults

**How they work:** Users deposit an asset (e.g., ETH). The vault sells weekly out-of-the-money call options on that asset. The option premium is distributed to depositors as "yield." If the options expire worthless, depositors keep their ETH plus the premium. If ETH rallies past the strike price, the vault's ETH is called away and depositors participate in none of the upside above the strike.

**Protocols:** Ribbon Finance (now Aevo), StakeDAO, Friktion (defunct, Solana), Thetanuts Finance.

**Embedded derivative:** Short call option. The depositor's economic position is identical to a covered call strategy — long the underlying asset, short a call option. This is a textbook derivatives position.

**Regulatory analysis:** The vault share is a security (pooled investment, common enterprise, expectation of profit from others' efforts — the vault operator selects strikes, expirations, and counterparties). The embedded option is a derivative. The vault operator is functioning as both an investment manager (selecting strategy) and a derivatives dealer (selling options using customer assets).

| Vault Type | Strategy | Embedded Derivative(s) | Risk Profile | TradFi Equivalent |
|-----------|---------|----------------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Covered call | Sell OTM calls on deposited assets | Short call option | Capped upside, full downside | Structured note with covered call overlay |
| Put-selling | Sell OTM puts, collateralized by stablecoins | Short put option | Enhanced yield, downside risk from strike | Cash-secured put writing fund |
| Strangle | Sell OTM calls AND puts | Short call + short put | Premium income, tail risk in both directions | Volatility selling fund |

## Delta-Neutral Vaults

**How they work:** Users deposit stablecoins. The vault constructs a hedged position — typically going long on a perpetual futures platform while shorting the same asset on another, or vice versa — to capture the funding rate differential or basis spread without directional exposure.

**Protocols:** Ethena (USDe), various basis trade vaults.

**Embedded derivatives:** Long and short perpetual futures positions (each a derivative), plus the funding rate swap component inherent in perpetual futures. The "yield" comes from the funding rate paid by leveraged longs to shorts (or vice versa, depending on market conditions).

**Regulatory analysis:** These vaults are pure derivatives strategies. The underlying is not a bond or deposit — it's a pair of offsetting derivatives positions. Every dollar of "yield" comes from derivatives trading. Under Dodd-Frank, perpetual futures are likely swaps (see Chapter 9). A vault that trades swaps using customer deposits is operating as an unregistered commodity pool.

!!! danger ""
    **Critical Regulatory Risk:** If you operate a vault that sells options using user deposits, you are structuring and distributing a derivatives product. Under U.S. law, you are likely: (1) an unregistered investment company or commodity pool, (2) an unregistered investment adviser or commodity trading advisor, (3) offering unregistered securities, and (4) trading derivatives without swap dealer or futures commission merchant registration. Each violation is independently actionable.


## Ethena's USDe: A Case Study in Complexity

Ethena's USDe stablecoin illustrates the regulatory complexity. USDe maintains its peg through a delta-neutral strategy: deposited staked ETH (stETH) is hedged with short ETH perpetual futures positions on centralized exchanges. The "yield" from sUSDe (staked USDe) comes from two sources: ETH staking rewards and perpetual futures funding rates.

Decomposed:
- **Deposit component:** stETH (a liquid staking token — itself potentially a security)
- **Derivative component:** Short ETH perpetual futures (likely a swap under CEA)
- **Yield source:** Staking rewards + funding rate (derivatives income)
- **Risk:** Negative funding rates, exchange counterparty risk, stETH depeg risk, smart contract risk

This is a structured product by any reasonable definition. The investor deposits collateral, the protocol executes a derivatives-based strategy, and the investor receives yield. Ethena's significant market cap makes it one of the most systemically important unregistered structured products in DeFi.

## The Commodity Pool Analysis

Under CFTC regulations, a **commodity pool** is any investment trust, syndicate, or similar enterprise that trades in commodity futures, options, or swaps. A **commodity pool operator (CPO)** is any person or entity that solicits funds for the purpose of trading in commodity interests.

DeFi vaults that trade derivatives using pooled user deposits fit squarely within this framework:

| CFTC Concept | TradFi Implementation | DeFi Vault Equivalent |
|-------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Commodity pool | Hedge fund trading futures/swaps | Vault smart contract holding pooled deposits |
| Commodity pool operator | Fund manager registered with NFA | Vault deployer / DAO governance |
| Commodity trading advisor | Advisor recommending futures trades | Strategy designer / parameter setter |
| Commodity interest | Futures, options, swaps | Perpetual futures, DeFi options, funding rate swaps |

The CFTC has not yet brought an enforcement action specifically against a DeFi vault operator for operating an unregistered commodity pool. But the legal framework is clear, and the agency's enforcement against Ooki DAO, bZeroX, and Polymarket demonstrates willingness to apply traditional derivatives regulation to DeFi.

## Section Summary

- DeFi yield vaults are structured products: pooled deposits + derivative strategies = customized yield
- Covered call vaults embed short options; delta-neutral vaults are pure derivatives strategies
- Vault operators function as commodity pool operators, investment managers, and derivatives dealers — all without registration
- Ethena's USDe demonstrates the complexity: staking token + perpetual futures hedge + funding rate yield
- CFTC enforcement against DeFi vault operators as unregistered CPOs is a significant and growing risk
