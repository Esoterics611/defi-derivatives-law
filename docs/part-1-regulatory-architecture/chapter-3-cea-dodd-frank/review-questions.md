# Chapter 3: Review Questions

!!! info "Instructions"
    These questions test both conceptual understanding and applied analysis. Questions marked with 🤖 are designed to be completed using AI assistance — practice structuring prompts and verifying AI output against the chapter content.


---

## Conceptual Questions

**1.** Explain why the definition of "commodity" in the CEA (7 U.S.C. §1a(9)) is considered the CFTC's most powerful jurisdictional tool for asserting authority over digital assets. What specific language gives it this breadth?

**2.** The Commodity Futures Modernization Act of 2000 (CFMA) explicitly excluded OTC derivatives from CFTC oversight. In hindsight, what specific risks did this exclusion create that materialized in the AIG crisis?

**3.** Dodd-Frank Title VII establishes four pillars of swap regulation: mandatory clearing, exchange trading, reporting, and registration. Rank these four pillars in order of importance for preventing another AIG-scale crisis, and justify your ranking.

**4.** Explain the difference between an "excluded commodity" and an "exempt commodity" under the CEA. Why does this distinction matter for how different types of swaps are regulated?

**5.** What is the "forward contract exclusion," and why is the "actual delivery" test within it one of the most contested issues in digital asset regulation?

## Applied Analysis

**6.** A DeFi protocol allows users to deposit ETH as collateral and mint synthetic tokens that track the price of gold. Using the swap taxonomy from Section 3.4, classify this instrument. Is it a "swap," a "security-based swap," or something else? What factors determine the classification?

**7.** Consider a decentralized prediction market that offers binary options on the outcome of Federal Reserve interest rate decisions. Using the exemption framework from Section 3.3, analyze whether this instrument qualifies for any exclusion or exemption under the CEA. If not, what registration requirements would the platform face?

**8.** A DAO governance proposal suggests creating a "DeFi Clearinghouse" — a smart contract that interposes itself between counterparties in on-chain derivatives trades, collects margin, and guarantees settlement. Using the Dodd-Frank framework from Section 3.2, what CFTC registration category would this smart contract system fall under? What regulatory obligations would attach?

## 🤖 AI-Assisted Questions

**9.** 🤖 Use an AI model to trace the regulatory history of a specific CFTC rule (choose one: Part 23 margin rules, Part 39 DCO requirements, or Part 43 real-time reporting). Prompt the AI to explain: (a) the Dodd-Frank statutory mandate, (b) the proposed rule, (c) key comment period issues, and (d) the final rule. **Then verify** at least three specific claims against the CFTC's Federal Register entries. Document any inaccuracies in the AI's output.

**10.** 🤖 Prompt an AI model with this scenario: *"A DeFi protocol offers leveraged trading of tokenized commodity futures with no KYC requirement and liquidity provided by an anonymous pool. The protocol is governed by a DAO with 12,000 token holders. Analyze the CFTC regulatory implications."* Evaluate the AI's response against what you've learned in Chapters 1-3. Where does the AI get the analysis right? Where does it oversimplify or miss key nuances?

!!! warning "AI Verification Practice"
    For questions 9 and 10, the point is not just getting a good AI response — it's developing the skill of **critically evaluating** AI legal analysis. Document: (a) claims you can verify as accurate, (b) claims you can verify as inaccurate, (c) claims you cannot verify without access to primary legal sources. This three-category assessment is the foundation of AI-augmented legal practice.
