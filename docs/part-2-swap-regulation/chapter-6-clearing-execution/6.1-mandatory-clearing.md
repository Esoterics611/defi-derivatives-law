# 6.1 Mandatory Clearing & the Clearing Mandate

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain the statutory basis for the clearing mandate under Dodd-Frank
    - Identify which swap categories are currently subject to mandatory clearing
    - Describe the CFTC's clearing determination process
    - Analyze exemptions from the clearing mandate and their DeFi implications

---

## The Statutory Mandate

Section 2(h)(1) of the CEA is blunt: **any swap that the CFTC has determined is required to be cleared must be submitted to a registered DCO for clearing.** This is the single most consequential infrastructure requirement in Dodd-Frank's swap framework. Before 2010, the vast majority of OTC derivatives traded bilaterally with no central counterparty standing between the parties. When AIG collapsed under the weight of $440 billion in uncleared CDS exposure, the case for mandatory clearing wrote itself.

The clearing mandate does not apply to all swaps. The CFTC must affirmatively determine that a specific swap category is subject to the mandate — and the process for making that determination is rigorous.

## The Clearing Determination Process

The CFTC can initiate a clearing determination through two pathways:

**Bottom-up (DCO petition):** A DCO that already clears a category of swaps submits a request to the CFTC under CEA §2(h)(2)(B). The CFTC then reviews whether mandatory clearing is appropriate for that category.

**Top-down (CFTC initiative):** The CFTC can initiate a review on its own under CEA §2(h)(2)(A), examining any group, category, type, or class of swaps.

In both cases, the CFTC considers five statutory factors:

1. **The existence of significant outstanding notional exposures, trading liquidity, and adequate pricing data** — is this market large enough and liquid enough that clearing is feasible?
2. **The availability of rule framework, capacity, operational expertise and resources, and credit support infrastructure** — can the DCOs actually handle it?
3. **The effect on the mitigation of systemic risk** — does clearing this category meaningfully reduce systemic risk?
4. **The effect on competition** — will the mandate unduly concentrate clearing in one DCO?
5. **The existence of reasonable legal certainty** — can the DCO manage the legal risk of clearing this product?

## What Must Be Cleared Today

The CFTC has issued clearing determinations for two broad categories:

| Swap Category | Specific Products | DCO(s) | Clearing Required Since | DeFi Parallel |
|--------------|-------------------|--------|------------------------|---------------|
| **Interest Rate Swaps** | Fixed-to-floating (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY); Basis swaps; FRAs; OIS | CME, LCH | March 2013 | Pendle fixed-rate markets; Notional Finance fixed-rate lending |
| **Credit Default Swaps** | CDX.NA.IG; CDX.NA.HY; iTraxx Europe (IG, Crossover, HiVol) | ICE Clear Credit, ICE Clear Europe | March 2013 | Credit default protocols (largely theoretical in DeFi; Opium Protocol attempted this) |

These two categories represent the overwhelming majority of OTC derivatives by notional volume. The Bank for International Settlements reports that interest rate derivatives alone exceed $500 trillion in notional outstanding, and roughly 75% of new IRS transactions are now centrally cleared.

!!! warning "What Is NOT Subject to Mandatory Clearing"
    The clearing mandate does **not** currently cover:

    - **Single-name CDS** — too illiquid and idiosyncratic for standardized clearing
    - **Equity swaps** — including total return swaps (recall Archegos from Chapter 5)
    - **Commodity swaps** — energy, metals, and agricultural swaps remain largely uncleared
    - **FX instruments** — the Treasury exemption covers FX swaps and FX forwards; FX options remain uncleared
    - **Bespoke or exotic swaps** — anything too customized for a DCO's margin models
    - **Inter-affiliate swaps** — transactions between commonly controlled entities (CFTC Part 50 exemption)

## The End-User Exemption (Preview)

Even for swaps that are subject to the clearing mandate, non-financial entities using swaps to hedge commercial risk can elect the **end-user exception** under CEA §2(h)(7). We cover this in detail in Section 6.5, but note it here because it is the single most important carve-out from the clearing mandate — and the one most likely to be invoked (or misused) by DAO treasuries claiming to hedge protocol risk.

## DeFi Implications

The clearing mandate creates a stark problem for DeFi derivatives protocols. Consider a protocol like GMX or dYdX that facilitates trading in perpetual futures on ETH or BTC. If those instruments are classified as swaps — and the CFTC has strongly signaled they would be — the question becomes: **must they be cleared through a registered DCO?**

Currently, no clearing determination covers crypto-asset derivatives specifically. But the CFTC has plenary authority to issue one. The moment the CFTC determines that a category of crypto-asset swaps must be cleared, every protocol facilitating those trades faces an impossible choice: route transactions through a registered DCO (which would require KYC, margin rules, and centralization) or cease operations for U.S. persons.

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note"
    **Using AI to determine if a specific swap type is subject to the clearing mandate:**

    *"I need to determine whether [describe specific instrument] is subject to the CFTC's mandatory clearing requirement. Analyze this instrument against: (1) the current clearing determinations in 17 C.F.R. Part 50, Subpart C; (2) the five statutory factors the CFTC considers under CEA §2(h)(2); and (3) any applicable exemptions (end-user, inter-affiliate, cooperative). Present your analysis as a decision tree: Is it a swap → Is it in a cleared category → Does an exemption apply → Result."*

    **What to verify:** AI models often know that IRS and index CDS must be cleared but lack precision on the specific product specifications (tenor ranges, currency pairs, index series). Always cross-reference the AI's classification against the CFTC's actual clearing requirement rules in Part 50.

!!! danger "AI Limitation Warning"
    AI models are not reliably current on CFTC clearing determinations. The CFTC periodically updates which specific products within a category must be cleared (e.g., adding new currency pairs for IRS). If the AI says a specific instrument is or isn't subject to clearing, verify against the current Part 50 regulations on the CFTC's website. Do not rely on AI for binary "must clear / need not clear" determinations without independent verification.

## Section Summary

- CEA §2(h)(1) requires clearing of swaps that the CFTC has determined must be cleared
- The CFTC evaluates five factors: notional exposure, DCO capacity, systemic risk, competition, and legal certainty
- Currently, IRS and index CDS are subject to mandatory clearing; most other categories are not
- DeFi derivatives protocols face existential risk if the CFTC issues clearing determinations for crypto-asset swaps
- The end-user exception (Section 6.5) is the primary carve-out from the mandate
