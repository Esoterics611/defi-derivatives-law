# Case Study: Polymarket — CFTC Settlement & the Election Markets Debate

!!! info "Case Study Objectives"
    This case study traces Polymarket's regulatory journey — from CFTC enforcement to becoming the largest prediction market in the world — to illustrate the evolving regulatory landscape for event contracts.

---

## Phase 1: The CFTC Settlement (2022)

In January 2022, the CFTC settled charges against Blockratize, Inc. (doing business as **Polymarket**) for offering off-exchange event contracts — binary option contracts on various outcomes — to U.S. retail customers without proper registration.

**The charges:** Polymarket offered event-based binary options contracts (e.g., "Will [event] occur by [date]?") that settled at $1 if the event occurred and $0 if it did not. These contracts were "swaps" under CEA §1a(47) (agreements dependent on the occurrence of an event with financial consequence) and/or "options" under CEA §4c(b). Polymarket offered them without being registered as a DCM or SEF.

**The settlement:** Polymarket paid a **$1.4 million civil monetary penalty** and agreed to wind down non-compliant markets. Critically, the settlement required Polymarket to cease offering binary options to U.S. persons. Polymarket implemented geo-blocking for U.S. IP addresses.

**The founder:** Shayne Coplan, Polymarket's founder, was in his early twenties at the time of the settlement. The CFTC's enforcement was relatively lenient — a monetary penalty and compliance commitment rather than litigation or criminal referral — reflecting the agency's somewhat sympathetic view of prediction markets as potentially useful information tools.

## Phase 2: The Election Markets Explosion (2024)

Despite the CFTC settlement, Polymarket did not shut down. It geo-blocked U.S. users and continued operating for non-U.S. users. In 2024, Polymarket experienced explosive growth driven by the U.S. presidential election:

- Open interest in the presidential election market exceeded **$300 million**
- Daily trading volume on election markets peaked at over **$100 million**
- Polymarket's election probability estimates were widely cited by mainstream media, including Bloomberg, the New York Times, and the Wall Street Journal
- Polymarket's prediction accuracy for the 2024 election was widely discussed as superior to traditional polling

## Phase 3: The Kalshi Ruling's Impact

The Kalshi v. CFTC litigation (2023-2024) reshaped the legal landscape for prediction markets. Kalshi, a CFTC-registered DCM, sought to list congressional election control contracts. The CFTC objected, arguing these were "gaming" contracts prohibited under §5c(c)(5)(C).

In September 2024 the federal district court in D.C. (Judge Jia Cobb) ruled for Kalshi, finding that its congressional control contracts did not involve unlawful activity or gaming as the statute uses those terms. The D.C. Circuit declined to keep the ruling on hold pending appeal, Kalshi listed election contracts before the 2024 election, and in 2025 the CFTC dropped its appeal. The outcome opened the door for registered exchanges to list election contracts.

## Current Regulatory Status

Polymarket's position has moved from enforcement target toward registration:

- It settled with the CFTC and agreed to restrict U.S. access
- Its geo-blocking was widely circumvented by U.S. users with VPNs
- The Kalshi ruling legitimized election markets, potentially reducing Polymarket's regulatory risk if it were to seek registration
- In November 2024, French authorities investigated Polymarket's operations as potential unregulated gambling
- In July 2025, after the DOJ and CFTC closed their investigations into U.S. access, Polymarket agreed to acquire QCEX, a CFTC-licensed exchange and clearinghouse, for $112 million to return to the U.S. under registration

!!! warning "The Unresolved Question"
    Polymarket's success created a paradox: the market proved that prediction markets provide valuable information (accurate probability estimates, efficient price discovery), but it did so through an unregistered platform that violated U.S. law. The question is whether the demonstrated value of prediction markets will lead to a more permissive regulatory framework — or whether the CFTC will intensify enforcement against unregistered platforms, including pursuing users who circumvent geo-blocking.
