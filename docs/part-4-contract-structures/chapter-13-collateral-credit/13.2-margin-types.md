# 13.2 Initial Margin, Variation Margin & UMR

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Distinguish between initial margin and variation margin
    - Explain the BCBS/IOSCO Uncleared Margin Rules and their phased implementation
    - Describe ISDA SIMM and how it calculates initial margin
    - Compare TradFi margin frameworks to DeFi over-collateralization

---

## Two Types of Margin

Derivatives margin serves two distinct purposes, and understanding the distinction is essential for comparing TradFi and DeFi risk management:

**Variation Margin (VM)** reflects the **current** mark-to-market exposure between counterparties. If Party A's swap has moved $5 million in Party B's favor since the last valuation, Party A must post $5 million in VM to Party B. VM is exchanged daily and represents a cash settlement of accumulated gains and losses. It is a backward-looking measure — it covers the exposure that has already materialized.

**Initial Margin (IM)** covers the **potential future** exposure that could materialize during the period needed to close out a defaulted counterparty's positions. If it takes 10 business days to unwind a complex derivatives portfolio, and the portfolio could lose up to $20 million in adverse market conditions during that period, $20 million in IM is required. IM is a forward-looking measure — it covers the exposure that might materialize if things go wrong.

| Feature | Variation Margin | Initial Margin |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| **What it covers** | Current mark-to-market exposure | Potential future exposure during close-out |
| **Calculation basis** | Portfolio valuation at market prices | Risk model (SIMM or internal model) |
| **Exchange frequency** | Daily | At inception and upon material changes |
| **Direction** | Bilateral (loser pays winner) | Bilateral (both parties post) |
| **Collateral type** | Cash only (post-UMR) | Cash, sovereign bonds, agency MBS, equities (with haircuts) |
| **Rehypothecation** | Permitted (title transfer CSA) | Prohibited (must be held in segregated custody) |
| **DeFi equivalent** | Unrealized PnL reflected in position value / funding rate payments | Collateral posted at position opening (initial deposit) |

## The Uncleared Margin Rules

The BCBS/IOSCO framework for margin requirements on non-centrally cleared derivatives (the "Uncleared Margin Rules" or UMR) represents the most significant post-crisis reform for bilateral derivatives. Phased in from 2016 through September 2022, UMR requires covered entities to:

1. Exchange VM daily for all uncleared derivatives (effective since March 2017)
2. Post and collect IM on a bilateral, two-way basis (phased implementation based on aggregate average notional amount, completed September 2022)
3. Segregate collected IM with an independent third-party custodian (no rehypothecation)
4. Calculate IM using either ISDA SIMM or an approved internal model

The IM threshold is $50 million per counterparty group — meaning IM is not required for the first $50 million of calculated IM between two counterparty groups. This threshold was designed to exempt smaller participants from IM requirements.

## ISDA SIMM

The Standard Initial Margin Model (SIMM) is ISDA's industry-standard methodology for calculating bilateral IM under UMR. Developed to avoid the inconsistency that would result from each firm using its own internal model, SIMM is:

**Risk-sensitivity based:** Rather than calculating margin from the full revaluation of positions under stress scenarios (computationally expensive), SIMM uses the risk sensitivities (deltas, vegas, curvatures) of each trade to estimate potential future exposure. Parties exchange sensitivities and can independently verify each other's IM calculations.

**Calibrated to a 99% confidence interval over a 10-day margin period of risk (MPOR):** The model is designed to cover 99% of potential adverse moves during the time needed to close out a defaulted portfolio.

**Recalibrated annually:** ISDA updates SIMM parameters each year to reflect current market conditions and volatility regimes.

## DeFi's Over-Collateralization Approach

DeFi protocols take a fundamentally different approach to margin: rather than using risk models to calculate minimum margin requirements, most protocols require **over-collateralization** at fixed ratios.

| Protocol | Collateral Ratio | Liquidation Threshold | Effective IM | Approach |
|----------|-----------------|----------------------|-------------|----------|
| **MakerDAO** | 150% minimum | Liquidation at 150% (vaults) | 50% of borrowed value | Fixed ratio, no risk model |
| **Aave** | 120-180% depending on asset | Varies by asset volatility tier | 20-80% depending on asset | Asset-specific but still fixed ratios |
| **GMX** | Variable (leverage up to 50x) | Liquidation when losses approach collateral | As low as 2% of position value (50x leverage) | Leverage-based, not risk-model-based |
| **dYdX** | 5% initial, 3% maintenance | Liquidation at maintenance margin | 5% of position value (20x leverage) | Fixed percentages, similar to futures exchange rules |

The contrast is stark: TradFi uses sophisticated risk models (SIMM) calibrated to specific portfolio compositions, confidence intervals, and market conditions. DeFi uses fixed ratios or simple leverage limits. TradFi's approach is more precise but requires institutional infrastructure (risk systems, regulatory approval, counterparty negotiations). DeFi's approach is simpler and more transparent but systematically over-collateralizes low-risk positions and under-collateralizes high-risk positions compared to what a risk model would require.

!!! warning "The Over-Collateralization Paradox"
    DeFi protocols require 150% collateral for a simple stablecoin loan (50% buffer) but allow 50x leverage on perpetual futures (2% buffer). From a risk management perspective, this is backward — the perpetual futures position has far more potential future exposure than the stablecoin loan. The difference exists because DeFi lending protocols evolved from conservative collateral models, while DeFi perp protocols evolved from crypto exchange margin trading, which has always permitted extreme leverage. Regulatory frameworks like UMR would impose more consistent, risk-based margin requirements across both.

---

## Section Summary

- Variation margin covers current exposure (daily mark-to-market); initial margin covers potential future exposure during close-out
- UMR requires bilateral IM and VM exchange for uncleared derivatives, with IM held in segregated custody
- ISDA SIMM is the industry-standard IM calculation methodology, based on risk sensitivities and calibrated to 99% confidence
- DeFi uses fixed collateral ratios instead of risk models, resulting in inconsistent margin levels across protocol types
