# Review Questions: Chapter 1

!!! info "How to Use These Questions"
    These questions test both conceptual understanding and practical application. For questions marked **[AI Exercise]**, use an AI model to generate an initial answer, then critically evaluate the AI's output for accuracy, completeness, and potential hallucinations.


---

**1. Systemic Risk and Regulation**

Explain why the AIG credit default swap crisis demonstrated the need for derivatives regulation. What specific regulatory failures allowed AIG to accumulate $440 billion in CDS exposure without adequate collateral?

---

**2. Jurisdictional Classification**

A DeFi protocol offers perpetual futures on the following three assets: (a) Bitcoin, (b) Apple stock, (c) a governance token that was distributed through an ICO with promises of profit sharing. For each asset, identify which federal regulator (CFTC, SEC, or both) would likely claim jurisdiction over the derivative, and explain your reasoning.

---

**3. Regulatory Ecosystem Mapping**

A DeFi protocol operates an on-chain order book for trading commodity swaps, collects margin from counterparties, and publishes all transaction data on a public blockchain. Which traditional regulated entities (DCM, SEF, DCO, SDR) does this protocol most closely resemble? Could it be classified as more than one?

---

**4. The Commodity Definition**

The CEA defines "commodity" to include "all services, rights, and interests... in which contracts for future delivery are presently or in the future dealt in." Explain why this definition is both extraordinarily broad and potentially circular. How did the launch of CME Bitcoin futures in 2017 resolve the circularity question for Bitcoin?

---

**5. CFTC vs. SEC Enforcement Powers**

Compare the CFTC's enforcement authority over commodity fraud (CEA §6(c)) with its regulatory authority over derivatives markets. Why is the distinction between "regulatory jurisdiction" and "enforcement jurisdiction" critical for DeFi protocols?

---

**6. Sufficient Decentralization [AI Exercise]**

Prompt an AI model to analyze whether a specific DeFi governance token passes the Howey test as a security. Then evaluate the AI's response: Does it correctly apply all four Howey prongs? Does it address the "sufficient decentralization" theory? Does it cite real case law or fabricated cases?

---

**7. Digital Asset Classification Decision Tree**

Walk through the regulatory classification decision tree from Section 1.2.6 for the following instrument: a DeFi protocol that allows users to deposit USDC as collateral and open leveraged long or short positions on ETH price, with positions automatically liquidated if the collateral ratio falls below 110%. What type of instrument is this? Which regulator has jurisdiction?

---

**8. The Expanding CFTC Scope**

Trace the CFTC's jurisdictional expansion from Coinflip (2015) through the Eisenberg prosecution (convicted 2024, vacated 2025). What pattern do you observe? Based on this trajectory, predict what types of DeFi protocols the CFTC is most likely to target next.

---

**9. Regulatory Entity Comparison [AI Exercise]**

Use an AI model to generate a comparison table of DCM, SEF, and DCO registration requirements, including: (a) statutory basis, (b) number of Core Principles, (c) key operational requirements, (d) current number of registered entities. Verify the AI's output against CFTC.gov registrant lists. Note any inaccuracies.

---

**10. Real-World Application**

You are advising a DeFi protocol that offers: (a) spot trading of various tokens on an AMM, (b) perpetual futures on BTC and ETH with up to 50x leverage, and (c) a prediction market for US election outcomes. Analyze the regulatory classification of each product line and identify which registrations the protocol would need to operate legally in the United States.
