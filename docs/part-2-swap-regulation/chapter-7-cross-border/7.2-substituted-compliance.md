# 7.2 Substituted Compliance & Comparability

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Explain the concept of substituted compliance and its policy rationale
    - Describe how the CFTC makes comparability determinations
    - Identify which jurisdictions have received comparability determinations and for which regulatory areas
    - Analyze why substituted compliance is structurally unavailable for DeFi protocols

---

## The Concept

Substituted compliance is the CFTC's mechanism for avoiding duplicative regulation. The logic is straightforward: if a non-U.S. swap dealer is already subject to a foreign regulatory regime that achieves comparable outcomes to CFTC regulations, requiring full independent compliance with both regimes would be wasteful and anticompetitive. Instead, the CFTC can determine that compliance with the foreign regime satisfies the equivalent U.S. requirement.

This is **not** mutual recognition (where two regulators agree to defer to each other). It is a unilateral CFTC determination that a specific foreign jurisdiction's rules, **in a specific regulatory area**, are comparable to the CFTC's own rules. The CFTC retains full authority to revoke or modify a comparability determination, and it evaluates each regulatory area separately — a jurisdiction might receive substituted compliance for margin rules but not for reporting rules.

## The Comparability Determination Process

A non-U.S. swap dealer (or its home regulator) can request a comparability determination from the CFTC. The CFTC then conducts an **outcome-based assessment**: it does not require that the foreign rules be identical to U.S. rules, but rather that they achieve comparable regulatory outcomes.

The CFTC evaluates comparability across several regulatory categories:

- **Entity-level requirements:** Capital adequacy, risk management, internal business conduct, CCO requirements
- **Transaction-level requirements:** Trade execution, clearing, margin, trade reporting, real-time public reporting, external business conduct

For each category, the CFTC compares the substance and enforcement of the foreign rules against its own. The assessment considers the scope of the rules, the rigor of enforcement, and the supervisory capacity of the foreign regulator.

## Current Comparability Determinations

| Jurisdiction | Regulatory Framework | Areas of Comparability | Key Gaps |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------------|----------|
| **European Union** | EMIR, MiFID II/MiFIR, CRD IV | Margin, risk management, clearing (for EU CCPs), some reporting | Trade execution (MiFID trading obligations differ structurally from SEF requirements) |
| **Japan** | FIEA (Financial Instruments and Exchange Act), JFSA rules | Margin, capital, risk management, some business conduct | Reporting format differences; limited scope for transaction-level requirements |
| **Australia** | Corporations Act, ASIC rules, APRA prudential standards | Margin, risk management, some entity-level requirements | More limited scope than EU or Japan determinations |
| **Hong Kong** | SFO (Securities and Futures Ordinance), HKMA rules | Margin requirements for authorized institutions | Narrow scope; primarily banking regulators' margin rules |
| **United Kingdom** | UK EMIR (onshored), FCA rules (post-Brexit) | Similar to EU determinations (inherited and updated) | Evolving as UK diverges from EU framework post-Brexit |

!!! warning "Comparability Is Not Equivalence"
    Comparability determinations are always **partial and conditional**. No jurisdiction has received blanket substituted compliance across all CFTC regulatory areas. The gaps are where compliance costs accumulate — a European swap dealer with substituted compliance for margin and risk management may still need to build separate infrastructure for CFTC reporting and trade execution requirements.

## Why Substituted Compliance Doesn't Help DeFi

Substituted compliance is structurally unavailable to DeFi protocols for a fundamental reason: **there is no "home jurisdiction" regulatory regime to substitute.**

The substituted compliance framework assumes a non-U.S. entity that is registered with and supervised by a foreign regulator under a comprehensive derivatives regulatory regime. A DeFi protocol deployed on Ethereum has no home regulator. A DAO incorporated in the Cayman Islands may be registered under Cayman law, but the Cayman Islands does not have a comprehensive derivatives regulatory regime comparable to CFTC rules. A protocol operating from a Cosmos app-chain has no regulatory nexus at all.

Even if a DeFi protocol establishes a legal entity in a jurisdiction with a robust regulatory framework (say, France under EMIR), the protocol itself — the smart contracts, the on-chain execution, the permissionless access — remains outside any single regulator's supervisory perimeter. Substituted compliance requires that the foreign regulator is actually **supervising** the entity's derivatives activity. No regulator currently supervises DeFi protocol operations in a way that would satisfy the CFTC's comparability standard.

---

## Section Summary

- Substituted compliance allows non-U.S. swap dealers to satisfy CFTC requirements through compliance with comparable foreign regulations
- The CFTC makes outcome-based comparability determinations on a per-category basis (margin, capital, reporting, etc.)
- Major determinations exist for the EU, Japan, Australia, Hong Kong, and the UK — all partial
- DeFi protocols cannot benefit from substituted compliance because they lack a home jurisdiction regulator conducting comparable supervision
