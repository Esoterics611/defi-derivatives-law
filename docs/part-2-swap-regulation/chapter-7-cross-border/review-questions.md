# Review Questions — Chapter 7: Cross-Border Derivatives Regulation

---

**1.** Explain how CEA §2(i) establishes the statutory basis for the CFTC's cross-border jurisdiction. What does "direct and significant connection with activities in, or effect on, commerce of the United States" mean in practice?

**2.** The CFTC's "U.S. person" definition extends beyond natural persons resident in the U.S. Identify three categories of entities that qualify as U.S. persons even if they are organized or operating outside the United States. Why does the CFTC define "U.S. person" so broadly?

**3.** A European swap dealer registered with the CFTC enters an interest rate swap with a Japanese pension fund. No U.S. person is involved in the transaction. Under the CFTC's cross-border framework, which requirements (entity-level, transaction-level, or both) apply? Could substituted compliance reduce the dealer's compliance burden?

**4.** Compare the CFTC's substituted compliance framework with the EU's equivalence framework under EMIR Article 13. What are the key structural differences, and why has cross-border regulatory recognition been politically contentious (particularly regarding UK CCPs post-Brexit)?

**5.** A DeFi perpetual futures protocol is incorporated as a foundation in the Cayman Islands. Its development team is distributed across Portugal, Singapore, and Dubai. The protocol's smart contracts are deployed on Ethereum. U.S. users can access the protocol through the front-end website (no geo-blocking) and directly through the smart contracts. Analyze the CFTC's jurisdictional basis for enforcement. Would geo-blocking change the analysis?

**6.** After the Ooki DAO judgment, evaluate "progressive decentralization" as a regulatory strategy. Why did the CFTC characterize the bZeroX-to-Ooki DAO transition as a "decentralization shell game"? Under what circumstances, if any, could decentralization genuinely reduce regulatory exposure?

**7.** The London Whale case resulted in $920 million in penalties from five regulators across two countries. If a DeFi protocol of comparable scale caused similar market distortions, which regulators would likely assert jurisdiction and under what legal theories? How would enforcement differ given DeFi's pseudonymous, decentralized architecture?

**8.** Compare how the CFTC, ESMA (under EMIR), and the MAS (Singapore) approach jurisdiction over DeFi derivatives protocols. Which jurisdiction's framework is best suited to regulating cross-border DeFi activity, and why?

**9.** A DeFi protocol implements the following strategy: (a) establishes a legal entity in Singapore under MAS licensing, (b) geo-blocks U.S. and EU users from the front-end, (c) deploys smart contracts on a non-Ethereum L1 with a smaller validator set, (d) implements on-chain KYC for users accessing through the front-end. Rate each element of this strategy on legal strength (strong/moderate/weak) and explain your reasoning.

**10.** Using the AI Practitioner Note prompt from Section 7.4, analyze a DeFi protocol of your choice for cross-border regulatory exposure. Run the prompt through an AI model, then critically assess whether the AI adequately distinguished between jurisdictional basis (does the regulator have legal authority?) and enforcement practicality (can the regulator actually enforce?).
