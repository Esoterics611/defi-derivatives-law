# 4.5 Equity Swaps & Equity-Linked Derivatives

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:
    - Classify equity derivatives by the breadth of their underlying index to determine CFTC vs. SEC jurisdiction
    - Explain variance and volatility swaps and their risk management function
    - Identify the jurisdictional split that makes equity derivatives the most complex classification challenge


---

## The Jurisdictional Split

Equity derivatives are the category where the CFTC/SEC jurisdictional divide is sharpest. The classification depends entirely on the **breadth of the underlying reference**:

| Underlying Reference | Classification | Regulator | Basis |
|---------------------|---------------|-----------|-------|
| Broad-based equity index (S&P 500, Russell 2000, MSCI World) | **Swap** | CFTC | CEA §1a(47) |
| Single stock (Apple, Tesla, etc.) | **Security-based swap** | SEC | Exchange Act §3(a)(68) |
| Narrow-based equity index (≤9 components, or highly concentrated) | **Security-based swap** | SEC | Exchange Act §3(a)(68)(A)(ii)(I) |
| Mixed (elements of both) | **Mixed swap** | CFTC + SEC jointly | CEA §1a(47)(D) |

!!! danger "The Narrow-Based Index Test"
    An index is "narrow-based" if it meets ANY of these criteria:
    - Has **9 or fewer** component securities
    - Any single component constitutes **more than 30%** of the index weight
    - The **five largest components** together exceed 60% of the index weight
    - The lowest-weighted components constituting **25% of the index** have an aggregate average daily trading volume of less than $50 million

    This test is critical because it determines whether CFTC or SEC rules apply. Getting it wrong means registering with the wrong regulator — or worse, not registering at all.


## Common Equity Swap Structures

**Equity index swaps:** One party pays the total return of a broad-based equity index, the other pays a financing rate. Used for portfolio hedging, asset allocation, and accessing markets without directly holding securities.

**Dividend swaps:** One party pays a fixed dividend amount, the other pays the actual dividends realized on a stock or index over the swap term. Used to hedge or speculate on dividend levels.

**Variance swaps:** One party pays a fixed variance strike, the other pays the realized variance of the underlying over the swap term. Settlement is based on the difference between realized variance and the strike, multiplied by a variance notional. These are pure volatility bets.

**Volatility swaps:** Similar to variance swaps but settle on realized volatility directly (the square root of variance). Less common due to the convexity adjustment required.

## The DeFi Dimension

DeFi "index" tokens that track baskets of crypto assets raise the narrow-based index question directly:

| DeFi Product | Components | Likely Classification |
|-------------|------------|----------------------|
| DeFi Pulse Index (DPI) | ~15 DeFi governance tokens | Potentially broad-based — CFTC if tokens are "commodities" |
| Token basket with 5 tokens | 5 specific tokens | Narrow-based — SEC if tokens are "securities" |
| Single-token leveraged exposure | 1 token | Security-based swap if token is a security; swap if commodity |

The classification hinges on whether the underlying tokens are "securities" or "commodities" — which loops back to the foundational question of Chapter 1. If BTC and ETH are commodities, then swaps referencing them are CFTC swaps. If a specific alt-token is a security, then a single-name swap on it is an SEC security-based swap.

---

## Section Summary

- Equity derivative classification depends on whether the underlying index is broad-based (CFTC) or narrow-based (SEC)
- The narrow-based index test has specific quantitative thresholds (9 components, 30% single weight, etc.)
- Variance and volatility swaps provide pure exposure to market volatility
- DeFi index tokens and leveraged products face the same broad-based vs. narrow-based analysis
- Classification requires first determining whether underlying tokens are securities or commodities
