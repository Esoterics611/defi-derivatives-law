# Chapter 5: Swap Dealers & Major Swap Participants

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this chapter, you will be able to:
    - Apply the four-prong swap dealer test and calculate de minimis threshold exposure
    - Distinguish swap dealer (SD) from major swap participant (MSP) designation
    - Enumerate the business conduct, capital, margin, and recordkeeping obligations that attach to registration
    - Analyze when DeFi protocol operators, market makers, or large participants might trigger registration requirements
    - Use AI to model threshold exposure and identify registration tripwires


!!! warning "Key Terms"
    - **Swap Dealer (SD)**: Any person who holds itself out as a dealer in swaps, makes a market in swaps, regularly enters swaps with counterparties as an ordinary course of business, or is commonly known as a dealer in swaps
    - **Major Swap Participant (MSP)**: A non-dealer entity that maintains a "substantial position" in swaps, whose positions create substantial counterparty exposure, or that is a highly leveraged financial entity not subject to bank capital requirements
    - **De Minimis Threshold**: The notional value below which an entity is exempt from SD registration — currently $8 billion in aggregate gross notional over the preceding 12 months
    - **NFA (National Futures Association)**: The self-regulatory organization through which swap dealers must register and maintain membership
    - **Business Conduct Standards**: CFTC rules governing how swap dealers interact with counterparties, manage risk, and operate internally (17 C.F.R. Part 23)

## Introduction: The Registration Tripwire

If your entity triggers swap dealer or major swap participant registration, the compliance burden is **transformative**. SD registration triggers dozens of CFTC regulations covering capital requirements, margin collection, business conduct standards, risk management programs, chief compliance officer mandates, recordkeeping obligations, and reporting duties. Compliance costs for a registered swap dealer are estimated at **$10-50 million annually** for a mid-sized firm.

This is not a minor regulatory formality. This is the difference between operating freely and operating under one of the most intensive regulatory regimes in financial services.

For DeFi, the question is immediate and practical: when does operating a derivatives protocol, providing liquidity, or market-making in DeFi derivatives cross the line into swap dealer territory? The CFTC has not yet brought an enforcement action specifically for failure to register as a swap dealer in DeFi — but the Ooki DAO action demonstrates the CFTC's willingness to apply traditional derivatives regulation to decentralized protocols.

!!! danger "The Stakes"
    Operating as an unregistered swap dealer is a **federal violation** under CEA §4s. The penalty is not just a fine — it's an enforcement action that can include injunctions, civil monetary penalties (up to the greater of $1 million or triple the monetary gain per violation), and disgorgement of profits. For a DeFi protocol processing significant volume, the exposure is existential.

## Section Summary

- SD and MSP registration triggers extensive CFTC compliance obligations
- Annual compliance costs for registered swap dealers: $10-50 million
- The de minimis threshold ($8 billion notional) is the primary safe harbor
- DeFi protocol operators and liquidity providers face unresolved registration questions
