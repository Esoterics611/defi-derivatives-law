# 4.4 Commodity Swaps & Energy Derivatives

!!! info "Learning Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:
    - Describe the mechanics of commodity swaps across energy, metals, and agricultural markets
    - Explain the physical vs. financial settlement distinction and its regulatory implications
    - Identify how commodity swaps interact with position limit rules
    - Analyze DeFi commodity-linked products through the commodity swap lens

## Commodity Swap Mechanics

A commodity swap is an agreement in which one party makes payments based on a fixed commodity price, and the other makes payments based on a floating (market) commodity price, over a defined term. The payments reference a specific quantity of a specific commodity, but typically no physical commodity changes hands — the swap is "financially settled."

### The Standard Structure

**Example:** A crude oil producer wants to lock in $75/barrel for 50,000 barrels/month over the next 12 months.

- **Producer (fixed receiver):** Receives $75/barrel × 50,000 barrels = $3,750,000/month fixed
- **Bank (floating receiver):** Receives the average WTI settlement price × 50,000 barrels

If WTI averages $70 in Month 1, the bank pays the producer $250,000 (the difference). If WTI averages $82 in Month 2, the producer pays the bank $350,000. The producer has effectively locked in $75/barrel regardless of market movements.

### Major Commodity Swap Categories

| Category | Common Products | Reference Prices | Typical Users |
|----------|----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Crude oil** | WTI swaps, Brent swaps, crack spread swaps | NYMEX WTI, ICE Brent | Producers, refiners, airlines |
| **Natural gas** | Henry Hub swaps, basis swaps (location spreads) | NYMEX Henry Hub | Utilities, producers, industrials |
| **Power/electricity** | Fixed-for-floating power swaps | Regional hub prices (PJM, ERCOT) | Utilities, generators |
| **Metals** | Gold, silver, copper, aluminum swaps | COMEX, LME settlement | Miners, manufacturers, jewelers |
| **Agricultural** | Corn, wheat, soybean, coffee, sugar swaps | CBOT, ICE settlement | Farmers, food processors |

### Physical vs. Financial Settlement

This distinction has profound regulatory consequences:

**Financially settled:** Payments calculated by reference to commodity price, but no physical delivery occurs. These are unambiguously "swaps" under the CEA and subject to full Dodd-Frank regulation.

**Physically settled:** Commodity is actually delivered. Forward contracts for physical delivery may qualify for the **forward contract exclusion** and escape swap regulation entirely.

!!! danger "The "Actual Delivery" Test — Critical for DeFi"
    The forward contract exclusion under CEA §1a(47)(B)(ii) excludes from the swap definition "any sale of a nonfinancial commodity or security for deferred shipment or delivery, so long as the transaction is intended to be physically settled."

    For digital assets, the CFTC has issued interpretive guidance (CFTC Guidance on Actual Delivery, 2020) defining "actual delivery" of virtual currencies as occurring when the buyer has "full possession and control" — meaning the buyer controls the private keys and can freely use or transfer the asset within 28 days.

    This matters because: if a DeFi token transaction constitutes "actual delivery" of a commodity, it may qualify as a forward (exempt) rather than a swap (regulated). But if delivery is delayed, synthetic, or conditional — it's a swap.


### Position Limits

Commodity swaps are subject to **federal position limits** (CFTC Part 150), which cap the number of contracts or equivalent swap positions any single entity can hold in specified physical commodity derivatives. This is unique to commodity swaps — interest rate, equity, and FX swaps are not subject to position limits.

The position limit framework was finalized in 2021 after a decade of rulemaking and applies to 25 core referenced contracts across agricultural, energy, and metals markets. Commodity swap positions must be aggregated with futures positions for limit calculation purposes.

## The DeFi Parallel

DeFi protocols that create commodity-linked exposure include:

| DeFi Product | Mechanism | Commodity Swap Equivalent |
|-------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Tokenized gold (PAXG, XAUT) | Backed by physical gold in vaults | Physical forward — may qualify for exclusion if actual delivery occurs |
| Synthetic oil tokens | Track oil price via oracles, no physical backing | Financially settled commodity swap — full regulation applies |
| Agricultural prediction contracts | Binary payouts based on crop yields/prices | Event contracts on exempt commodities — CFTC jurisdiction |
| RWA protocols (commodity-backed) | Tokenized claims on commodity inventories | Depends on whether delivery is actual or synthetic |

## Section Summary

- Commodity swaps exchange fixed commodity price payments for floating market price payments
- Financial settlement = unambiguous swap regulation; physical settlement may qualify for forward exclusion
- The "actual delivery" test is critical for digital asset classification (28-day CFTC guidance)
- Commodity swaps uniquely face federal position limits (CFTC Part 150)
- DeFi commodity-linked products range from potentially exempt (backed, deliverable) to clearly regulated (synthetic, no delivery)
