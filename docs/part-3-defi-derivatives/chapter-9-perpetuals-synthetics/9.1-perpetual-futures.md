    - Classify perpetual futures under the CEA as futures contracts or swaps

## What Is a Perpetual Future?

A perpetual future — "perp" — is a derivative contract that tracks the price of an underlying asset (typically BTC, ETH, or another crypto token) with **no expiry date**. Unlike traditional futures contracts that settle on a specified date through delivery or cash settlement, perps can be held indefinitely. The holder maintains a leveraged long or short position that is continuously marked to market.

The mechanism that makes perps work without expiry is the **funding rate**: a periodic payment (typically every 8 hours) exchanged between long and short position holders. When the perp price trades above the spot price (indicating excess demand from longs), longs pay shorts. When the perp price trades below spot, shorts pay longs. This mechanism creates an economic incentive for the perp price to converge with the spot price — performing the function that delivery and expiry perform in traditional futures.

Perpetual futures were invented by BitMEX founder Arthur Hayes in 2016. They have since become the dominant derivative instrument in crypto markets. The reason is simple: they combine the leverage and directional exposure of futures with the convenience of never having to roll positions at expiry. For traders, perps are perpetual leveraged bets on price direction.

## CFTC Classification

The CFTC's analysis of perpetual futures is straightforward:

**Are they futures?** A futures contract is an agreement for the purchase or sale of a commodity for future delivery. Perps do not involve delivery, but neither do cash-settled futures, which the CFTC has regulated since the 1980s. A perp is an agreement that provides for payments based on the value of a commodity (BTC, ETH) over time — this is either a futures contract or a swap under CEA §1a(47).

**Are they swaps?** The CEA's swap definition captures "any agreement, contract, or transaction that provides for any purchase, sale, payment, or delivery that is dependent on the occurrence, nonoccurrence, or the extent of an occurrence of an event or contingency associated with a potential financial, economic, or commercial consequence." A perp's PnL is dependent on price movements — a financial contingency. It also fits §1a(47)(A)(iii): an agreement providing for the exchange of payments based on the value of a commodity.

**The practical answer:** The CFTC has not formally classified perps as futures versus swaps, but in enforcement actions it has treated leveraged crypto derivatives as instruments within its jurisdiction under both theories. The specific classification matters for which regulatory requirements apply (futures must be traded on a DCM; swaps may be traded on a SEF), but either classification results in registration requirements that no DeFi perp protocol currently meets.

**Must perps be traded on a registered platform?** Yes. If classified as futures, they must be traded on a DCM under CEA §5(a). If classified as swaps and subject to the clearing mandate and a MAT determination, they must be traded on a SEF or DCM. Either way, operating a platform that facilitates perp trading without registration violates the CEA.

!!! info "AI Practitioner Note"
    **Analyzing funding rate mechanisms and their regulatory classification:**

    *"Analyze the following perpetual futures protocol's funding rate mechanism: [describe mechanism]. Determine: (1) Is the funding rate a separate derivative instrument (a periodic swap between longs and shorts), or is it an integral feature of the perp contract? (2) Does the funding rate mechanism constitute a 'payment based on the value of a commodity' under CEA §1a(47)? (3) How does the funding rate's price convergence function compare to the settlement/delivery function in traditional futures? (4) If the funding rate fails to maintain convergence (e.g., during extreme market conditions), what are the regulatory implications?"*

    **What to verify:** AI models often describe funding rates correctly at a mechanical level but miss the regulatory implication that the funding rate itself might constitute a separate swap — a periodic payment between counterparties based on the difference between the perp price and spot price. This is economically identical to a basis swap, which is a regulated instrument.

## Section Summary

- Perpetual futures are the dominant crypto derivative, with DeFi perp volume exceeding regulated exchange volume
- The funding rate mechanism replaces delivery/expiry as the price convergence mechanism
- Perps are either futures contracts or swaps under the CEA — either classification triggers registration requirements
