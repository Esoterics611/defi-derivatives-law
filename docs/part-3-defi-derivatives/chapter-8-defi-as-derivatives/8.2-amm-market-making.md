# 8.2 AMMs & Liquidity Pools as Market-Making Infrastructure

!!! info "Section Objectives"
    After completing this section, you will be able to:

    - Describe how AMMs function as automated derivatives market infrastructure
    - Explain why impermanent loss is mathematically equivalent to a short options payoff
    - Classify different AMM architectures by regulatory risk
    - Identify when liquidity provision itself creates a regulated derivative exposure

---

## AMMs as Market Makers

An Automated Market Maker replaces the human market maker with a mathematical formula. In traditional markets, a market maker (often a bank's derivatives desk) quotes bid and ask prices, maintains an inventory of positions, and earns the spread between buying and selling prices. The market maker is a regulated entity — typically a swap dealer registered with the CFTC.

An AMM performs the same economic function: it provides continuous liquidity by standing ready to trade with any counterparty at algorithmically determined prices. Liquidity providers deposit assets into a smart contract pool, and traders execute against the pool. The key AMM models:

**Constant Product (x × y = k):** Uniswap v2's formula. The product of the two token reserves must remain constant after every trade. This creates a convex bonding curve that becomes more expensive to trade as the pool becomes imbalanced. Simple but capital-inefficient — most liquidity sits unused at extreme price levels.

**Concentrated Liquidity:** Uniswap v3's innovation. LPs allocate liquidity within specific price ranges rather than across the entire curve. This dramatically increases capital efficiency but creates LP positions that behave like short options on the underlying token pair.

**Virtual AMM (vAMM):** Used by perpetual futures protocols like Perpetual Protocol. No actual token reserves — the AMM formula determines prices for derivative positions, with collateral pools backing the synthetic exposure. This is the clearest case of derivatives market infrastructure disguised as an AMM.

**Multi-Asset Pools:** GMX's model. A single pool of multiple assets (GLP/GM) serves as the counterparty for all traders. LPs provide diversified liquidity, and trader PnL is paid from or accrues to the pool. The pool functions as both market maker and clearinghouse.

| AMM Type | Mechanism | Regulatory Classification Risk | Comparable TradFi Activity |
|----------|-----------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Constant product (spot) | x × y = k for token swaps | Low — spot trades, not derivatives (unless LP position reclassified) | Spot FX market maker |
| Concentrated liquidity (spot) | LP within price range | Moderate — LP positions have options-like payoffs | Writing short straddles/strangles |
| Virtual AMM (perps) | Formula-based pricing for leveraged derivatives | **High** — explicitly a derivatives exchange | Futures exchange market-making function |
| Multi-asset pool (perps) | Diversified pool as counterparty to derivatives traders | **High** — pool functions as both exchange and clearinghouse | Combined DCM + DCO function |

## Impermanent Loss as a Derivative Exposure

The academic insight that transforms the regulatory analysis: impermanent loss in a constant product AMM is **mathematically equivalent to selling a put and a call (a short straddle) on the relative price of the token pair**.

The proof, simplified: when an LP deposits equal value of Token A and Token B into a constant product pool, the pool's rebalancing mechanism automatically sells the appreciating token and buys the depreciating token as prices move. This is the same payoff profile as a short straddle — the LP profits when prices stay stable (earning fees) and loses when prices move significantly in either direction (impermanent loss exceeds fees earned).

Guillaume Lambert's research formalized this equivalence for Uniswap v3 concentrated liquidity positions: a concentrated LP position within a price range [Pa, Pb] has the same payoff as writing a put option at Pa and a call option at Pb. The narrower the range, the more leveraged the effective options position.

!!! danger "The Regulatory Implication"
    If LP positions are derivative exposures, then providing liquidity to a pool is economically equivalent to writing options. Under the CEA, options on commodities (including crypto-commodities like BTC and ETH) are regulated instruments. A platform that facilitates the creation and trading of these positions is performing a regulated exchange function. This reclassification would bring Uniswap — currently analyzed as a spot venue — within CFTC jurisdiction.

    The CFTC has not yet pursued this argument in enforcement, but the academic foundation is established. This is a regulatory time bomb.

## When Does LP = Swap Dealer?

Separate from the question of whether LP positions are derivatives is whether large liquidity providers are performing a **swap dealer function**. Recall from Chapter 5 that a swap dealer is any person who holds itself out as a dealer in swaps, makes a market in swaps, or regularly enters swaps as an ordinary course of business.

A sophisticated LP that regularly provides substantial liquidity to derivative AMMs — particularly vAMMs and perpetual futures pools — is arguably making a market in derivatives. If the value of their market-making activity exceeds the de minimis threshold ($8 billion in aggregate gross notional), swap dealer registration could be triggered.

This is currently theoretical. No LP has been charged with unregistered swap dealing. But the legal framework supports the classification, and as DeFi derivatives volumes grow, the argument strengthens.

## Section Summary

- AMMs perform the economic function of market makers, with different architectures carrying different regulatory risks
- Virtual AMMs and multi-asset pools used for perpetual futures are clearly derivatives exchange infrastructure
- Impermanent loss is mathematically equivalent to a short options payoff, potentially bringing spot AMM LP positions within CFTC jurisdiction
- Large LPs on derivatives AMMs may be performing unregistered swap dealer functions
- The CFTC has not yet pursued LP-as-derivative or LP-as-swap-dealer theories, but the legal foundation exists
